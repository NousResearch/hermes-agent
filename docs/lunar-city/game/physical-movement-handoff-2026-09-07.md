# Physical movement and retained actor placement

Scope: local presentation movement against actual verified navigation geometry and emitted solid scenery. This does not perform backend work or introduce a rigid-body simulation.

`movement-safety.ts` uses the navigation GLB's transformed triangles as the floor. It resolves actor feet onto a nearby supported surface and clips complete movement segments against triangle intervals, requiring continuous floor coverage. It does not infer a ground plane from screen appearance or sample only widely spaced points. Building collisions use rotated boxes; trees use cylinders emitted from actual trunks. A swept 0.35 m body radius and 1.2 m height represent the default worker body. Individually protruding tools, loose objects and moving actor-to-actor bodies are not convex collision meshes in this implementation.

The navigation controller rejects nonfinite, snapped-start and partial unsafe paths; it validates the requested target and the final connector. Each applied movement step is grounded and tested against solids. Long frames are capped to 250 ms of movement, so waking a suspended renderer cannot move an actor across a whole route in one frame. Missing navigation geometry or failed Recast holds movement. Reduced motion holds the existing position rather than teleporting to the target. A collision stop is unavailable, not a successful building arrival.

The entity registry keeps a separate immutable source-origin record and a locally resolved physical position. Ordinary polling cannot reset an actor to its old origin. Changed observation coordinates do not grant teleport permission; retained actors stay at their safe current position and route from there. New actors can spawn only on validated floor, with a validated declared-destination fallback; otherwise they remain unplaced. The exact navigation asset identity and collider layout scope a bounded in-memory position cache: default 512 actors per scope, up to eight scopes and a 10-minute freshness lease. Removal forgets the identity, stale snapshots do not renew it, and renderer-world remount can restore a still-valid placement. **A full browser/application reload does not preserve this process-local cache.** No localStorage or backend state is written.

The root environment builder owns the `navigation.colliders` contract: `{id,kind:'box',center:[x,y,z],halfExtents:[x,y,z],rotationY}` or `{id,kind:'cylinder',center:[x,y,z],radius,height}`. Centers are vertical centers, not base points. The manifest parser validates finite coordinates and positive dimensions. Changed collider layout creates a different position-cache scope.

Focused verification passes 80 movement, floor/collider, entity-registry, cache and world-lifecycle tests. The wider 604-test UI run exposed one historical teleport expectation; its two-test arrival-slot file now passes with pause-hold/resume behavior. Actual island runtime verification now passes in [physics-runtime.json](evidence/physics-runtime.json): all 21 routes completed through the real movement controller with continuous floor and swept-solid checks, all 28 collider centers and an off-mesh point rejected, and an actual imported worker walked before retaining its exact position through fresh polling, reduced-motion pause and world remount. The harness waits for actual rendered frames; its first 600 ms timing assumption was too short during resource initialization and was replaced with a bounded frame-based wait. Arrival-slot offset was independently checked: its endpoint had valid floor and a clear final connector.

Collider-center rejection alone can also be caused by absence of navigation floor, so focused tests independently place a rotated building and a cylindrical trunk on known continuous floor and prove blocked crossings versus clear passes. A separate 1 cm floor-gap invariant prevents sparse sampling from hiding unsupported space.
