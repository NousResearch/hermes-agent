# Landowner Group Communications (WhatsApp) — DRAAS Project Negotiations

Patterns for drafting messages NDR sends to a **project landowner WhatsApp group**
(Ranka North Star / Allalasandra is the worked example). This is high-stakes, multi-year
relationship correspondence: the same group carries milestone updates, logistics, procedural
pushback, consensus-seeking and conflict de-escalation, often within the same week.

## Hard workflow rule — text first, links after

**Always present the complete draft as plain text for approval BEFORE generating links.**
NDR's exact instruction: *"just show me the draft here… the entire text content… once I
approve it, give me the WhatsApp links for it."*

Do **not** jump straight to links. When two variants are offered, print **both in full** so
he can pick. Only after he says go do you produce the tap-to-send links. Expect 1–3
revision rounds on wording before he sends — treat each correction as an edit request, not
a restart.

## Link mechanics

- **Group messages take NO phone number.** The link opens WhatsApp and NDR picks the group.
  A recipient-specific link is only for 1:1 messages and needs the verified number.
- Long messages **auto-split into 2–3 parts**. Deliver each part as its own link and say
  explicitly: "send Part 1, then Part 2, in order."
- Use the sanctioned WhatsApp link generator — never hand-encode the URL. If it isn't
  registered as a tool in the session, use the skill's script path rather than building a
  URL by hand.
- A long legal or procedural message can split to 3 parts; keep section headings with their
  content when the split falls mid-message.

## Formatting that NDR asks for

His words: *"format using WhatsApp markdown… heading being bold, bulleting, numbering,
separating the paragraph, giving titles to each paragraph which are bold."*

- `*BOLD UPPERCASE SECTION HEADINGS*` on their own line, each introducing one idea.
- `•` bullets for parallel items; `1)` numbering for sequenced steps or objectives.
- Blank line between every block — never a wall of text.
- Emojis sparingly at the top (`🙏` / `🌟`) and for punctuation of sentiment, not decoration.
- A title line naming the message's purpose, then the greeting, then the substance.
- Sign off `With gratitude, *Nishant Ranka*` (he signs personally; no company signature for
  landowner groups).

## Tone calibration

- **Informal and warm is the default when the news is good.** NDR explicitly asked to make
  a confirmed-meeting message *"slightly less formal"*. Relaxed phrasing is fine —
  "Simple.", "my treat", "just let me know", "Please confirm at the earliest".
- **He leads with warmth and respect, then the ask.** Persistent theme: acknowledging that
  landowners *"have invested, waited and shown patience for so long — and I hold that with
  the deepest respect"* before any request.
- **Psychological framing is wanted, not discouraged.** He asked for *"a very cooperative
  message using all psychology of human psychology elements"* — light at the end of the
  tunnel, auspicious beginnings, shared credit, "together we finish this".
- **Religious/auspicious openers when the occasion genuinely fits.** Ganesha Chaturthi was
  woven in as serendipity for good news landing that day, closing with
  "With gratitude and Ganesha's blessings". Only when the date actually supports it.
- **Firm on substance, soft in delivery.** Refusing to be drawn into landowner-vs-landowner
  disputes is stated kindly but unmistakably: their responsibility *as a group*, decision
  *"between the landowners — it has nothing to do with me"*.
- **Every message ends with a specific, easy ask** — confirm a date, share a contact,
  propose availability. Never close on a statement.

## Recurring content shapes

| Situation | Shape of the message |
|---|---|
| **Milestone reached** | Auspicious/positive opener → what was achieved (bullets) → what it means → meeting request → two objectives → follow-on timeline → commitment → close |
| **Confirming a meeting** | Thank the confirmer → ask the rest to confirm the same date **at the earliest** → logistics (time, lunch, transport, venue) → per-person asks → travel/accommodation offer → close |
| **Asking one person to move** | Name them → make the ask directly and warmly → explain *why it is necessary* briefly → logistics offer → *"in the interest of crossing this last hurdle"* → close |
| **Seeking consensus** | Report whose support is already secured → put the specific questions → ask the two decision-makers to confirm → confirm what is binding on whom → close |
| **Procedural bad news** | Be transparent → state the authority's position plainly → what it means → what you are doing right now → the request that unblocks it → realistic revised timeline → what continues in parallel → thanks for patience |
| **Declining to referee a dispute** | Repeat the documentary position → say the document provides for proportionate recovery → invite them to resolve privately → one forward-looking condition (be clear by the sharing agreement) |

## Pitfalls

- **"Legal ears" is a transcription of "legal heirs".** NDR corrected this himself. In any
  landowner message, ears → heirs. Carry the correction forward rather than re-introducing it.
- **"Refundable" only — never "refundable and non-refundable".** He corrected this
  explicitly: *"There is only refundable. There is no non-refundable at all."* The developer
  has captured everything on refundable in the addendum to the JDA; from the developer's
  side there is nothing outstanding. Writing "refundable and non-refundable" reopens a
  settled point.
- **Amend one section, don't re-draft the whole message.** When he asks to redo "the two
  objectives portion", show only that portion for approval (he asked for exactly this) and
  leave the rest of the approved message untouched.
- **Never invent the other side's reply.** If he says "below is the reply I received" and the
  text is missing from the paste, say so in one line, then work from his dictation — do not
  fabricate a plausible landowner response.
- **Verify figures before they go in writing.** The addendum's operative clauses (33% of
  built-up area on 1.75 FAR; 15% of super built-up area on any FAR over 1.75, with the
  developer bearing the cost) and the refundable totals (₹2,40,00,000 + ₹50,00,000) come from
  the executed instrument — read it, don't recall it.
- **Timestamp reality matters.** NDR's voice notes are long and multi-topic (the landowner
  update was ~900 words across six distinct asks). Enumerate the asks in order before
  drafting, so none is dropped.
