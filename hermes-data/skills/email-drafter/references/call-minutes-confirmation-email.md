# Minuting a call as a confirmation email ("this is what we agreed")

NDR comes off a phone/video call with staff + an external and dictates: *"send an update email
confirming from \<A\>, \<B\> and everything that this is what we had discussed and agreed… and if
any task has to be updated or a new one added, do the needful."*

The email is the record. Get it wrong and it becomes the thing people point at later. Four steps.

## 1. Find the thread that already tracks it — before drafting

Search the mailbox by the **subjects and participants**, not by the words NDR used on the call
(he speaks in outcomes, not filenames):

- `from:(<external name>) OR to:(<external name>) <project>` — catches the liaisoning/vendor leg
- `<project> (flow plan OR DTCP OR sanction OR setback)` — catches the internal leg

This session's thread was **"Oasis 1500 villa plans"** (Sinchana → Vinay Prasad, 15-Oct, plus a
reminder) — nothing in NDR's dictation would have led you to that subject line. State the thread
you found and what it already contains, so he can see the record exists.

## 2. Check what has ALREADY been actioned — the call may be half-done

Read the thread and the day's messages before writing "please do X" into an email. In this
session Sinchana had **already emailed the plans three times, 20 minutes earlier** — so the mail
had to *confirm receipt and name what went out*, not ask for it again. Sending a "please send
today" instruction to someone who sent it an hour ago destroys the email's credibility.

Reconcile NDR's dictation against the evidence and say what you found:

- he said "5 or 6 different flow plans" → what actually went out was **3 plan types × east/west
  1500 = 6 variants**, applied across **21 plots**
- he said "identify 15 plots" → 21 had already been circulated, so the narrowing to 15 is a step
  still to be taken, not a fact

Flag the reconciliation in your reply to NDR in one line each. Do not silently "correct" his
numbers inside the email and do not silently reproduce a number the evidence contradicts.

## 3. Structure — numbered, verbatim where it matters

Multi-point coordination → **HTML**, not plain text (plain-text `*bold*` runs together and reads
badly in his threads). Number the agreed points, one paragraph each, and keep his own nouns:
*flow plans, setbacks, height with reference to the master plan road width, total built-up area,
car parking, DTCP norms, sanction*. Use a bold **Timeline** line with the actual date and weekday.

Then close with any **blocker that will decide the outcome**, phrased as one ask. This session's
was: Kavita indicated self/online approval only goes to **G+1** and the Oasis villas are **G+2**
— so the approval route decides how the plots reach sanction.

## 4. Declare every line you added beyond the dictation

NDR dictates the substance, not the whole document. Anything you add — a blocker, a deadline
weekday, a corrected figure — gets **called out explicitly** in your reply: *"I added one line
you didn't dictate — asking Vinay to confirm the G+2 route. Strike it if you'd rather keep the
mail narrow."* Never let an unannounced addition sit inside an email he is about to send as his
own words.

Same rule for gaps: if a person he named has no email address on record (**Kavita** — not in
Gmail, not in the contacts sheet, not on any thread), say so and hold the draft rather than
guessing an address or quietly dropping her from the To line.
