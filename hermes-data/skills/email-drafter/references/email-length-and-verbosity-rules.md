# Email length and verbosity — match the length to the purpose

NDR, 23-Oct-2026, dictating a task-instruction mail to his admin:

> *"Taking sections of that email … any detailed email to Sartak. **Don't make it
> too verbose. Nobody reads too verbose emails. But all the key points and basic
> instruction** to Sartak, please do the needful to get the address updated."*

And on the same principle, the same day, for a field brief to the sales lead, he
wanted the opposite — the full seven-section, headed, bulleted document with the
comparison table, the five data points and the channel-partner script.

So "too verbose" is **not** a global cap. It is a judgement about purpose. The
rule below is what to reach for before drafting.

## The three lengths

**1. Task / instruction mail (admin, colleague, vendor) — ONE SCREEN.**
The test: can it be read on a phone, standing up, and acted on without scrolling
back? Structure:

- one line saying what the mail is for;
- a small table or two-line block of *what is on record now* vs *what it must
  become* — facts only, no narrative;
- numbered actions (what, then by when);
- the reference data needed to act (documents accepted, contacts, links);
- the one or two traps worth knowing.

Nothing else. No background paragraph, no restatement of the request at the
bottom, no "please revert for any clarification" boilerplate.

**2. Field brief / checklist / comp study — LONG, headed, bulleted.**
This is the walk-around document the person carries on site. Long is correct
here; every heading earns its place because it maps to something they must
answer. Do not compress it into a note to look tidy.

**3. Multi-point coordination or internal briefing — HTML, headed, no forced
line breaks.** (His standing format preference; plain-text `*bold*` markdown
runs together and reads badly.)

## Concrete rules

- **Match the reader.** Admin/support staff get the short form; a specialist
  doing fieldwork gets the long form.
- **Facts in a table, instructions in a list.** Prose is for the *why*, and the
  why gets one sentence, not a section.
- **Never open with the background.** Open with the objective.
- **Put the deadline next to the action,** not in a closing paragraph — a
  deadline three paragraphs below the task gets missed.
- **If a detail is unknown, leave a visible placeholder** (`[full postal address
  and pincode to be confirmed]`) rather than padding the mail with hedging about
  it. NDR fills gaps faster than he reads caveats.
- **The subject line carries the ask**, e.g. *"My demat address – change from
  Ranka Chambers, Cunningham Road to 1503, Embassy Habitat"*.

## Anti-patterns seen and rejected

- Restating the whole request in the last paragraph "to summarise" — reads as
  padding.
- A "background / context" section in a task mail — the reader already knows.
- Long mails where every section is the same length: if everything is emphasised,
  nothing is. The trap that matters should be the only highlighted block.
- Marking a housekeeping mail "URGENT" — reserve that for genuine cut-offs, and
  then state the cut-off time explicitly instead.
