# Re-manding a colleague who has stepped back instead of settling

**Trigger.** NDR forwards/points at a long email from a colleague on his own side (site head,
co-director, project owner — Bhavik Ranka on Ranka Iris, similar with Dharmesh, Anbu) where the
colleague has written a detailed defence and then closed with some version of:

> *"I do not want to get involved with this. Whoever has been handling this can go ahead and do it.
> I have presented all facts … The remaining is upto everyone else's interpretation."*

NDR's instruction is almost always the same shape: *"analyse his email, tell me the key points, and
tell me the appropriate response keeping my objective in mind"* — and the objective is **get that
person to drive the settlement with the vendor**, not for NDR to adjudicate it.

## What NDR wants out of the turn

Three things, in this order, in the chat:

1. **Key points of the colleague's email** — bullets, plus any *new* facts he introduced.
2. **What the response must do** — one short paragraph naming the tension (his stance vs NDR's
   objective) and the lever that resolves it.
3. **The draft reply** — full text, ready to send.

He is asking for a *recommendation*, not for a Gmail draft. Do **not** create the draft in the same
turn; end with "want me to put this into Gmail as an HTML draft reply on that thread (nothing sent)?"

## The structure that works

The whole email turns on one move: **anchor the mandate on the one thing only he owns.** Not "please
help", not "please coordinate" — *you issued the work order; you know the pluses and minuses; you
drive it.* Bhavik's whole email argued that he had done his duty by putting facts on record; that
argument collapses the moment ownership of the commercial instrument is named.

Then, in numbered points:

- **Concede the record, not the conclusion.** One sentence — "I am not going to argue it point by
  point, I accept that as your stand" — then refuse the resignation of responsibility in the next
  breath. Do not re-litigate the technical items; two of Bhavik's answers were literally *"I did not
  follow"*, so a second explanation of the same arithmetic buys nothing.
- **Give the counterparty's position, verbatim in effect.** *"He is clear: without the PDC he will not
  complete. We need the job done. We are not paying more than is due, but we will agree something."*
  This licenses the colleague to settle — without it he thinks any concession is a loss.
- **Split the cards for him: hold what is evidenced, drop what is not.** Name the two or three items
  that survive a counter-argument (measurable BOQ deviations, undelivered scope, work not handed over)
  and explicitly tell him to trade the ones that don't. A negotiator with no permission to concede
  comes back with nothing.
- **Give the fixed baseline.** Restate the money so he negotiates against one number, not a range:
  WO value + extras = total scope − paid − advances = **net payable before debits**. NDR's own
  reconciliation email carried exactly this ladder; reproduce it.
- **State what stays non-negotiable and that it is being chased on a separate track.** The colleague
  must not think holding those items is his job (NDR chases them through the project manager —
  Anbu — on safety, clean finish, sand cushion/brick cover, earthing chambers).
- **Withhold-money lever.** Where the vendor has taken more than he has invoiced, say it plainly:
  *no further money without a tax invoice; that gap is leverage, use it.*
- **Acknowledge his grievance without yielding the sequence.** Bhavik's real grievance was a PDC
  encashed before materials fully landed. Answer it — "your point is noted and it is a fair one" —
  and reframe: the answer is not to stop settling, it is to settle the number first and control the
  release. Never leave a counter-party's fairness point unaddressed; it is what makes him dig in.
- **Close on a date and a cost of delay**, in NDR's register: *"Every day spent here is a day the
  transformer stays dead."*

## Field notes

- Analyse **off the thread**, not off the snippet. Bhavik's inline answers (in red) sat inside NDR's
  own quoted mail — `gws_gmail_get` returns the whole thing with the `>`-quoted parent, which is where
  the questions and answers both live. Read it end to end before summarising.
- **Surface any new fact that contradicts NDR's stated plan**, in the chat, separately from the draft.
  Here: Bhavik recorded that on 23 Oct the replacement meters were rejected as incompatible with the
  panels, while NDR was telling the vendor "meters go in tomorrow". Flagging that before he sends is
  worth more than the draft.
- Keep the numbers in Indian format and reconcile them against the attachment named in the thread
  (`…_WO616_Payment_Reconciliation.xlsx`) rather than retyping from the prose.
- Bhavik is family/co-director: firm, never cold. No cc escalation, no "for the record" language
  aimed at him.
