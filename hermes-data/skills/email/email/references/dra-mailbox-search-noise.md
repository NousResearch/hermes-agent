# Searching a DRA staff mailbox (sales1.blr, admin*, colleagues) — noise exclusion + honest-negative protocol

Verified 2026-10-26 on sales1.blr@draas.com (Bharat) hunting "the email NDR sent about Kelsa
upgradation / integration".

## 1. The mailbox is drowning in machine mail — exclude it or every search is useless

Two automated families dominate ANY query containing `kelsa` or `from:ndr@draas.com`:

| Noise | Sender | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| Kelsa attendance | `Nishant Ranka <ndr@draas.com>` (generated by Kelsa) | `Please sign in for the day` / `Please sign out` |
| Kelsa digest | `Kelsa Notifier <no-reply@kelsa.io>` | `Kelsa: Your Daily Summary` |
| Calendar | `calendar-notification@google.com` | `Notification: ...` |
| Drive | `drive-shares*-noreply@google.com` | `... shared with you` |
| Read AI | `executiveassistant@e.read.ai` | `... | Read AI` |

A `from:ndr@draas.com kelsa` query returned 25 results of pure sign-in noise; the one real
email never surfaced on page 1. Working query shape:

```
from:ndr@draas.com kelsa -subject:"Please sign" -subject:"Please sign out"
  -subject:"Sign In" -subject:"Daily Summary" -from:no-reply@kelsa.io
```

`from:ndr@draas.com newer_than:15d -subject:"Please sign" -subject:"Daily Summary"` is the
reliable "what did NDR actually write to me recently" sweep.

## 2. Multi-axis ladder — run all of these before saying "not found"

1. Sender + topic: `from:<person> (<term1> OR <term2>)`
2. Recipient: `to:<addr>` and `in:sent` variants (mail sent BY the user, not to them)
3. Subject fragment: `subject:(upgradation OR implementation OR integration OR automation)`
4. Body terms from the likely text: `"allotment letter"`, `"cost sheet"`, `"sale agreement"`
5. Domain sweep of the outside party: `sinchana@kelsa.io`, `@kelsa.io`, `"O3 Infotech"`
6. No date filter — one pass with NO `newer_than`/`before`
7. `in:anywhere` (bare queries silently drop Trash + Spam)

## 3. When the sweep is genuinely empty — the report, not a guess

State: mailbox swept, axes used, and that the only hits are X. Then offer exactly TWO named
options:

1. "Give me one hint — rough month, or 3–4 words from the subject line."
2. "Say *search <person>'s mailbox*" — and only then use DWD on that person's account.

Never quietly search another @draas.com person's mailbox because the mail "should" be there;
never invent a subject/date to look helpful. A user who is *certain* the mail is in his own
inbox is frequently remembering a mail he was shown, or one addressed to his colleague, not
one delivered to him. Verified: no NDR→Bharat mail on Kelsa upgradation existed in Bharat's
mailbox at all, across every axis above.

## 4. Cross-check before concluding the wrong email

When the user says "not this one" about a candidate you found, do not just re-rank — enumerate
what else exists on the topic and say how many there are ("the only Kelsa integration mail in
your mailbox is the 23-Oct one"). Volume is a fact to establish, not to assume.
