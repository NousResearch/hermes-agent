# Anti-Slop Quality Gates (A–L)

Apply these detectors at every drafting and revision stage. Flag the specific line or passage. Provide concrete replacement.

## Gate A — Generic Dialogue
Flag: Lines interchangeable between characters.
Fix: Tie to specific voice fingerprint, current want, power, and history. Vary rhythm and vocabulary.

## Gate B — Theme Declaration
Flag: Dialogue that states the "lesson" directly.
Fix: Dramatize through choice and consequence. Let the audience discover the tension.

## Gate C — Emotional Labeling
Flag: "She was furious." "He felt a wave of sadness."
Fix: Translate to behavior, object interaction, physical tells, strategic language, or silence.

## Gate D — Exposition Dump
Flag: Unmotivated block of information delivery.
Fix: Motivate via conflict, procedure, persuasion, misunderstanding, or urgent consequence. Break into smaller beats.

## Gate E — False Cinematic Language
Examples to reject:
- "A tense silence fell."
- "The weight of the moment hung in the air."
- "Everything was about to change."
- "A storm brewed inside him."
- "Time seemed to stop."
- "An eerie feeling filled the room."
- "Their eyes said everything."

Fix: Replace with observable image, sound, action, or spatial fact.

## Gate F — Narrative Compression (Unearned Change)
Flag: Instant trust, sudden romance, unearned forgiveness, abrupt competence, ideological flip, unexplained betrayal, effortless victory, instant grief recovery.
Fix: Show the pressure, decision, cost, or repeated failure that produces the change.

## Gate G — Continuity Drift
See continuity.md. Run ledger audit explicitly.

## Gate H — Passive Protagonist
Flag: Extended sections where protagonist only reacts or observes without choice or meaningful refusal.
Fix: Give them an active objective in the scene; force a decision or refusal that costs something.

## Gate I — Repetitive Scene Dynamics
Flag: Consecutive scenes with same objective type, emotional register, conflict pattern, or outcome.
Fix: Vary pressure source, tactic, stakes, or outcome. Map emotional arc across sequence.

## Gate J — Unfilmable Writing
Flag: Internal explanation, abstract atmosphere, inaccessible backstory as prose, "the room felt heavy with regret."
Fix: Externalize through action, props, behavior, or motivated dialogue. Cut what cannot be seen or heard.

## Gate K — Trailerization
Flag: Sequences of impressive images, cryptic lines, slogans, and dramatic fragments without causal tissue.
Fix: Reconnect with decision → consequence → complication. Add connective beats.

## Gate L — Synthetic Symmetry
Flag: "Not because X, but because Y", three-part aphorisms, mirrored declarations, excessive rhetorical questions, polished antithesis without voice justification.
Fix: Use only when the character would speak that way. Break symmetry for most voices.

## Gate M — Symbol Explanation
Flag any line that explains a symbol the scene has already made legible.

## Gate N — Occult Collage
Flag arbitrary mixtures of sigils, astrology, tarot, sacred geometry, alchemy, deity imagery, numerology without a governing grammar.

## Gate O — Symbolic Redundancy
Flag when composition, dialogue, prop, music and color all communicate the exact same meaning simultaneously. Use two channels at most unless deliberate overdetermination is dramatically justified.

## Gate P — One-to-One Symbolism
Flag simplistic equations (bird = freedom, water = emotion, mirror = identity, red = danger). Require contextual transformation.

## Gate Q — Repetition Without Mutation
Flag recurring images that do not change meaning, ownership, context, form or consequence.

## Gate R — Archetype Costume
Flag characters who resemble archetypes only through clothing, names, props or iconography but do not enact the archetypal function.

## Gate S — Tradition Flattening
Flag unsupported equivalence or aesthetic blending across cultural and religious systems.

## Gate T — Numerology Inflation
Flag arbitrary number insertion that has no perceptible structural effect.

## Gate U — Symbolic Supremacy
Flag scenes where symbolic design compromises: causality, character agency, spatial clarity, tone, emotional credibility, production feasibility.

## Gate V — Mystery by Obscurity
Flag ambiguity created merely by withholding basic causal information or using cryptic dialogue. True symbolic ambiguity preserves clear events while leaving their larger relation open.

## Gate W — Premature Closure
Flag explicit confirmation of the “correct” symbolic interpretation.

## Application

In DIAGNOSE mode: list gates triggered (A–W) with line references.
In DRAFT/REVISE: repair before delivering.
In POLISH: final gate sweep.

Document any deliberate violations with justification. When symbolic dramaturgy (Module 5B) is active, explicitly run Gates M–W.
