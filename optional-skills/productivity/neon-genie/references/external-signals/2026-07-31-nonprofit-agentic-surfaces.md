## 1. Macro Adoption vs Impact Gap

### OBSERVED
- 85–92% of nonprofits report using AI in some capacity (2026 sector surveys).
- Only ~7–33% report major organizational capability gains or highly effective use. A small “AI-Adaptive” cohort (~10%) captures most of the measurable upside (revenue, retention, productivity).
- Average donor retention remains low (~43% year-over-year). Even modest lifts from better stewardship agents produce material revenue without new acquisition.
- AI-assisted grant processes are reported to raise win rates (historically ~15–20% → claimed 35–45% range in some analyses) while cutting staff time per application.
- Larger nonprofits (200+ employees) show higher rates of piloting or deploying autonomous agents (~80% in one 2026 dataset), while governance lags badly.

### INFERRED
The sector has crossed the awareness and experimentation threshold. The bottleneck has shifted from “should we use AI?” to “how do we turn fragmented individual use into governed, outcome-producing agent systems under extreme capacity and compliance constraints.”

### Signal classes
human friction · institutional friction · economic friction · repeated workarounds

## 2. Agentic Giving & Fundraising Agents

### OBSERVED
- **Agentic Giving** has been explicitly named as a new category (Fundraise Up, late July 2026): donors state intent to an AI assistant; the assistant discovers, evaluates, and completes (or proposes) the gift. Shift from navigation to delegation.
- Voice-driven, agentic fundraising platforms are shipping (e.g., CharityEngine Copilot): agents that do not only answer questions but execute (launch campaigns, update records, segment audiences) inside a unified data plane.
- Classic fundraising workflows (donor scoring, lapse prediction, personalized outreach, grant drafting, board packets) are the most common early agent targets.

### INFERRED
The commercial surface is moving upstream from “better donation form” to “infrastructure that lets trusted agents act on behalf of donors and on behalf of nonprofit staff.” Nonprofits that are not agent-discoverable and agent-actionable risk becoming invisible in an agent-mediated discovery layer.

### SPECULATIVE surfaces
- Agent-readable impact data, campaign cards, and verifiable outcome objects that AI assistants can trust.
- Scoped spend / gift-completion agents with strong donor consent and audit trails.
- Retention and upgrade agents that close the loop with measurable lifts in the 43% baseline retention rate.

**Maps to Isenberg:** #07 (phone / voice agent analogue), #02 (spend controls for gift agents), #12 (AI enablement for the other 95%), #03 (judgment layer for impact claims).

### Signal classes
economic friction · technology frontier · repeated workarounds

## 3. Capacity, Governance & Security Pressure

### OBSERVED
- Nonprofits face chronic under-staffing, high turnover, and budget constraints. Agents are positioned as a capacity multiplier rather than a headcount replacement.
- Governance vacuum is severe: large majorities lack formal AI strategy or restrictions on tool use → widespread shadow AI.
- Security threat density has risen sharply. One 2026 identity dataset reported nonprofit login attempts that are fraudulent rising to ~78%, making the sector the most-attacked in the sample.
- AWS and others have published explicit governance frameworks for nonprofit agentic AI (boundaries, identity, observability, auditability) recognizing board accountability and compliance requirements.
- Nonprofits are increasingly performing de-facto welfare-state functions (triage for food, shelter, medical referrals) with algorithmic decision support, raising transparency and bias concerns.

### INFERRED
Any durable agent deployment in this sector must solve identity, scoped permissions, auditability, and explainability earlier and more strictly than many commercial verticals. The cost of failure includes mission, regulatory, and donor-trust damage.

### Signal classes
institutional friction · human friction · technology frontier

## 4. Funding & Enablement Flows

### OBSERVED
- Major AI labs and foundations are directing capital specifically at nonprofits + AI (OpenAI Foundation People-First AI Fund at $50M scale with unrestricted grants to mid-size community organizations; Google.org accelerators and challenges; other Tech for Good grants).
- AI-linked philanthropy is projected to grow substantially as wealth from foundation-model companies is committed.
- Enablement programs (fellows, accelerators, literacy initiatives) are proliferating, aiming to move organizations from experimentation to systemic use.

### INFERRED
Capital and tooling are available; the scarce resources remain implementation capacity, clean data, governance design, and products that fit extreme resource constraints.

### Signal classes
economic friction · institutional friction · unused capacity

## 7. Synthesis for Neon Genie Opportunity Mining

The nonprofit sector presents a distinctive combination:
- Extreme capacity constraints (high willingness for leverage).
- High adoption, low systemic impact (clear blocked transition).
- Rising security and governance pressure (must-have, not nice-to-have).
- Emerging category definition around Agentic Giving.
- Dedicated philanthropic capital looking for implementation partners.

**Highest-leverage immediate targets for a governed run:**
1. Agentic Giving / donor-agent infrastructure (discovery + secure completion).
2. Retention and stewardship agents with externally checkable lifts.
3. Nonprofit-grade governance, identity, and audit layers for agent fleets.
4. Grant/compliance agents that reduce staff time while improving package quality.

These surfaces sit at the intersection of economic friction, institutional friction, and technology frontier, with relatively concrete buyers (nonprofit executive / development leadership, platforms serving them, and funders underwriting capacity).
