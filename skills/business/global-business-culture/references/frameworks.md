# Frameworks: the documented foundation

Five research-backed frameworks. Triangulate at least two before writing a brief for an uncovered country. None ranks cultures; all describe relative differences.

## 1. Erin Meyer — The Culture Map (2014), 8 scales

Each culture sits on a spectrum; **only the relative gap between two cultures predicts friction**.

| Scale | Low pole | High pole | Business consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Communicating | Low-context (US, DE, NL): precise, explicit, repeated | High-context (JP, CN, KE, LA): implied, between the lines | Read silence and context; repeat less, listen more |
| Evaluating | Direct negative feedback (NL, DE, RU) | Indirect negative feedback (US softens, JP, TH) | Wrap criticism; never correct in public |
| Persuading | Principles-first (FR, RU, IT): theory → application | Applications-first (US, CA): case → rule | Lead with data and cases, or with concepts, to match |
| Leading | Egalitarian (DK, NL, AU) | Hierarchical (JP, CN, SA, NG) | Titles, seating, greeting order matter |
| Deciding | Consensual (JP, SE, DE) | Top-down (IN, CN, RU, EA) | Find the real decision-maker; allow consensus time |
| Trusting | Task-based (US, DE, AU): trust from work delivered | Relationship-based (CN, BR, SA, NG, LA): trust from meals, time, personal ties | Invest in the relationship BEFORE the agenda |
| Disagreeing | Confrontational (FR, DE, NL): debate is fine | Avoids confrontation (JP, CN, TH, LA, most of Asia/Africa) | No open debate in meetings; dissent moves offline |
| Scheduling | Linear-time (DE, CH, JP, US) | Flexible-time (IN, ME, LA, most of Africa/LatAm) | Buffer time; deadlines renegotiate around relationships |

Meyer mapped ~30–67 countries across these scales (source: *The Culture Map*, 2014; HBR "Navigating the Cultural Minefield", 2014).

## 2. Hofstede — 6 cultural dimensions

0–100 scores per country. Most business-relevant:

- **Power Distance (PDI)** — acceptance of unequal power. High (80+: PH 94, MY 100, several Gulf states) → deference, subordinates wait for instructions; Low (AT 11, DK 18) → challenge the boss, first names.
- **Individualism (IDV)** — US 91, UK 89, AU 90 vs. Guatemala 6, CN 20, TH 20, ID 14 (Laos: no published score; regionally collectivist). Collectivist → group credit, family hiring, loyalty over rules.
- **Uncertainty Avoidance (UAI)** — high (GR 112, PT 99) wants rules and detail; low (SG 8, DK 23) tolerates ambiguity, improvises.
- **Long-Term Orientation (LTO)** — CN 87, JP 88 vs. US 26: patience as strategy, relationship maintenance as investment.
- **Masculinity/Motivation (MAS)** and **Indulgence (IVR)** — secondary for deal work.

Sources: Hofstede, *Culture's Consequences* / Hofstede Insights; ACCA technical article "Hofstede cultural dimensions".

## 3. GLOBE — 10 societal clusters, endorsed leadership

62 societies, 17,300 managers (House et al. 2004). Clusters: Anglo, Nordic Europe, Germanic Europe, Latin Europe, Eastern Europe, Sub-Saharan Africa, Middle East, Southern Asia, Confucian Asia, Latin America.

Six endorsed leadership dimensions — charismatic/value-based, team-oriented, participative, humane-oriented, autonomous, self-protective:

- **Anglo + Nordic + Germanic**: participative leadership valued highly; Germany most autonomous.
- **Middle East + Eastern Europe**: participative LOW; self-protective/autonomous higher — authority is personal.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa**: highest humane-orientation score globally — leaders expected to be generous, considerate, communal.
- **Confucian + Southern Asia**: team + humane oriented, participative low — harmony with hierarchy.

Use GLOBE to predict how a counterpart's *leaders* are expected to behave, and who gets listened to.

Sources: globeproject.com/study_2004_2007; OpenStax Principles of Management ch. 6.3; House & Javidan 2004.

## 4. Lewis — 3 behavioral types (*When Cultures Collide*, 1996/2005)

- **Linear-active** (Germanic, US, UK, Nordics, JP-ish): task-oriented planners, one thing at a time, agenda-driven, punctual.
- **Multi-active** (Latin Europe, Latin America, Arab world, Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia): people-oriented, many things at once, relationship-driven, flexible with agendas and time.
- **Reactive** (CN, JP, KR, VN, TH, FI): listen first, rarely initiate, courtesy and harmony, silence is meaningful.

Lewis's practical gift: meeting behavior. Linear-actives want the agenda; multi-actives want the relationship; reactives want to not be rushed.

## 5. Trompenaars — 7 dilemmas (use selectively)

- **Universalism vs. particularism** (rules vs. relationships): US/CH/DE universalist; CN/VE/RU particularist — "the contract is subordinate to the relationship" is literally the particularist answer.
- **Neutral vs. affective**: display of emotion in business (JP, UK neutral; IT, LA, ME affective).
- **Specific vs. diffuse** (separate work/private life): US specific; CN/ES/MEA diffuse — dinner IS the deal.
- **Achievement vs. ascription**: status from performance (US, AU) vs. age/family/lineage (CN, IT, much of Africa/Asia) — send the senior person.

## Triangulation procedure (uncovered country)

1. GLOBE cluster → leadership + humane-orientation expectations.
2. Lewis type → meeting/time behavior.
3. Meyer anchors of neighbors → the 3 scales that matter for the task.
4. Hofstede PDI/IDV if scores exist → hierarchy + group orientation.
5. Overlay LDC/informal-economy layer if GDP per capita < ~USD 4k or informal employment dominant.
6. Verify current specifics with `web_search`; mark everything extrapolated as such.
