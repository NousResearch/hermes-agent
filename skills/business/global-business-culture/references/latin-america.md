# Latin America (Mexico, Brazil, Andean, Southern Cone)

Schema: Snapshot / Communication / Trust / Decisions / Negotiation & contracts / Etiquette / Taboos / Variation / Field moves.

## Shared pattern

**Personalismo**: business runs on personal trust in an individual, not the institution they represent. Relationship-first, hierarchy respected more than questioned, warmth (simpatía) is a scored professional trait, and time is polychronic — Hall's flexible-time at scale (Rework LatAm guide; EDC Canada). GLOBE Latin America cluster: charismatic/value-based leadership ranked highest of all clusters; autonomous leadership LOW — teams and warmth over lone-wolf styles.

## Communication

- Expressive, warm, high-context relative to the US: evasive or delayed responses often mean "more discussion needed before yes" — direct negatives are avoided (EDC). Relationship maintenance language wraps every message.
- Soft confrontation: disagreement phrased to preserve the relationship; volume and interruption (overlapping speech) signal engagement, not disrespect.

## Trust

- Personal: meals, family inquiry, sustained presence. Cold calling is ineffective; the warm introduction is the channel. Exchange of favors is normal social lubricant — distinguish from corruption (see ldc-playbook compliance section).
- Simpatía/confianza (Mexico): your counterpart is quietly scoring warmth and trustworthiness in every early interaction; confianza, once earned, unlocks flexibility impossible before it.

## Decisions

- Concentrated at the top even when org charts look flat; seniority visible (office size, who speaks last). Decisions take longer than US tempo; pushing hard deadlines reads as disrespect.

## Negotiation & contracts

- Slow by Northern standards: small talk, multiple meetings, meals. High initial price expectations negotiated down gradually; when you win a concession, acknowledge it as the counterpart's initiative (relationship math). "Final" has a soft border — last-minute reopening happens; stay warm.
- Contracts: Brazil detail-oriented and slow contract review (allow time); Chile more contract-formal; personal guarantees/relationships still carry enforcement weight everywhere; deadline renegotiation tied to seniority and trust (Global Negotiator).

## Etiquette

- Standoff-close conversational distance (backing away reads cold); sustained eye contact; formal dress; professional titles (Licenciado, Ingeniero, Doctor) until invited; long business lunches/dinners (sobremesa — the after-meal talk where real alignment happens); punctuality expectations flex 10–30 min by country (meetings start late without offense — still arrive on time as the foreigner); greeting: firm handshake, eye contact; in business contexts a light cheek kiss is common woman-to-woman / man-to-woman socially, not in first formal meetings.

## Taboos

- Rushing to business; checking watch/phone during a meal; public criticism or blunt "no"; discussing politics (per-country: PRI/PAN, Bolsonaro/Lula, chavismo) or personal finances; assuming Spanish works in Brazil (Portuguese — attempting it earns enormous goodwill); comparing countries to Argentina with Argentines or to Mexico with Mexicans; US-centrism ("America" = the continent).

## Variation

- **Brazil:** warmest, most flexible, improvisational; relationship depth extreme; humor and personal closeness open doors. **Mexico:** NAFTA-hybrid professionalism + personalismo; strong hierarchy; dinner culture. **Andean (Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Colombia):** more formal, more indirect, patience at premium. **Southern Cone (Chile, Argentina, Uruguay):** most European-formal; Chile punctual-contractual by regional standards; Argentina relationship + drama, economic-volatility reflexes (dollar hedging talk is normal business).
- **Remedy for gringo-directness:** warmth calibration — open warmer, slow 20%, add 2 relationship touches per deal cycle.

## Field moves

1. Front-load relationship: accept the long meal, attend the sobremesa, reciprocate invitations.
2. Bring the senior: title-matching matters; send someone empowered AND senior.
3. Negotiate with patience theater: never show hurry; deadlines flex or kill deals.
4. Convert "mañana" ambiguity: friendly written recaps with dates + named owners (warm tone, precise content — same move as South Asia).
5. Learn names + families + football allegiances; remember them next visit.
