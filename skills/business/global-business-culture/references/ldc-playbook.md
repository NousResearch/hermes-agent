# Least-developed & frontier-market playbook (the LDC overlay)

Apply this file ON TOP OF the region file whenever the economy is informal-dominant, institutionally thin, or conflict/recovery-affected (Laos, Cambodia, Myanmar, much of Sub-Saharan Africa, Afghanistan-type contexts, small island states). This layer is where 20 years of operating experience live; it is what the frameworks do not cover.

## The documented base

- Informal sector in a typical developing country: **~70% of employment, ~30% of GDP**; Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia medians ~90% informal employment; some SSA economies: informal output up to 62% of GDP (World Bank, *Informality: Why Is It So Widespread...* 2019; *The Long Shadow of Informality*, Ohnsorge & Yu eds. 2021).
- Where courts, registries, and contract enforcement are weak or slow, **relationships and reputation substitute for legal enforcement** — this is rational, not backward. The verbal commitment from a person of standing can be MORE enforceable socially than a court judgment is practically.

## What changes operationally

1. **Contract strategy.** The written contract is a relationship marker and an anchor for the future — not a weapon you will realistically wield. Draft it short, clear, and renegotiation-friendly; put REAL enforcement in structure instead: milestones, escrow, retained local partner, personal guarantees, payment-on-delivery, small initial volumes that grow with trust.
2. **The verbal-writer loop.** Frontier deals run on verbal agreement; your protection is same-day written memos: "As discussed today... to be confirmed." Gentle, consistent, never weaponized in dispute. Over years this builds a record AND a reputation for professionalism without face-cost.
3. **Decision anatomy.** The org chart lies. Map: (a) the beneficial owner/family, (b) the patron whose word moves ministries, (c) the operator who actually executes. Often three different people. The deal dies if (a) or (b) was skipped.
4. **Timeline physics.** 2–3× your home-market duration is normal; long silent gaps are normal (harvest, festivals, funerals, Ramadan-equivalents, visa runs). Budget liveness: a short warm check-in message during silence maintains the channel without pressure.
5. **Cash and rails.** Expect cash-dominant micro-economies; mobile money (M-Pesa and siblings) is often the real settlement rail for staff, suppliers, agents — design payment flows around agent networks, not bank branches (World Bank mobile money evidence review). Bring/bank notes professionally: clean USD accepted and sometimes preferred for large items in some markets; know local FX rules BEFORE quoting.
6. **Bureaucracy and gatekeepers.** Permits move at the pace of relationships. Use ONE trusted fixer/lawyer for interface with the state; let them absorb the queue. Every original document is precious: certified copies, spares, laminates — lost paper costs months.
7. **Family + patronage economics.** Family hiring is trust architecture, not nepotism. The nephew on the payroll is often the succession plan — treat him accordingly. Favors create enforceable social debt; track what you owe and settle visibly.
8. **Corruption red lines (non-negotiable).** Meals, patience, respect, presence = legitimate. Cash/gifts to officials for decisions = FCPA (US persons/companies) and UK Bribery Act (UK nexus) exposure regardless of local normality; "facilitation payments" criminalize under UKBA. Practical stance: pay nothing that would embarrass you in a discovery process; build the wasta/relationship layer the legitimate way (more time, right introductions); when a "speed payment" is solicited, the answer is policy, not personality ("our compliance requires..."). Flag every gray-zone ask to the user with specifics.
9. **Donor/NGO/diplomatic overlay.** In aid-heavy economies, a parallel formal sector exists (NGOs, embassies, DFIs): deadline-driven, compliance-heavy, per-diem culture. Behaves like a Western counterparty with local constraints. Useful bridge: NGO-vetted local partners carry pre-verified reputations.
10. **Risk cadence.** Frontier markets re-price politics fast: coups, currency steps, border closures. Monthly `web_search` sweep on each active market (politics, FX, fuel, regulations); maintain an exit-aware structure (assets light, cash fast, IP home).

## Practitioner notes — Laos operating rules (contributed by the author, 20 years)

- The introduction IS the deal: enter through a mutually respected third party; cold approaches are near-worthless.
- First meeting: relationship only. Accept the dinner. The agenda is the relationship.
- Never force an on-the-spot answer; a Lao "yes" under pressure is worth less than a considered silence.
- Serve hierarchy in everything: greeting order, seating, serving order, who receives the gift.
- Small unprompted favors early (a scarce introduction, help with a document) buy more than any discount.
- After the verbal agreement: a one-page warm memo the same day. After signing: stay present — absence is renegotiation.
- Contracts: short, bilingual (Lao prevails), milestone-structured. The relationship enforces; the paper reminds.
