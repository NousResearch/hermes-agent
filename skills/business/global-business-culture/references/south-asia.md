# South Asia (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal)

Schema: Snapshot / Communication / Trust / Decisions / Negotiation & contracts / Etiquette / Taboos / Variation / Field moves.

## Shared pattern

High power distance, family-centered collectivism, relationship before transaction, flexible time, indirect refusals, and **jugaad** (जुगाड़) — improvised, resourceful problem-solving under constraint (documented widely; see Rework "Business Culture in South Asia", TMF Group, Kaiwa etiquette guide). GLOBE Southern Asia cluster: self-protective + charismatic + humane-oriented leadership endorsed; participative ranked LOW — the boss decides.

## India

**Snapshot.** Hierarchical, relationship-based, price-sensitive, negotiation-loving; the largest and most varied market in the region.

- **Communication.** Politeness-driven indirection: "We'll try", "It may be difficult", topic changes and deferrals are the "no". Head-wobble is ambiguity-tolerant acknowledgment, not yes/no. English fluent — but fluency ≠ directness.
- **Trust.** Personal foundation before the transaction: a first meeting of small talk and family questions is the trust-building mechanism, not a delay. Tea/meal invitations are relationship infrastructure.
- **Decisions.** The senior person has the final say; decisions without visible senior sign-off are provisional. Family businesses consult the family. Multiple meetings are normal; timelines stretch. "Indian Standard Time" for internal starts — BUT client-facing delivery dates (esp. IT/services) are treated seriously: distinguish the two.
- **Negotiation & contracts.** Bargaining expected, price anchored high both ways, discounts ritual. Contracts: detailed and increasingly enforceable, yet flexibility may be requested after signing — frame the contract as a living schedule with change mechanics, not a fortress. A reflexive "yes" in a meeting is relationship-smoothing; the signal is the follow-up (specifics written, clarifying questions, dates).
- **Etiquette.** Greet the most senior first ("Sir/Madam", titles + surnames until invited); business cards exchanged respectfully; right hand for giving/receiving/eating; modest dress; remove shoes when invited into homes/offices; accept offered tea.
- **Taboos.** Public criticism; pushing for fast commitment; discussing caste/religion/politics unprompted; assuming the junior in the room is the note-taker (may be the nephew who inherits the deal); left-hand exchanges; beef/alcohol assumptions; personal questions about salary/marriage unless they ask first (they will — reciprocate gently).
- **Variation.** Bangalore/Delhi/Mumbai corporates far more Western-paced than family firms in tier-2/3 cities; South India more formal/conservative than the north; Gujarati/Marwari trading communities distinct deal cultures.
- **Field moves.** Convert polite yeses into commitments: "walk me through the steps to get this done by Friday" + same-day written recap with owner and date (TMF conversion framework). Build the senior relationship in parallel with the working level. Arrive with hierarchy: send your senior to meet their senior.

## Pakistan & Bangladesh

- Pakistan: stronger hierarchical formality + Islamic business rhythm (prayer times, Ramadan tempo, no-alcohol hospitality default); family-firm decision concentration; relationship loyalty is long once given; bargaining vigorous.
- Bangladesh: garment-driven export tempo with consensus-plus-patron internals; extreme politeness masks firm positions; verify verbal commitments through the trusted lieutenant; RMG sector runs on Western compliance audits — do the relationship work anyway.

## Sri Lanka & Nepal

- Sri Lanka: courteous, hierarchical, relationship-based; Buddhist holiday rhythm; multilingual business (EN/Sinhala/Tamil). Nepal: similar hierarchy + face; informal-economy overlay strong (see ldc-playbook.md); Kathmandu family networks concentrate decisions.

## Field moves (regional)

1. Sequence: senior introduction → relationship meetings → detailed negotiation → senior sign-off ritual.
2. Replace "can you do X by Friday?" with process questions that expose real constraints.
3. Everything agreed verbally gets a warm written recap — respectful tone, precise content.
4. Price: open with room, concede slowly and conditionally, expect a long final round after "final".
5. Honor the jugaad instinct: set hard fences only around compliance/quality/security; let method flexibility live.
