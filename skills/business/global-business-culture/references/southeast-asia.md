# Southeast Asia (ASEAN: Laos, Thailand, Vietnam, Cambodia, Myanmar, Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore)

Schema: Snapshot / Communication / Trust / Decisions / Negotiation & contracts / Etiquette / Taboos / Variation / Field moves.

## Laos — the anchor case (practitioner-documented)

**Snapshot.** Relationship-first, high-context, hierarchical, unhurried. The rule that surprises every Western-trained executive: **the verbal relationship IS the agreement; the contract is a formality that follows it, not the instrument that creates it.** Deals move at the speed of trust.

- **Communication.** Indirect; face (na) is central; direct refusals avoided — "yes" can mean "I heard you", not "I agree". People may give the reply they think you want while signaling discomfort non-verbally; read context and follow up privately. (Cultural Atlas; Expat Arrivals)
- **Trust.** Built across repeated meetings, shared meals, dinners, golf; family/village/long-standing networks decide who gets business. Introductions and reputation beat pitch and price. Hiring family and close friends is expected, not nepotism. (Austrade; Globelore)
- **Decisions.** Slow, hierarchical, consensus-laundered: information relays up and down the org; senior approval is sought privately. Identify the actual decision-maker — usually the most senior person, who may say least in the meeting. Decisions "can take a long time" (Austrade). Bargaining expected, but Laotians are gentle hagglers.
- **Negotiation & contracts.** Punctual to meetings but meetings have no rigid agenda; pushing hard deadlines or Western high-pressure closing damages trust. A short written memo after each verbal understanding keeps alignment WITHOUT being wielded as a weapon. Formal contracts often lag the real (verbal) agreement and get revisited as the relationship evolves.
- **Etiquette.** Punctual arrival; handshake now standard (the nop — palms together — remains the traditional greeting); cards in the initial meeting, ideally bilingual EN/LAO; address and defer to the most senior person; gifts small, presented with the right hand; stay calm and respectful always.
- **Taboos.** Public criticism or contradiction; touching anyone's head; pointing feet at people/Buddha images; women touching monks; raising your voice; rushing the senior person; political commentary about party/state; disrespecting Buddhism or the national religion-symbols.
- **Field moves (20 years of practice, distilled).** (1) Get introduced by a mutual trusted party, not a cold contact. (2) First meeting is relationship, not agenda — accept the long dinner. (3) Let silence work; never force an on-the-spot answer. (4) Serve the hierarchy: greet/seated/serve senior first. (5) Follow every verbal agreement with a gentle same-day memo ("as discussed") — it preserves face AND creates the record. (6) Budget 2–3× your home-market timeline. (7) Deliver a small favor early with no invoice — reciprocity is the currency.

## Thailand

- **Kreng jai** (เกรงใจ, considerate deference) governs: never impose, never force conflict; the smile has many meanings including embarrassment and refusal. Wai greeting by rank; cards two-handed; senior-first. Sanuk (fun) belongs in work. Decisions hierarchical but draped in consensus manners; "yes" may mean "I am being polite". Never touch heads, never point feet, NEVER disrespect the monarchy (legally dangerous). Meals and golf build the channel.

## Vietnam

- Relationship-based but noticeably faster and more commercial than Laos; hierarchy + Confucian formality with entrepreneurial drive. Face rules as in China; contracts treated as relationships' snapshots; bargaining vigorous but with a smile. Green-tea patience plus dôi-mói speed: expect both warm hospitality and hard price negotiation. Senior endorsement unlocks everything; mid-levels cannot commit.

## Cambodia & Myanmar

- patronage- and family-network-driven; formal hierarchy; extreme importance of respectful greetings and titles; informal-economy overlay strong (see ldc-playbook.md). Relationships and intermediaries are mandatory; timelines elastic; paperwork secondary to personal commitment.

## Indonesia

- **Musyawarah** (deliberation toward consensus) + **mufakat** (agreement): decisions emerge from patient consultation; Bapak (senior male patron) authority is real. Never cause public embarrassment; the left hand is unclean (give/receive with right); pork/alcohol care with Muslim counterparts; Friday prayers midday. Trust is warm, personal, long — the world's largest Muslim-majority market moves on relationship density.

## Malaysia

- Multi-ethnic triad: Malay (Muslim, hierarchical, relationship), Chinese (guanxi-driven, fastest deal tempo), Indian (family-firm hierarchy). Identify which culture you are actually negotiating with; adapt per file. Bumiputera policy awareness for JV structuring; halal etiquette defaults with Malay counterparts.

## Philippines

- **Personalismo** like LatAm: personal loyalty IS the business relationship; hiya (shame/face) and pakikisama (getting along) avoid refusal — an unanswered message can be the "no". English-language business, US-flavored formality, warm hospitality; hierarchy + family conglomerates decide; intermediaries (a respected mutual) open doors faster than any pitch.

## Singapore

- The regional counterweight: linear-active, punctual, contract-forward, low-corruption, English-speaking — treat as a Western-style counterparty with Asian face-sensitivity. Do not import flexible-time expectations here.
