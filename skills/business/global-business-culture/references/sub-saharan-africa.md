# Sub-Saharan Africa (West, East, Southern Africa)

Schema: Snapshot / Communication / Trust / Decisions / Negotiation & contracts / Etiquette / Taboos / Variation / Field moves.

## Shared pattern

Communal, relationship-first, high-context, humane-oriented at the maximum of any GLOBE cluster (Sub-Saharan Africa scores the world's highest humane-orientation endorsement — GLOBE 2004). Ubuntu — "umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu", *a person is a person through other people* — frames decisions as communal and businesses as webs of reciprocity, respect for elders, compassion, consensus (Mangaliso; Khoza; UP theses). Lewis classifies the region multi-active: many things at once, relationship-driven, agenda-flexible.

## Communication & greetings

- **Greetings are the meeting's opening transaction and non-negotiable.** Extended greetings (health, family, journey — 5–10 minutes) signal respect; skipping them reads as arrogance. In many West African cultures the elder is greeted FIRST and by title. (Documented across regional etiquette literature.)
- Politeness indirection: direct refusals avoided; "I will try" and silence carry meaning. Age is automatically seniority regardless of rank/title/education — an older junior outranks a young executive in courtesy (Amoako-Agyei; UP Ubuntu thesis).
- Handshakes: common, sometimes longer than Western reflex; a softer grip can signal respect, not weakness (regional etiquette literature — do not read it as low energy).

## Trust

- Personal trust precedes everything: visits, calls, meals, remembering family details. Business follows relationship, and relationships live in communities — your reputation transits the network faster than any contract.
- Communal consultation: decisions reference family/community stakeholders. Patience with this is itself a trust signal.

## Decisions & meetings

- Hierarchy + consensus blend: the senior elder/patron blesses; consultation (family, board, community) follows; timelines flex ("African time" — buffers, and meeting start times are lower bounds). Formal agendas secondary to relationship maintenance.

## Negotiation & contracts

- Bargaining expected across markets; opening prices leave room. Contracts increasingly formal (especially banking/extractives/telco) but enforcement reality is relational — local partners and reputation are your real security. Payment terms drift; build in milestones and local collection relationships. In frontier markets overlay ldc-playbook.md (informal employment >90% in some SSA economies; informal output up to ~62% of GDP — World Bank).

## Etiquette

- Greet everyone individually on arrival and departure (never a wave to the room); right hand or both hands for exchanges; two-handed handshake-with-left-hand-supporting-forearm shows respect in several cultures; pass/receive objects with right hand; modest formal dress; accept offered tea/food; titles + honorifics; standing when seniors enter; inquire about family generally.

## Taboos

- Rushing to business before greetings; disrespecting an elder (career-ending); public criticism; left-hand exchanges; photographing without consent; assuming Anglophone/Francophone norms are interchangeable; discussing tribal/ethnic politics; skipping funeral/wedding attendance for significant partners (presence is relationship currency).

## Variation

- **West Africa (Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire):** busiest bargaining cultures; Nigerian deal tempo fast and vigorous beneath the courtesy; Ghana gentler pace. Anglophone vs Francophone formality differs (Francophone more formal, French-language courtesies).
- **East Africa (Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Ethiopia):** softer-spoken, consensus-strong; Ethiopia: distinct calendar/clock conventions + Orthodox Christian rhythm; coffee ceremony is relationship infrastructure.
- **Southern Africa (South Africa, Botswana, Namibia, Zambia, Zimbabwe):** South Africa bicultural: white business culture closer to Anglo directness; black African business culture ubuntu-communal — read your counterpart, not the average. Botswana: measured consensus + kgotla tradition. Donor/NGO-heavy economies (Malawi, Zambia) carry their own project-culture tempo.

## Field moves

1. Open every call/meeting with real greetings; ask after the family; never truncate them.
2. Map eldership: identify the senior-most person and the community stakeholders in any decision.
3. Invest in presence: visits beat video; attend the weddings/funerals of key partners when invited.
4. Bargain warmly with big margins; anchor on relationship-length ("we will be here ten years").
5. Pair every contract with a local partner/reputation strategy — that is what actually enforces it.
6. Overlay `references/ldc-playbook.md` for informal-economy mechanics, mobile money rails (M-Pesa et al.), and compliance discipline.
