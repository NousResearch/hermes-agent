# Western business cultures (Anglo, Germanic, Nordic, Latin Europe)

Schema: Snapshot / Communication / Trust / Decisions / Negotiation & contracts / Etiquette / Taboos / Variation / Field moves.

## United States & Canada (Anglo)

**Snapshot.** Low-context, task-based trust, top-down-ish but egalitarian manners, applications-first persuasion, linear time. Fastest tempo in the West: relationship and deal compressed together.

- **Communication.** Explicit, positive-framed, repeated in writing. "How are you" is a greeting, not a question. Directness is softened by optimism ("this is great, one small tweak...").
- **Trust.** Task-based: reliability, responsiveness, delivered results. Small talk is short and instrumental. Email counts as relationship.
- **Decisions.** Fast, individual, empowered mid-levels; reverses equally fast. Get the decision in the meeting.
- **Negotiation & contracts.** The contract IS the deal; exhaustive, enforceable, lawyers involved early. Time-to-close pressure is real; anchors aggressive; concessions traded, not gifted.
- **Etiquette.** First names immediately; punctuality assumed; business cards unceremonious; gifts can raise compliance flags (keep nominal, check corporate policy).
- **Taboos.** Overstaying agenda; asking personal questions (age, family, salary); interrupting; dirt jokes; touching on politics/religion unprompted.
- **Variation.** Canada: softer, more consensus-flavored, less overt anchoring. US South/Silicon Valley warmer/slower than East Coast finance.
- **Field moves.** Lead with outcome + numbers; leave-behind one-pager; confirm next steps with dates in the first email after.

## United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand

**Snapshot.** Anglo but more indirect than the US; understatement is the register; humor (incl. self-deprecation) is a trust tool.

- **Communication.** Indirection by US standards: "interesting" often means "I disagree"; "perhaps we should consider" = "do it". Irony in meetings is normal — do not read it as unprofessional.
- **Trust.** Task-based but cooled by reserve; competence shown quietly. Understatement of your own achievements beats promotion.
- **Decisions.** Consensus-seeking committee culture in UK corporates; slower than the US, faster than Germany.
- **Negotiation & contracts.** Contract enforceable and detailed, but relationship tone still governs renewal decisions.
- **Etiquette.** Punctuality matters; queuing and politeness formulas matter more than visitors expect; after-work pub culture (UK/AU) is where alignment actually happens — attend.
- **Taboos.** Brashness, overselling, taking irony literally, discussing money/religion early.
- **Field moves.** Match understatement; use humor carefully; read "fine" skeptically.

## Germanic Europe (Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Netherlands)

**Snapshot.** Lowest-context communicators in the West; Meyer places German feedback among the most direct globally; punctuality is moral, not logistic.

- **Communication.** Blunt negative feedback is respect, not aggression — criticism of the idea is not criticism of the person. Detail, data, qualifications. Small talk minimal.
- **Trust.** Task-based + credential-based: titles, engineering rigor, delivered specs. Netherlands: more egalitarian, even blunter, agenda-disciplined meetings.
- **Decisions.** Surprisingly consensual (Meyer: Germany consensual-once, then immovable): long analysis, then a decision that does NOT reopen. Do not push for a decision at the first meeting.
- **Negotiation & contracts.** Contract is near-sacred and detailed; deviations are renegotiated formally, never by vibe. Price anchoring moderate; facts beat rapport.
- **Etiquette.** Punctual to the minute; agenda sent and followed; Sie/formality until invited; gifts modest; dinners do not usually carry deal weight.
- **Taboos.** Late arrival; exaggeration/marketing speak ("world-class"); joking in early meetings; calling at 17:05 Friday.
- **Variation.** Switzerland: more formal, consensus even slower. Austria: hierarchical + formal. Netherlands: informal dress + blunt, fast.
- **Field moves.** Send data before the meeting; state weaknesses of your own proposal proactively — it buys credibility; put agreed steps in writing the same day.

## Nordic Europe (Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Finland)

**Snapshot.** Most egalitarian on earth (DK PDI 18); flat hierarchies, silence is comfortable, decisions by wide consensus, low formality.

- **Communication.** Modest, literal, quiet; modesty law-of-Jante flavor: nobody is more special. pauses are thinking, not awkwardness.
- **Trust.** High default institutional trust — fast to trust, unforgiving of breached specifics. Summer (mid-June–August) is dead time; plan around it.
- **Decisions.** Consensual and slow-feeling but with strong buy-in; empower juniors genuinely — the most junior engineer's objection can stop a deal.
- **Negotiation & contracts.** Fewer lawyers, shorter contracts than Germany, but equally binding; honesty assumed — hidden defects destroy the relationship permanently.
- **Etiquette.** First names, no titles; punctuality; no gift expectation; coffee breaks sacred in Sweden (fika).
- **Taboos.** Flashy claims, status displays, interrupting, overselling past a clear no, scheduling July meetings.
- **Field moves.** Include the technical junior in your pitch; accept silence after your offer; be explicit about risks — it reads as integrity.

## Latin Europe (France, Italy, Spain, Portugal)

**Snapshot.** Latin Europe bridges West and South: hierarchical (FR), principles-first persuasion (FR), more affective and relationship-flavored than the north, flexible time in the south.

- **Communication.** France: formal, rhetorical, principles-first (theory before case), debate respected — disagreeing openly is engagement, not attack. Italy/Spain: expressive, interrupting shows interest.
- **Trust.** France: credential + intellectual respect; long lunches are working relationship infrastructure. Italy/Spain: personal warmth precedes business trust.
- **Decisions.** France: top-down by the (often grand-école) senior; Italy: owner/family decides (many SMEs are family firms); Spain: hierarchy with consultative manners.
- **Negotiation & contracts.** France: contract detailed but the intellectual framing of the deal matters; expect vigorous debate on principles. Italy: personal guarantee of the principal can outweigh paper; payment terms drift — build in milestones.
- **Etiquette.** Formal address (vous/Lei) until invited; good dress; long lunches (FR/IT) are the real meeting; gifts: quality wine/artisanal, not lavish.
- **Taboos.** Poor French/Italian attempts at chit-chat are forgiven, but sloppy dress or discussing money first is not; never rush a French lunch.
- **Field moves.** Bring the senior counterpart; engage the debate rather than deflecting it; confirm everything in writing while staying warm — formality of paper ≠ coldness.
