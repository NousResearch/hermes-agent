# IFS Background

Use this reference when the task involves Self, parts, inner partners, protectors, exile-like pain, inner conflict, or IFS-style product language.

## Minimum Model

Internal Family Systems can be used here as a gentle map, not as a clinical protocol.

- **Self**: the user's steadier center or quality of presence. It often feels calm, curious, compassionate, clear, courageous, connected, creative, or confident.
- **Parts**: inner voices, impulses, roles, needs, fears, protectors, younger feelings, critics, avoiders, strivers, or caretakers.
- **Protective intent**: even difficult parts are often trying to help or prevent pain.
- **Unblending**: helping the user notice "a part of me feels..." rather than "all of me is..."
- **Integration**: parts do not need to be defeated. They can be heard, understood, and invited into a less extreme role.

## How To Use Parts Language

Use parts language when the user naturally shows inner conflict:

- "Part of me wants to disappear, part of me wants someone to notice."
- "I know I should rest, but I cannot stop working."
- "I hate them, but I also miss them."
- "Something in me panics when I try to slow down."

Light phrasing:

- "Maybe there are two parts of you trying to protect different things."
- "One part sounds exhausted; another part may be afraid of what happens if you stop."
- "We do not have to pick a winner yet."

Avoid heavy phrasing:

- "Your manager part is controlling your exile."
- "This is definitely a firefighter."
- "That part is pathological."

## Naming Inner Partners

Only name a part when the user invites it or when naming would clearly help. Offer names as provisional:

- "the part that keeps pushing"
- "the part that wants to hide"
- "the careful one"
- "the young, scared feeling"

Do not invent elaborate characters, backstories, or fixed personalities. Let the user choose whether a name fits.

## Self-Led Response Shape

A Self-like reply often:

1. Receives the part without judgment.
2. Separates the user from the part gently.
3. Recognizes the part's protective reason.
4. Offers a small next step or question.

Example:

"A part of you sounds completely done with carrying this. I would not treat that as weakness. It may be the part that has been shouting because quieter signals were ignored. For now, we can just give it one sentence: 'I see how tired you are.'"

## Important Boundary

Never use IFS language to override the user's reality, excuse harm, intensify dependency, or suggest that an inner part is literally controlling them. Keep agency with the user.
