# Analytics & reporting 2026 — verified

*Volatile. Re-verify quarterly (benchmarks/metric names shift).*

## Vanity vs actionable (the core)
Most teams **track the wrong numbers** — celebrating 10k new followers while engagement drops 40%.
Only ~**4–6 KPIs actually correlate with revenue** for most businesses, yet the average dashboard shows
20+. **Vanity metrics** (total followers, total impressions, total likes) look impressive, are **easily
inflated/faked**, and don't map to outcomes. The **three tests** for a real metric: (1) Can it **inform
a decision**? (2) Does it **correlate with a goal/revenue**? (3) Is it **hard to manipulate**? **If a
metric won't change what you do next week, it's a diagnostic — not a headline KPI.** Pick **3–5 that
matter.**

## The KPI stack (map the funnel)
Four layers: **Distribution** (reach, impressions, frequency, follower-growth-rate — "did the algorithm
give you a chance") → **Attention** (avg watch time, completion rate, **saves per 1k reach**, **shares
per 1k reach** — "did the creative earn time") → **Action** (CTR, CVR, CPA, ROAS, RPM — "did it move
people") → **Risk** (fraud/authenticity/misleading-reporting guard). Map **goal → 1 primary KPI + ~2
supporting**, and fix the **measurement window** (7 / 28-day / campaign) so it's apples-to-apples.

## The signal metrics (2026, over likes)
- **Saves & shares** — better leading indicators than likes; signal **intent + relevance** (saves = "I'll
  return"; shares = sent to non-followers = discovery).
- **Video:** **average watch time, completion rate, audience retention, drop-off** (track in 25%
  increments; YouTube ~70% avg watch ≈ exceptional).
- **Engagement rate by reach** (not by followers, not raw totals) — accounts for algorithmic reach.
- **Follower growth RATE** `(new ÷ starting)×100` — resonance/health, not raw count.
- **CTR / link clicks** and **social-attributed conversions** (UTM + GA4) for traffic/sales.

## Platform-specific signal (optimise the right one)
Each platform rewards a different action — optimising the wrong one is wasted effort: **Instagram →
saves**; **TikTok → shares**; **LinkedIn → dwell time + saves**; **YouTube → watch time/retention**;
**Pinterest → outbound clicks/saves**; **X → replies/bookmarks**; **Facebook → saves/shares**.

## Benchmarks — relative, not absolute (verify-quarterly)
A "2% engagement rate" means nothing without context. **Benchmark vs your own trend over time and your
vertical/competitor set**, not platform averages. Rough 2026 "strong" ER ranges (vary widely): IG ~3–6%
brand, TikTok ~5–10%, LinkedIn ~2–4% personal / ~0.5–1.5% company. SaaS/B2B on visual platforms runs
lower — expected, not failure.

## Attribution + privacy (honest limits)
Cookieless era → **first-party data essential** (email, pixels, consented tracking). Use **UTM + GA4
conversion events**. **Assume social attribution undercounts true impact by ~30–50%** (last/first-touch
miss the journey) — read **directional** signals, not false precision.

## Reporting
**Flip the hierarchy:** lead with **business outcomes**, use content metrics to explain *why*. A monthly
report should take ~**30 min to compile / 10 to present** on a **standardized template**; its most
important section is **Recommendations — 3 specific next actions.** **Tier by cadence:** weekly pulse
(top posts, completion, best times → next batch) · monthly trends · quarterly strategy. **Don't overreact**
to one weak post or strong week. Set metric **definitions** early (they drift across platforms/agencies).
