# Jobs To Be Done — the lens that beats personas

Most audience research produces a demographic character sheet that never helps anyone write a
post. This is the lens that does: people don't follow accounts because of their age — they
follow, read, and buy to make **progress** in their lives. Capture the progress, and you know
what to make.

## What a "job" is

A job-to-be-done is the progress someone is trying to make in a given situation. They "hire"
content (and products) to get that job done. Jobs come in three layers, and the best content
hits more than one:

- **Functional job** — the practical task. *"Run payroll without messing up taxes."*
- **Emotional job** — how they want to feel. *"Stop feeling anxious and out of my depth."*
- **Social job** — how they want to be seen. *"Look competent to my team / not like an amateur."*

A payroll tool's audience isn't hiring "payroll software." They're hiring *"get this scary thing
off my plate so I can stop worrying and look like I have it together."* That sentence tells you
what to post; "35, urban, $80k income" does not.

## Why JTBD beats demographic personas

| Demographic persona | Jobs-to-be-done |
|---|---|
| "Marketing Mary, 35, likes coffee" | "Wants to prove content ROI to a skeptical boss" |
| Describes *who they are* | Describes *what they're trying to do* |
| Rarely changes the content | Directly generates content angles |
| Often invented | Grounded in real behavior |

Demographics matter only when they actually change the content (region for a local shop,
seniority for a B2B tool). Otherwise they're theater.

## The three things to capture around each job

- **Pains** — what blocks the job or makes it painful now. (The problem your content addresses.)
- **Desires** — the outcome they actually want. (The promise your content makes.)
- **Objections** — why they hesitate to act / believe / buy. (What content must answer
  pre-emptively.)

Every pain is a content idea. Every objection is a post that removes friction. Every desire is a
transformation you can show.

## How to find the jobs

- **The "what were they trying to do?" question** — for any review, comment, or sale, ask what
  progress they were after.
- **The before → after** — what state were they in before the brand, and after? The gap *is* the
  job.
- **The switch** — what made them seek a solution / start following? The trigger reveals the job.
- **Mine the evidence** — the voice-of-customer sources (`voice-of-customer.md`) are where jobs
  show up in the audience's own words.

## Turning jobs into content

Each job and its pains/objections is a content angle:

- Functional job → how-to / educational content.
- Emotional job → reassurance, relatable, story content.
- Social job → status, identity, "look smart sharing this" content.
- Objection → myth-busting, proof, FAQ content.

A content plan built from jobs is automatically relevant, because it's built from what the
audience is actually trying to do.
