# Content Mix — balance and variety

A feed people follow has a through-line and a rhythm. This is how you build balance into a batch
instead of producing 30 disconnected posts (or 30 ads).

A plan is organized along three axes: **pillars** (the themes), **intents** (the job each post
does), and **formats** (the shape). Vary all three across the period.

---

## Pillars — the themes (the *what*)

3–5 recurring themes the brand owns, drawn from `content-pillars` or derived from the brand
profile's positioning, audience, and point of view. Every post belongs to a pillar. Pillars
keep the feed coherent and make planning faster — you're filling known buckets, not inventing
from scratch each time.

Spread posts roughly evenly across pillars over the period so no single theme dominates (unless
a campaign justifies it).

## Intents — the job each post does (the *why*)

Tag every post with one primary intent:

- **Educate** — teach something useful (how-to, tips, breakdowns). High save value.
- **Entertain** — make them feel something / smile (relatable, story, behind-the-scenes). High
  reach/shareability.
- **Inspire** — show what's possible (transformation, vision, customer wins).
- **Connect** — build community (questions, opinions, shared identity, replies). High comments.
- **Promote** — ask for the action (launch, offer, CTA-forward).

**The ratio matters more than any single post.** Lead heavily with educate / entertain /
connect; keep **promote a clear minority** (a common working guideline is roughly 80% value &
connection, 20% promotion — adjust to the brand, but never invert it). A feed that only ever
asks gets muted. Give a lot; ask a little.

## Formats — the shape (rotate them)

Don't run the same format every day. Rotate across what the platforms and brand support:
single image, carousel, short video (Reel/TikTok/Short), text post, thread, story, behind-the-
scenes. Match format to intent — how-tos work as carousels; stories and entertainment as video;
hot takes as text. Rotating formats keeps the feed visually varied and reaches different
audience preferences.

Also rotate **hook mechanisms** (via `hook-writer`) so openings don't all sound the same.

## Recurring series — anchor the calendar

Build in one or two repeatable series — a weekly tip, a Friday behind-the-scenes, a monthly
customer story. Series:

- cut decision fatigue (the slot's purpose is pre-decided),
- build audience habit and recognition,
- make the calendar feel intentional, not random.

Leave the rest of the calendar for timely and one-off posts.

---

## The mix anti-patterns

- **All promotion** — the audience tunes out. Promotion rides on earned attention from value.
- **All one format** — monotonous; caps reach to one audience preference.
- **All one pillar** — narrow and repetitive; the brand looks one-note.
- **All educational, never human** — competent but cold; connection content is what builds
  loyalty.
- **No series / all random** — exhausting to sustain and forgettable to follow.

## A simple balanced week (illustrative)

For ~5 posts/week on one platform:

1. **Educate** (carousel) — pillar A
2. **Connect** (question) — pillar B
3. **Entertain / story** (video) — pillar C
4. **Inspire / proof** (image + caption) — pillar A
5. **Promote** (the week's single ask) — pillar D

Four give, one ask. Three formats. Four pillars. Scale the pattern up for a month; keep the
ratio.
