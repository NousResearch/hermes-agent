# The reality of before/after content in 2026 (verify-quarterly)

The FTC rules here are law, not trend — attribute them, and mark only the shifting enforcement details
verify-quarterly. The craft principles (the middle is the content; consistent conditions; honesty is the edge)
are stable.

## Why the format is so powerful — and so distrusted
- A before/after is **visual, built-in proof** and inherently satisfying (the reveal); it over-indexes on saves,
  shares, and completion.
- It's also the **single most abused and distrusted** format — staged befores, flattering-vs-unflattering
  lighting/posing, unrealistic timelines, undisclosed edits/filters/aids. Audiences are trained to be skeptical,
  which is exactly why **honesty is the competitive edge:** a believable transformation (real timeline, consistent
  conditions, the real messy journey) beats a suspiciously-perfect fake that trips the skepticism reflex.

## The FTC spine (this is law — attribute, verify-quarterly for enforcement)
Before/after photos + testimonials are, in the FTC's words, an enforcement "favorite." The rules (16 CFR Part 255
+ Health Products Compliance Guidance):
- **A before/after implies the result is *typical*.** Using it, you must either show typical results **or** clearly
  and conspicuously **disclose the actual typical/generally-expected results** — and have substantiation for them.
- **"Results not typical" / "results vary" is NOT enough.** If the result isn't typical, you must disclose the
  **actual** typical result. (FTC Gut Check.)
- **Use the median, not an outlier-skewed mean**, for the typical figure; **no survivorship** — you can't base
  "typical" only on those who stuck with it, ignoring quitters.
- **"Net impression" test:** visuals **alone** can be a deceptive claim — a lighting/posing/edit trick is itself a
  false claim even with no words.
- **"Actual consumers":** if it implies real customers, use real customers **or** disclose they're not (paid
  actors/stock). Fabricated/bogus endorsements are deceptive.
- **Health claims** need **competent and reliable scientific evidence** (RCTs); weight loss > ~1 lb/week over a
  long period is unusual, and "lose weight without effort" is deception.
- **Clear and conspicuous** = it stands out, sits close to the claim, and alters the net impression — not buried
  in hashtags/footnotes.
- **Live enforcement:** the FTC's 2025 NextMed action penalized a firm for paid-actor testimonials, before/after
  photos sourced off Craigslist, and an unsubstantiated "average 53 lbs" it didn't track. (attribute;
  verify-quarterly.)

## The craft edge (stable)
**The middle is the content** — endpoints alone are a flex; the process, setbacks, and real timeline build
relatability, credibility, and value. **Isolate the variables** (same angle/lighting/framing) so the change is
real. **Tie it to the viewer** — a relatable before + an attainable, honestly-framed after + an actionable
takeaway.

## House metrics
Saves/shares + watch-through + profile visits/inquiries — native only; never fabricate a result, timeline, or
average.
