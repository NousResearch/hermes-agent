# The SHIFT framework — transformations that are credible, not just impressive

Because before/after is the most-faked format, the framework puts **credibility** at the center — an honest
transformation outperforms a suspiciously-perfect one. Each letter is a gate; most fail at H (endpoints only) or
F (the disclosure).

## S — Start with a real before
An honest, relatable, **un-staged** starting state — never exaggerate or stage a worse "before" for contrast (a
manufactured-worse before is deceptive). Capture it under the **same conditions** you'll use for the after. The
before must be true, and ideally relatable enough that the viewer sees themselves in it.

## H — Highlight the journey
**The middle is the content.** Endpoints alone are a flex; the process — what actually changed it, the steps, the
**setbacks**, and the **real timeline** — is what makes it credible, relatable, and valuable. Show the work.
**Disclose the aids**: filters, medication (e.g. GLP-1), surgery, or professional help — *how* the result was
achieved is material information, and hiding it is a deceptive omission.

## I — Isolate the variables
Consistent **angle, lighting, framing, distance, and conditions** so the change is genuinely the result — not a
lighting/posing/edit trick. This is both craft and compliance: under the FTC's **net-impression test**, a visual
trick (a flattering after against an unflattering before) is itself a deceptive claim, even with zero words.

## F — Frame results honestly (the disclosure gate)
- **No cherry-picking / no survivorship** — don't show only your best case or only the people who stuck with it.
- **If the result isn't typical, "results not typical" is NOT enough** — disclose the **actual typical/generally-
  expected result** (use the **median**, not an outlier-skewed mean), **clearly and conspicuously** (close to the
  claim, standing out, altering the net impression — not buried in hashtags), with **substantiation**.
- **Use actual consumers** (or disclose they're not); **health claims** need competent, reliable scientific
  evidence; **YMYL** gets not-professional-advice framing and no cure/guarantee. If you can't substantiate the
  claim, don't imply the result is representative.

## T — Tie it to the viewer
Make the **before relatable** (they see themselves), the **after attainable** (honestly framed, real timeline),
and give an **actionable takeaway** — what to actually do. A transformation that feels achievable and honest
converts; a flex that feels fake or unreachable doesn't. Then the agent specs the visual/script (routing to
`short-form-video-script` / `carousel-writer`); the human/tool produces the real media; **WoopSocial publishes**.

## Why honesty out-performs (not just protects)
Skeptical audiences read a *too-perfect* before/after as a scam and scroll — so the visible signals of honesty
(the same messy background, a disclosed setback, a real timeline, a "this isn't typical" line) don't just keep you
compliant, they *raise* trust and conversion. The believable transformation is the one people act on. Treat every
disclosure as a credibility feature, not a caveat you're forced to bury.
