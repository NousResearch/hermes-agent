# The REVEAL framework — transparency that builds trust, not regret

Behind-the-scenes content works when it's true, specific, and deliberately bounded. Each letter is a gate; the
counterfeits fail at R (didn't happen) or A (performed), and the career-enders fail at V (shouldn't have left
the building).

## R — Root it in what actually happened
BTS material is surfaced, never invented: this week's real work, a decision you actually made, a mistake you
actually survived, a milestone you actually hit, a tool/process you actually use. Good prompts: *what did we
decide this week and what did it cost? · what broke? · what would a new hire be surprised by? · what does hour
two of the workday actually look like?* If you can't point at the moment on a calendar, it isn't behind the
scenes — it's fiction with a hoodie on.

## E — Expose the specifics + stakes
The gap between a press release and BTS is specificity and stakes.
- **Press release:** "Excited to share we shipped v2 of our onboarding!"
- **Behind the scenes:** "We rewrote onboarding twice. The first version tested great and tanked live — 43
  refunds in a week. Here's the decision that fixed it."
One real number, one real tradeoff, one thing it cost you. The "boring" middle — the spreadsheet, the failed
draft, the pricing debate — is exactly what the audience never gets to see anywhere else. That's the asset.

## V — Vet the transparency tier (the gate that keeps you employed and unsued)
Three tiers; the human makes the call, and the call is permanent:
- **Share freely:** process, decisions + reasoning, tools, lessons, resolved mistakes, culture-in-action,
  day-in-the-life, launches and the mess behind them.
- **Handle with care (deliberate, owned choice):** revenue/MRR (once you start, stopping reads as bad news),
  pricing logic, ongoing struggles (share from a stable footing, not mid-freefall), personal health/family.
- **Never:** customer data or identifiable customer stories without explicit consent · team conflicts,
  performance issues, firings, or anything about a named/identifiable employee · active legal, regulatory, or
  dispute matters · security, credentials, or exploitable operational detail · numbers you're contractually or
  legally bound to keep private.
**The permanence test:** would you be fine with this post on screen in a lawsuit, a hiring call, and your
biggest customer's inbox? All three, or it doesn't ship. Regulated industries (finance/health/legal): revenue
and results claims go through compliance first.

## E — Establish whose voice
- **Founder-voice:** first person, from the personal account, owns "I", includes the doubt and the decision.
  This is where the reach and trust live — but only if it's actually the founder's voice and feelings. The agent
  drafts in that voice; it never ghost-writes emotions the founder doesn't have.
- **Brand-voice:** the company account curates — "we", team culture, process, how-it's-made. Warmer than a press
  release, but it doesn't perform intimacy it can't have.
Pick one per piece. A brand account doing first-person confession is as off as a founder doing corporate-speak.

## A — Avoid the counterfeits
- **Manufactured vulnerability:** the engineered tear-jerker ("I cried in my car. Then I closed the deal.") —
  the genre's defining cringe; audiences smell it and trust dies (`linkedin-post-writer`'s avoiding-cringe file
  is the calibration).
- **The humble-brag:** a "mistake" that's secretly a flex ("my biggest flaw? I care too much"). A real mistake
  post names what it cost and what you'd do differently.
- **Struggle-porn:** raw pain shared for reach while you're still inside it. Share struggles from a stable
  footing, with a lesson or an honest "here's where we are" — not as a live breakdown.
- **The fabricated parable:** the homeless-man-taught-me-marketing genre. If it didn't happen, it doesn't post.

## L — Loop it into a cadence
Build-in-public is a rhythm, not a stunt. Commit to a sustainable cadence (weekly or biweekly beats daily-then-
vanish), decide the recurring beats up front (metrics update, decision log, lesson, ship note), and **share the
middle, not just wins** — an all-wins feed is just marketing with extra steps. Serialize into the
`content-calendar`, route cuts to the format writers (`caption-writer`, `linkedin-post-writer`,
`short-form-video-script`, `story-writer`, `thread-writer`), publish via `scheduling-and-queue` → WoopSocial,
and have the founder show up in the replies — the comments are where the trust actually compounds.

## The gut check before you post
Three questions: (1) Did this actually happen, exactly as written? (2) Does it pass the permanence test —
lawsuit, hiring call, customer's inbox? (3) Am I sharing this because it's useful/true, or performing it for
reach? If any answer is wrong, fix the reveal — don't polish the performance.
