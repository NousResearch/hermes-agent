# Audience

The goal is not a demographic table. It's to know **who the content is for**, what they
already believe, what they want, and where they pay attention — sharply enough that a skill
can write something that stops *them* specifically.

## Capture one or two segments, not "everyone"

Social rewards specificity. A post written for everyone lands with no one. If the user
insists on a broad audience, push for the **one segment that moves the business most right
now**, and build for them. You can add segments later.

For each segment, capture:

- **Who they are** — role/identity in plain terms ("ops leads at 50–500-person companies,"
  "new parents in the first year," "indie game devs"). Demographics only if they actually
  change the content.
- **What they care about** — their goals, the outcome they want, what keeps them up at night.
- **What they already believe** — their current mental model, so content can meet them there
  or challenge it deliberately.
- **Objections / friction** — why they hesitate, what they're skeptical of. Great content
  answers objections before they're voiced.
- **Where they are** — which platforms, and in what mindset on each.
- **Sophistication level** — beginner vs expert changes vocabulary, depth, and how much you
  explain.

## Buyer vs follower — especially in B2B

The person who *buys* and the person who *follows and shares on social* are often different.
A CFO approves the purchase; the finance analyst follows the brand, shares the post, and
champions it internally. Capture both when they differ, and note which one a given content
pillar is really for. Writing only to the economic buyer makes for dry, unshareable social.

## Jobs to be done (a sharper lens than personas)

Often more useful than a persona: what "job" is someone hiring this brand's content to do?

- To learn something that makes them better at their work.
- To feel seen / less alone in a struggle.
- To be entertained in a dead minute.
- To look smart by sharing it.
- To decide whether to buy.

Knowing the job tells a skill what *shape* the content should take, not just the topic.

## The transformation

In one line: where is this audience *before* the brand, and where are they *after*? This
before→after is the spine of most high-performing content and the thing many profiles miss.

## By business type

- **B2B:** separate buyer and practitioner; lead with the practitioner for social reach.
- **Ecommerce / DTC:** segment by use-case or moment (gifting vs self-purchase; the problem
  the product solves) more than by demographic.
- **Local / SMB:** geography and community are the segmentation; "people within 5 miles who
  care about X."
- **Creator / personal brand:** the audience is whoever resonates with the person; capture the
  specific kind of person who's "their people."
- **Nonprofit:** often *two* audiences — beneficiaries and donors/supporters — with very
  different content needs. Note both.

## Don't over-build

Two sharp segments beat eight vague ones. The test: could a writer who has never met this
audience picture a real person and write to them? If yes, you have enough. If no, ask one
more question.

## Keep it alive — the profile is a snapshot, the audience is a stream

This section captures today's best understanding; the loop keeps it true. Route ongoing
signals back here: `audience-research` for deeper personas, `interactive-content` (every poll
is a micro-survey), `community-management` (what the human hears in comments and DMs), and
`idea-generation-and-ideation` (clustered audience questions). When the signals contradict
the profile, the profile updates — not the other way around.
