# Examples — filled profiles across six business types

These are abbreviated, realistic profiles to ground the agent. They show the *level of
specificity* to aim for. They are illustrative, not real companies. Notice that every one has
a concrete lexicon, a real point of view, and specific proof — never adjectives alone.

---

## 1. B2B SaaS — payroll tool

- **Identity:** Payroll software for small businesses that runs payroll in under five minutes.
- **What's different:** The only payroll tool built for owners who do it themselves, not for
  HR departments.
- **Point of view:** Payroll shouldn't require a specialist. Most tools are bloated because
  they're sold to enterprises; small businesses pay for complexity they'll never use.
- **Audience:** Owner-operators of 2–30-person businesses (buyer + follower are the same
  person). Cares about: not screwing up taxes, getting time back. Believes payroll is scary.
- **Voice:** Formality 2/5 · Authority 4/5 · Humor 3/5. *Confident, never preachy. Plain,
  never dumbed-down.* Bans: "solution," "seamless," "empower." Uses: "run payroll," "owners,"
  "the IRS won't email you — we will."
- **Proof:** 40,000 businesses; "set up in 11 minutes on average"; SOC 2.
- **Guardrails:** No tax *advice* (point to a CPA); never promise specific tax outcomes.

## 2. Ecommerce / DTC — sustainable sneakers

- **Identity:** Sneakers made from recycled ocean plastic, shipped carbon-neutral.
- **What's different:** Fashion-first sustainability — they look good *first*, the eco story
  is the bonus, not the apology.
- **Point of view:** Sustainable products fail when they ask you to sacrifice. Ours don't.
- **Audience:** Design-conscious 25–40s who'd buy them even without the eco angle. Job:
  look good + feel good about it.
- **Voice:** Warmth 4/5 · Energy 4/5 · Polish 4/5. *Playful, never preachy; confident, never
  smug.* Emoji: sparingly, on-brand 🌊👟. Uses: "drop," "colorway," "every pair = 12 bottles."
- **Proof:** 2M bottles recycled; 4.8★ from 30k reviews; featured in {press}.
- **Guardrails:** No overstated eco claims (greenwashing risk); cite the real numbers only.

## 3. Local / SMB — neighborhood coffee shop

- **Identity:** A third-wave café and roastery on Oak Street, owner-run since 2019.
- **What's different:** They roast in-house and know regulars by name.
- **Point of view:** Coffee is a daily ritual, not a status symbol. Good coffee shouldn't be
  intimidating.
- **Audience:** People within ~3 miles; remote workers, students, weekend families.
- **Voice:** Warmth 5/5 · Formality 1/5 · Polish 2/5. *Warm and human; the owner's real
  voice.* Lowercase, friendly, lots of behind-the-counter moments. Uses first names.
- **Proof:** community-led; regulars; local press; the roast itself.
- **Guardrails:** keep it personal and local; avoid corporate-café tone entirely.

## 4. Professional services — boutique financial advisory

- **Identity:** A fee-only advisory firm helping tech employees manage equity comp.
- **What's different:** Specialists in RSUs/options, not generalist wealth managers.
- **Point of view:** Most advice is generic because most advisors are generalists; equity comp
  needs a specialist.
- **Audience:** Mid-career tech employees with significant equity; smart, skeptical,
  time-poor.
- **Voice:** Authority 5/5 · Warmth 3/5 · Humor 1/5. *Authoritative, never stiff;
  trustworthy, never salesy.* Bans hype and FOMO language.
- **Proof:** credentials (CFP), years, anonymized client outcomes.
- **Guardrails (critical):** No specific investment advice in posts; required disclaimers; no
  performance promises; compliance review before publishing. Record all of this so every
  downstream skill respects it.

## 5. Personal brand / creator — indie maker

- **Identity:** A solo founder building in public, sharing what works and what doesn't.
- **What's different:** Radical transparency — real revenue, real failures.
- **Point of view:** Most "founder content" is performative; honesty is the moat.
- **Audience:** Aspiring and early indie founders; "their people" are builders, not hustle-
  culture fans.
- **Voice:** the *person's* actual voice — dry, self-deprecating, specific. First person
  always. Boundaries: shares business numbers, not family.
- **Proof:** the journey itself; real MRR screenshots; shipped projects.
- **Guardrails:** stay honest; don't fake metrics; keep family/private life off-limits.

## 6. Nonprofit — youth literacy

- **Identity:** A nonprofit getting books into the hands of kids in under-resourced schools.
- **What's different:** Books chosen *by* the kids, not for them.
- **Point of view:** Kids read more when they choose; "what's good for them" misses the point.
- **Audiences (two):** (a) supporters/donors — want to see impact; (b) educators/partners.
- **Voice:** Warmth 5/5 · Authority 3/5. *Hopeful, never saccharine; dignified, never pity-
  driven.* Lead with kids' agency and concrete impact, not sad statistics.
- **Proof:** books distributed, schools served, reading-gain data, kid quotes.
- **Guardrails:** dignity of the kids; consent for any photos/names; impact over pity.

---

**The pattern across all six:** a one-sentence identity, a real difference, a defensible
point of view, two-or-fewer sharp audiences, voice captured as dimensions + lexicon + a
"sound ___ never ___" line, concrete proof, and guardrails that would actually stop a bad
post. Aim for this level every time, whatever the business.
