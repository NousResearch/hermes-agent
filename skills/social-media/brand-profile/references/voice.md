# Voice

"Brand voice" is where most profiles go generic. "Professional but approachable" describes
roughly every company on earth and tells a downstream skill nothing. This reference makes
voice **concrete and reusable** so any skill can write on-voice without guessing.

**Boundary:** this reference captures voice **by interview**. If the user has a body of
existing writing (posts, emails, docs), hand off to `voice-builder` to derive the voice from
that corpus, then merge the result here. The profile *stores* the voice; `voice-builder`
*derives* it; `writing-style-and-tone` *applies* it per piece and per moment.

Voice has four capturable parts: **dimensions** (the personality), **lexicon** (the words),
**signature devices** (the mechanics), and **context shifts** (how it flexes by platform).

---

## 1. Dimensions — place the brand on each spectrum

Don't ask "what's your voice?" Ask the user to place the brand on each spectrum below, and to
say *where it shouldn't go*. Record a value plus a one-line note. These six cover almost every
real brand:

1. **Formality** — Casual ‹———› Formal
2. **Warmth** — Matter-of-fact ‹———› Warm & personal
3. **Authority** — Peer / "one of us" ‹———› Expert / authoritative
4. **Energy** — Calm & measured ‹———› Bold & high-energy
5. **Humor** — Serious throughout ‹———› Playful & funny
6. **Polish** — Plainspoken & raw ‹———› Polished & crafted

Two techniques that get honest answers:

- **The two-brand test.** "Name a brand whose social you'd love to sound like, and one you'd
  hate to sound like." The contrast locates the brand faster than any scale.
- **The "we sound ___, never ___" sentence.** Force one for each dimension that matters:
  "We sound confident, never arrogant." "Warm, never cutesy." These pairs are gold for
  downstream skills because they encode the *edge* — the failure mode to avoid.

> A point on a slider is only useful with its guardrail. "Funny" without "never at a
> customer's expense" produces posts that get screenshotted for the wrong reasons.

---

## 2. Lexicon — the actual words

This is the single most useful thing you can capture. Collect:

- **Words & phrases we use** — the brand's natural vocabulary, signature phrases, how it
  refers to its product/customers/category. (e.g., "members" not "users"; "ship" not
  "release".)
- **Words & phrases we ban** — jargon, clichés, hype words, anything off-brand. Almost every
  brand should ban a few: "synergy," "game-changer," "revolutionary," "unlock," "in today's
  fast-paced world," "we're thrilled to announce," "🚀 to the moon." Ask which make them wince.
  For the 2026 AI-tell vocabulary (the delve-class words that read as machine-written), don't
  duplicate a list here — apply `writing-style-and-tone`'s tell cluster at edit time.
- **How we say the hard things** — how the brand handles a price increase, an outage, a
  competitor, a mistake. Tone under pressure reveals real voice.
- **Spelling/style choices** — title case vs sentence case, Oxford comma, em-dash habits,
  numerals vs words, regional spelling.

---

## 3. Signature devices — the mechanics that make it recognizable

- **Hooks/openers** — does it open with a question, a bold claim, a number, a story? Any
  recurring pattern.
- **Sentence rhythm** — short and punchy? long and flowing? a mix? One-line paragraphs?
- **Emoji policy** — none / sparingly / liberally, and which ones are on-brand (and which are
  banned).
- **Hashtag policy** — none / a few branded / many. Where they go.
- **Punctuation tics** — em-dashes, ellipses, ALL-CAPS for emphasis, lowercase-everything.
- **Formatting** — line breaks, lists, the "hook → space → body" structure on LinkedIn, etc.

---

## 4. Context shifts — voice flexes by platform

Voice is consistent; register changes. Capture the deltas so skills don't write LinkedIn
posts in TikTok's voice:

- **LinkedIn / X-thoughtful:** usually a notch more formal, more authority.
- **TikTok / Reels / Shorts:** faster, looser, more playful, sound-on energy.
- **Instagram:** visual-led, warmer, lifestyle register.
- **Threads / X-casual:** conversational, reactive, opinionated.
- **Reddit / community (advisory only — not a WoopSocial publishing platform):** drop the
  marketing voice entirely; be a helpful peer or don't post.

Record only the shifts that matter for the platforms this brand actually uses.

## How voice differs by business type (quick guidance)

- **B2B SaaS:** authority + plainspoken usually wins; ban hype; let a strong POV carry
  personality. A named human (founder) voice often outperforms the logo voice — 2026 trend
  research (Hootsuite's Social Media Trends report) finds audiences trust employees and
  founders more than faceless brand accounts (attribute; verify-quarterly).
- **Ecommerce / DTC:** warmth + energy; product-as-hero; voice often playful and benefit-led.
- **Local / SMB:** the owner's real human voice; community and place; low polish is an asset.
- **Personal brand / creator:** maximum specificity — it's literally one person. Capture their
  actual speech patterns and boundaries.
- **Professional services (legal, finance, health):** authority + warmth, tightly bounded by
  compliance; "trustworthy, never stiff" is a common target.
- **Nonprofit:** warmth + clarity; dignity over pity; concrete impact over abstraction.

## The "show, don't tell" capture (do this for every brand)

Adjectives drift; examples don't. Collect:

- **3–5 posts the brand loves** (theirs or others'), each with one line: *what* they love
  about it.
- **1–2 posts they hate**, with *why*.

Store these in the profile. Downstream skills pattern-match against real examples far more
reliably than against a slider value. If the brand has no posts yet, use admired accounts as
references and label them as aspirational.
