# Examples — captions across platforms and goals

These show the method in action: same brand, adapted properly per platform and goal — never
copy-pasted. Voice here is the blunt indie-founder voice from the voice-builder examples
(short sentences, no exclamation marks, self-deprecating, lands on an imperative). Your output
uses the actual brand's `voice.md`.

Asset for examples 1–3: a screenshot of a feature being deleted from the product.

---

## Example 1 — Instagram · goal: saves · format: value/story

> Deleted our most-requested feature today.
>
> Nobody used it. We just kept hearing about it in sales calls, so we built it. Six months,
> gone in one commit.
>
> What we learned, so you don't repeat it:
> – "Requested" isn't "used." Watch behavior, not surveys.
> – Build the boring thing people already do by hand.
> – Shipping less is a feature.
>
> Save this before your next roadmap meeting.

*First line wins the tap inside ~125 chars. Body pays off the hook. One CTA (save). On-voice:
short, no exclamation marks, dry. ~3 relevant hashtags would go at the end.*

---

## Example 2 — LinkedIn · goal: comments · format: hot take

> "Requested" is the most expensive word in product.
>
> We spent six months building a feature customers asked for in every sales call. We deleted it
> today. Nobody used it.
>
> The ask was real. The need wasn't. People will tell you what sounds good and do something
> else entirely — and if you build for the words instead of the behavior, you'll ship beautiful
> things nobody touches.
>
> We're going to watch what people *do* now, not what they *say*.
>
> Curious if I'm wrong here — does anyone actually build straight from feature requests and have
> it work?

*Hook lands inside the ~140-char cutoff. Whitespace for dwell. Ends on a genuine question (the
comments goal). Link, if any, goes in comments.*

---

## Example 3 — X · goal: reach · format: contrarian (the post IS the caption)

> "Customers requested it" is how you justify six months of building something nobody uses.
>
> Deleted ours today. Watching behavior from now on.

*Two lines, the whole point up front. No hashtags. Punchy. Same idea, stripped to the platform.*

## Example 4 — same goal, two formats (Instagram · goal: comments)

Asset: a photo of a messy desk.

**Option A — question format**
> 14 tabs open to avoid starting the one task that matters.
>
> Tell me I'm not the only one. What's your version of "productive procrastination"?

**Option B — relatable/confession format**
> My productivity system is opening more tabs until the real task feels less scary.
>
> It does not work. I do it every day anyway.

*Recommend A if the goal is comments (it ends on an easy question); B if the goal is reach (more
relatable, more shareable). Both inside the cutoff, both on-voice. Showing two real alternatives
beats one take.*

---

## What every example shares

- The **first line** works alone, inside the cutoff.
- The body **pays off** the hook — no bait-and-switch.
- **One** CTA, matched to the goal.
- Mechanics (hashtags, links, length) fit the **platform**.
- It **sounds like the brand** — fingerprint hit, "never" list respected.

If your draft misses any of these, fix the first line first, then the rest.
