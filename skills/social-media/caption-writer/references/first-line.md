# The First Line — winning the "…more" tap

Every caption is truncated. The reader sees one line and a "…more." If that line doesn't earn
the tap, nothing else you wrote exists. This is where most captions are won or lost, so spend
disproportionate effort here: draft several, keep the best.

> Rule: the first line must create a gap the reader needs to close — curiosity, tension,
> recognition, or stakes — **within the platform's preview cutoff** (≈125 chars on Instagram,
> ≈140 on LinkedIn, even shorter on Facebook). If the hook lands after the cutoff, it doesn't
> exist.

For deeper hook work across formats, `hook-writer` goes further; this is the caption-opening
essentials.

---

## What a first line must do

Do exactly one of these, fast:

- **Open a curiosity gap** — promise an answer you make them tap for.
- **Make a bold or contrarian claim** — a position they need to see defended.
- **Name a specific, relatable moment** — recognition pulls people in.
- **Raise the stakes** — a number, a loss, a deadline, a consequence.
- **Start mid-story** — drop into a scene already in motion.

It must NOT:

- Greet ("Hey everyone!", "Happy Monday!").
- Describe the image ("Here's a photo of our team at…").
- Lead with hashtags or @mentions.
- Bury the point after throat-clearing ("So I've been thinking lately about…").

---

## Patterns that earn the tap

Rewrite these in the brand's voice; they're shapes, not scripts.

- **The number that shouldn't be:** "We deleted 80% of our features. Revenue went up."
- **The confession:** "I've been doing ___ wrong for three years."
- **The contrarian flag:** "Hot take: ___ is a waste of time."
- **The specific scene:** "It's 11pm and I'm reformatting a spreadsheet for the fourth time."
- **The open loop:** "The reason this post is doing numbers has nothing to do with the photo."
- **The stakes:** "This mistake cost us a customer. Don't make it."
- **The direct promise:** "How we ___ in ___ days — the actual steps."
- **The pattern interrupt:** a line that contradicts what they expect from the visual.

---

## Test your first line

Read only the first line (cut at the platform's preview length) and ask:

1. **Would a stranger need the next line?** If they'd shrug, rewrite.
2. **Does the gap close too early?** If line one gives away the payoff, there's no reason to
   tap. Hold something back.
3. **Does it sound like them?** A hooky line in the wrong voice still fails. Check the
   fingerprint.
4. **Is it inside the cutoff?** Front-load the tension; don't let it land at character 200.

---

## Drafting method

1. Write the caption body first if it helps you find the point.
2. Then write **5 different first lines** using different patterns above.
3. Cut each to the platform's preview length and read them cold.
4. Keep the one that most makes you need line two. Discard the rest without mercy.

The first line deserves as much editing as the entire rest of the caption combined.
