# Formats — caption archetypes

A format gives a caption a proven shape. Pick the one that fits the asset, the point, and the
goal — then write it in the brand's voice. Don't default to the same format every time; variety
is what keeps a feed from feeling like a template.

For each: when to use it, its shape, and a compressed example (write the real thing in voice).

---

## 1. Story / micro-narrative
**Use when:** you have a real moment, lesson, or before→after. Highest trust and shareability.
**Shape:** drop into the moment → tension → turn → lesson/payoff.
**Example skeleton:** "Lost our biggest client on a Tuesday. Here's what it taught me about ___."
**Goal fit:** awareness, community, conversion (via trust).

## 2. Value / how-to
**Use when:** you can teach something useful in a few lines. The most "save-able" format.
**Shape:** promise the outcome → deliver 3–5 tight, concrete steps/points → CTA to save.
**Example skeleton:** "How to ___ in 4 steps: 1) … 2) … 3) … 4) … Save this for next time."
**Goal fit:** saves, authority.

## 3. Hot take / contrarian
**Use when:** the brand has a real point of view (from brand-profile). Drives comments.
**Shape:** state the unpopular position plainly → back it in 2–3 lines → invite disagreement.
**Example skeleton:** "Posting daily is overrated. Here's what actually grew our account → ___."
**Goal fit:** comments, reach. Stay on-brand; never manufacture outrage.

## 4. Question / fill-in-the-blank
**Use when:** the goal is comments and the topic invites opinion or identity.
**Shape:** quick setup → one sharp, easy-to-answer question.
**Example skeleton:** "Which one are you — ___ or ___? (No wrong answers.)"
**Goal fit:** comments, community. Keep the question low-effort to answer.

## 5. Behind-the-scenes
**Use when:** humanizing the brand, showing process, building familiarity.
**Shape:** "here's the part you don't usually see" → the real detail → why it matters.
**Goal fit:** community, trust. Especially strong for personal and local brands.

## 6. Social proof / testimonial
**Use when:** you have a real result, review, or customer story.
**Shape:** the result up front (number/quote) → brief context → soft CTA.
**Example skeleton:** "'___' — said a customer who ___. Here's what we did → ___."
**Goal fit:** conversion, trust. Use only verifiable proof per the brand profile.

## 7. Announcement / launch
**Use when:** something is genuinely new. Lead with the benefit, not the fanfare.
**Shape:** the thing + why it matters to *them* → one clear next step.
**Avoid:** "We're thrilled to announce…" (banned in most voice profiles). Lead with the value.
**Goal fit:** awareness, conversion.

## 8. Relatable / "it's just me?"
**Use when:** a shared frustration or moment your audience will recognize.
**Shape:** name the specific, slightly oddly-specific experience → land the feeling.
**Example skeleton:** "Opening 14 tabs to avoid starting the one task. Just me?"
**Goal fit:** reach, community. The specificity is what makes it travel.

## 9. List / listicle
**Use when:** multiple quick points; great for carousels and saves.
**Shape:** promise → numbered/■ items, one line each → CTA to save.
**Goal fit:** saves, authority.

## 10. Carousel / swipe intro
**Use when:** the asset is a carousel. The caption's job is to pull people into slide 1.
**Shape:** hook that promises what the swipe delivers → "→" / "keep reading" only if on-voice →
the payoff lives in the slides; the caption sets it up.
**Goal fit:** saves, dwell.

---

## Choosing a format

- **Goal = saves** → value/how-to or list.
- **Goal = comments** → hot take or question.
- **Goal = reach** → story or relatable.
- **Goal = conversion** → social proof or announcement (benefit-led).
- **Goal = community** → behind-the-scenes or question.

When unsure, the **story** and **value** formats are the safest high-performers across almost
every brand. Rotate formats over a content calendar so the feed stays varied.
