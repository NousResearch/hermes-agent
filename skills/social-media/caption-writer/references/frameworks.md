# Frameworks — structure, goal, length, CTA

Format gives a caption a recognizable shape; this is the engine underneath — the structure,
the goal that drives it, the right length, and the call to action.

---

## The caption spine

Almost every effective caption is some version of:

**Hook (first line) → Body (deliver the promise) → CTA (one action).**

- **Hook** earns the "…more" tap. It is the single highest-leverage line. See
  `first-line.md`.
- **Body** pays off what the hook promised — the value, the story, the point. Don't bait and
  switch; the fastest way to lose trust is a great hook with a hollow body.
- **CTA** points to one action that serves the goal.

The spine is the default. Formats (`formats.md`) are variations on it.

---

## Goal drives structure

A caption "for engagement" has no goal. Pick one, and let it shape the structure and CTA:

| Goal | What the caption optimizes for | Structure emphasis | CTA shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness / reach** | stop the scroll, be shareable | strong hook, tight payoff, relatable | "send this to someone who…", "share if…" |
| **Comments** | provoke a reply | open loop, opinion, or a genuine question | "what would you add?", "agree or no?" |
| **Saves** | be a reference worth keeping | value/how-to, listy, useful | "save this for later / your next ___" |
| **Clicks / traffic** | move off-platform | tease value, withhold the full payoff | "full breakdown in bio / link below" |
| **Community** | deepen belonging | shared identity, story, in-jokes | "tell me I'm not the only one…", "drop a ___" |
| **Conversion** | drive a buy/sign-up | benefit + proof + low-friction ask | "try it free", "grab yours" (respect guardrails) |

Match the CTA to the goal *and* the platform (no clickable-link CTA where links aren't
clickable). One CTA only.

---

## Length: match it to platform and goal

Short isn't a virtue; *appropriate* is. Decide length from platform mechanics + goal:

- **Short (1–3 lines):** X, Threads, Bluesky; TikTok captions; awareness goals; punchy takes.
- **Medium (3–6 short paragraphs):** Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook; comment and save goals;
  most value content.
- **Long (mini-essay):** LinkedIn and Instagram storytelling, when the first line earns it and
  the payoff justifies the scroll. Long works only if every line pulls to the next.

When in doubt, write it long, then cut every line that doesn't move the reader forward. Brevity
earned by editing reads better than brevity from having nothing to say.

## The CTA library

Pick one, in the brand's voice, matched to the goal. These are shapes, not scripts — rewrite
in voice:

**Comments:** "What would you add?" · "Which one are you?" · "Unpopular opinion — am I wrong?"
· "Finish this sentence: ___"
**Saves:** "Save this for your next ___." · "Bookmark before it scrolls away." · "Steal this
checklist."
**Shares / reach:** "Send this to someone who needs it." · "Tag the person who does this."
**Traffic:** "Full guide — link in bio." · "Watch the whole thing (link below)." · "We broke
it all down — link in comments."
**Community:** "Tell me I'm not the only one." · "Drop a 🙋 if this is you." (only if emoji fits
voice) · "What's your version?"
**Conversion:** "Try it free." · "Start today — link in bio." · "Grab one before they're gone."
(only where honest and on-brand)
**Soft / no-ask:** sometimes the strongest close is a resonant last line, not a CTA — common in
high-authority and personal-brand voices. A caption can end on a mic-drop and earn comments
anyway.

> One CTA. Two asks split attention and both lose. If the goal needs two things, you have two
> posts.

---

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Wasting line one on "Happy Monday!", a greeting, or describing the photo.
- Stacking hashtags at the top where the hook should be.
- Two or three CTAs competing.
- A great hook with a thin or off-topic body (bait-and-switch).
- Emoji or buzzwords the voice profile bans.
- The same caption pasted across every platform.
