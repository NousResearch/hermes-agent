# Platform Mechanics

Caption rules differ on every platform: how much shows before the cutoff, how long you can go,
whether hashtags or links help or hurt, and what the feed rewards. Get these wrong and a good
caption underperforms.

> **Verify before relying on exact numbers.** Platforms change limits and algorithm behavior
> often; these are accurate as of build and should be re-checked quarterly. Treat the
> *principles* as durable and the *numbers* as approximate.

---

## Instagram (feed / Reels)

- **Caption limit:** ~2,200 characters.
- **Preview cutoff:** ~125 characters before "…more." The first line/sentence must hook within
  that.
- **Hashtags:** up to 30 allowed, but more ≠ more reach. Instagram has said hashtags help
  *categorize*, not boost reach — use a handful (≈3–5) of genuinely relevant ones. In caption
  or first comment both work; pick one and stay consistent.
- **Links:** not clickable in captions → route to "link in bio." Don't write a clickable-link
  CTA.
- **Rewards:** saves and shares (sends) are the strongest signals; comments next. Write toward
  "save this" / "send this to someone."
- **Length:** flexible — short punchy or long story both work if the first line earns it. Use
  line breaks for readability.

## LinkedIn

- **Limit:** ~3,000 characters.
- **Preview cutoff:** ~140 characters on mobile before "…see more" (a bit more on desktop). The
  hook line is critical; the "…see more" tap is the engagement you're fighting for.
- **Hashtags:** ~3–5 relevant ones, usually at the end.
- **Links:** external links in the post body can suppress reach; a common tactic is to put the
  link in the first comment and say "link in comments." Use judgment per the brand.
- **Rewards:** dwell time and early comments. Whitespace and one-line paragraphs increase
  read-through. Posts that spark genuine discussion travel.
- **Length:** medium-to-long performs well when it earns the read; format with generous line
  breaks.
- Note: for dedicated thought-leadership posts, hand off to `linkedin-post-writer`.

## TikTok

- **Caption limit:** up to ~4,000 characters (expanded from 2,200; verify).
- **Preview:** only a short portion shows over the video; lead with the hook or a curiosity
  gap.
- **Hashtags & keywords:** relevant hashtags help categorization; **keywords in the caption
  feed TikTok search/SEO** — write for what people type, not just tags. A couple of specific
  tags beat a pile of broad ones.
- **Links:** not clickable in captions → "link in bio."
- **Rewards:** watch time/rewatches (the video does this) and comments. Captions that pose a
  question or set up the video's payoff drive comments.
- **Length:** usually short; the video carries the message. Use the caption to add context,
  a hook, or a question.

## X / Twitter

- **Limit:** 280 characters (free); much longer with Premium, but short still travels.
- **Hashtags:** 0–2, often none. More than a couple reads as spam and can hurt.
- **Links:** fine; some believe links dampen reach (debated) — lead with the point, link after.
- **Rewards:** replies, reposts, and bookmarks. Lead with the strongest line; the post *is* the
  caption.
- **Length:** short and punchy. Cut everything that isn't the point. For multi-post threads,
  hand off to `thread-writer`.

## Facebook

- **Limit:** very long (~63,206 characters) — irrelevant in practice.
- **Preview cutoff:** ~80 characters before "See more." Hook fast.
- **Hashtags:** largely ineffective; skip or use sparingly.
- **Links:** clickable and fine.
- **Rewards:** comments and shares; meaningful interactions. Often an older audience — adjust
  references accordingly.
- **Length:** short-to-medium; conversational.

## Threads

- **Limit:** ~500 characters per post.
- **Tags:** one topic tag per post (not multi-hashtag culture). Keep it conversational.
- **Links:** clickable.
- **Rewards:** replies and conversation; casual, reactive, opinion-friendly.
- **Length:** short and conversational; lead with the take.

## Pinterest

- **Pin description:** ~500 characters.
- **SEO is the game:** keywords in the title and description drive discovery far more than
  hashtags (which are now minor). Write descriptive, keyword-rich copy people would search.
- **Links:** clickable — Pinterest is a genuine traffic driver; the CTA can point to the link.
- **Rewards:** saves and outbound clicks. Write toward "save for later" and clear destination
  value.

## YouTube (descriptions / community posts)

- **Description limit:** ~5,000 characters.
- **Preview:** first 2–3 lines (~100 chars) show above "…more" — put the hook and key link/
  keywords there.
- **Keywords:** the description aids search/SEO; front-load the important terms naturally.
- **Links:** clickable.
- **Community posts:** short, conversational, image/poll-friendly.

## Bluesky

- **Limit:** ~300 characters.
- **Hashtags:** supported; a few relevant ones.
- **Links:** clickable.
- **Rewards:** replies and reposts; casual, community-forward, less algorithmic. Lead with the
  point.

| LinkedIn | ~3,000 | ~140 ch | ~3–5 | Reach risk → comments |
| TikTok | ~4,000 | short | few + keywords | No (bio) |
| X | 280 (free) | n/a | 0–2 | Yes |
| Facebook | huge | ~80 ch | skip | Yes |
| Threads | ~500 | short | 1 tag | Yes |
