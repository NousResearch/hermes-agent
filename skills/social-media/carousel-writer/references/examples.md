# Examples — carousels slide by slide

Worked carousels showing the spine, the slide text, and the design/image direction — plus a
text-dump version for contrast. Voice is the blunt indie-founder voice from earlier skills (short,
dry, no exclamation marks, concrete). Your output uses the loaded `voice.md`.

---

## Example 1 — How-to carousel (Instagram, 4:5) · goal: saves

**Spine:** cover promises 5 lessons from deleting a feature → one lesson per slide → payoff → save.

- **Slide 1 (cover):** "We deleted our most-requested feature. 5 lessons so you don't waste 6 months →"
- **Slide 2:** "1. 'Requested' isn't 'used.' / Watch behavior, not surveys."
- **Slide 3:** "2. The boring thing wins. / Our dull export button beat the big feature."
- **Slide 4:** "3. Sales calls aren't a roadmap. / Prospects describe what closes *them*."
- **Slide 5:** "4. Every feature is forever. / Docs, bugs, support, clutter. Deleting helped."
- **Slide 6:** "5. We built to feel productive, not to be useful. / Now: behavior first."
- **Slide 7 (payoff):** "Build less. Watch more. Ship the boring thing."
- **Slide 8 (CTA):** "Save this for your next roadmap meeting. / @handle"

**Design direction:** 4:5, off-white background, one ink headline + one gray supporting line, a
single green accent on the number, "n/8" bottom-left, identical layout every slide.

**Cover image prompt (Ideogram/Nano Banana):**
> "A portrait 4:5 social carousel cover slide. Minimal editorial style, off-white background, large
> bold dark headline, one small green accent. Headline text (render exactly): 'We deleted our
> most-requested feature.' Supporting line: '5 lessons so you don't waste 6 months →'. High
> contrast, generous margins, mobile-legible, no clutter."

*(Body slides reuse the same skeleton, swapping the headline/line and number.)*

## Example 2 — Myth-bust carousel (LinkedIn PDF) · goal: authority + saves

- **Cover:** "4 things you believe about LinkedIn that are killing your reach →"
- **Slide 2:** "Myth: post every day. / Reality: post when you have something. Quality drives dwell."
- **Slide 3:** "Myth: links get clicks. / Reality: links in the post suppress reach. Put them in
  comments."
- **Slide 4:** "Myth: more hashtags = more reach. / Reality: 3–5 relevant ones. The rest is noise."
- **Slide 5:** "Myth: company pages win. / Reality: people follow people. Post from a person."
- **Slide 6 (payoff):** "Reach isn't a volume game. It's a dwell-and-comments game."
- **Slide 7 (CTA):** "Save this. Then go fix your next post. / @handle"

**Design:** square PDF, clean professional template, consistent header band, slide number, brand
accent. (Post copy + the link-in-comments handled by `linkedin-post-writer`.)

## Contrast — the text-dump version (do NOT do this)

- A cover that's a small-type paragraph with no hook.
- Slides each packed with 4–5 sentences, edge to edge, different layouts.
- No spine, no momentum, no save-CTA.

→ Nobody swipes past slide 1, and nobody saves it. One idea per slide, big legible type, a cover
that promises the swipe — that's the difference.

---

## What the good examples share

- A **cover that hooks + promises** the swipe, legible at thumbnail.
- **One idea per slide**, headline + ≤1 line, consistent layout.
- A clear **spine** and **momentum** to the last slide.
- A **payoff + one save-leaning CTA**.
- A **consistent design direction** + ready image prompts (or a template spec).
- On-voice, no fabricated stats, AI-disclosure where generated.
