# Slide Craft

A carousel is read at a glance, on a phone, one slide at a time. Each slide is a small poster, not
a paragraph. This is how to write and lay out each one.

## The cover formula

The cover does two jobs in a few words: **hook + promise.**

- **Hook** — a real mechanism from `hook-writer` (curiosity, contrarian, specificity, stakes).
- **Promise** — what the swipe delivers ("5 hooks that actually work", "How we cut churn in half").
- **A swipe cue** — "→" or a visual arrow, so people know to swipe.

It must be **legible at thumbnail size** (it shows small in the feed) and stand alone as a strong
image. One big idea, big type, minimal words. No slow build, no tiny text.

Cover shapes (rewrite in voice):
- *Listicle:* "5 LinkedIn hooks that actually earn the swipe →"
- *Contrarian:* "Stop writing carousels like it's 2021 →"
- *Outcome:* "How we doubled saves with one change →"

## Each body slide = one poster

- **Headline first.** A short, bold headline carries the slide's one idea. It should be readable in
  a second.
- **At most one supporting line.** A brief clarifier under the headline — not a paragraph. If you
  need more, it's two slides.
- **Front-load.** The point is the headline, not buried below.
- **Minimal words.** Aim for a headline + ≤1 line. White space is your friend; cramming is the
  enemy.

## Visual hierarchy

Every slide should have a clear hierarchy the eye reads instantly:

1. **Headline** — biggest, boldest.
2. **Supporting line** — smaller, secondary.
3. **Slide number / progress** — small, consistent corner.
4. **Accent** — one brand color/element for emphasis, used consistently.

Keep the hierarchy and layout **consistent across all slides** — same type sizes, margins, palette,
position of the slide number. Consistency is what makes a carousel look professional; inconsistency
makes it look thrown together.

## Legibility rules

- Big type; high contrast text vs background.
- Don't fill the frame edge-to-edge — leave margins (and respect platform safe zones).
- One idea visible without zooming.
- Test: glance at the slide for one second — do you get it? If not, cut text.

## Momentum between slides

- Number slides ("3/7") for orientation.
- Use a light forward pull where natural ("the next one is the one people skip →").
- Keep a consistent visual rhythm so swiping feels effortless.

## The CTA slide

The final slide makes **one** ask, in voice, save-leaning:

- *Save:* "Save this for your next post." (the default — carousels are saved)
- *Follow:* "Follow for more breakdowns like this."
- *Comment:* "Which one do you already do?"

Keep it clean — one ask, on-brand, plus the handle/logo. Don't stack three CTAs on the last slide.

## The text spec you hand off

Deliver the exact text **per slide** (cover, each body slide, CTA), labeled and numbered, so it can
be designed or fed to an image generator verbatim. Precise slide text is what makes the image pack
(next reference) produce usable slides.
