# Superfans & Honest Scope

The most valuable people in any community are the small, devoted core — and the rules that keep
community-building genuine.

## Superfans: find, nurture, elevate

Most of a community's life comes from a **small minority of highly engaged members.** Prioritizing them
beats chasing broad reach.

**Find them.** They're the people who interact with almost everything, reply thoughtfully, defend you
unprompted, create about you, and show up first. (You spot them by presence over time — natively, not
via WoopSocial analytics.)

**Nurture them.** Move them up the ladder — **lurker → regular → advocate:**

- **Recognition** — name them, spotlight them, thank them publicly (with consent).
- **Access** — early/exclusive content, a direct line, a peek behind the scenes.
- **Status / roles** — make them moderators, ambassadors, beta testers, "founding members."
- **Collaboration** — invite them to co-create, give feedback, shape what you build.

**Elevate them into advocates.** A nurtured superfan becomes a word-of-mouth engine, a UGC source, and
a moderator who helps the community run itself. A handful of true advocates outperforms a large passive
following.

## Honest scope (what this is, and isn't)

- **Advisory + content, not a community platform.** WoopSocial **publishes/schedules** the
  community-building **content** (rituals, prompts, spotlights, announcements) via `scheduling-and-queue`.
  It does **not** run Discord/Slack/groups, manage broadcast channels or an inbox, identify superfans
  from data, track membership/retention, or show analytics. The **human runs the actual space and
  relationships in-platform**; the **agent advises strategy and drafts/schedules the content.**
- **No fake community — ever.** Don't buy members, run engagement pods, or astroturf activity to look
  busy. It's **fake, detectable, and corrodes the one thing community runs on: trust.** Genuine
  belonging is built slowly through real value and real interaction — there's no shortcut, and faking it
  backfires.
- **Consent & member privacy.** Get a member's **permission before spotlighting** them or reposting
  their content; don't expose members' personal information; respect the privacy of closed spaces.
- **It's a long-term commitment.** Community is a program you sustain (consistency > speed), not a
  campaign you run once. Don't open a space you can't keep tending — an abandoned community is worse
  than none.
- **Protect the people.** Running community is real work; pair it with the boundaries in
  `engagement-routine`, and protect the space (and yourself) per `crisis-and-moderation`.

## Self-sustaining is the goal

You've succeeded when the community **doesn't need you for every interaction** — members welcome
newcomers, answer each other, and carry the culture themselves. Build toward handing them the keys, not
toward being the center of every exchange.
