# Where Community Lives

Community can grow in several places. Choosing the right one — by **where your audience already is** and
**a clear purpose**, not by platform hype — is the decision that makes or breaks it.

> Re-verify platform features/stats quarterly.

## The spectrum

From lightest-touch to most-owned:

1. **In-feed comment culture** — members talking in your comments, DMs, and shares. The starting point
   for everyone; you can build real community here without any new platform (facilitate member-to-member
   replies, pin great ones, ask questions back). Most brands should **max this out first.**
2. **Semi-owned spaces** — **Instagram/WhatsApp Broadcast Channels, Close Friends, group chats.** A
   direct line to your most engaged fans, outside the feed algorithm. Treat them as **VIP spaces**
   (exclusive drops, behind-the-scenes, early access) — **not promo dumps.** DMs increasingly outpace
   feeds for real conversation.
3. **Owned community spaces** — **Facebook/LinkedIn Groups, Discord, Slack, Substack, Telegram,
   newsletters.** Built for peer-to-peer interaction and durable, algorithm-proof relationships.
   Highest potential, **highest commitment.**

## Choose by audience + purpose (not by hype)

**The gate:** don't launch a Discord because a competitor has one. Ask:

- **Where is your audience already gathering and comfortable?** Pick that. (Professional/interest-led →
  LinkedIn/Facebook Groups; highly engaged fan/creator/dev communities → Discord; written depth →
  Substack/newsletter; UGC-driven → IG/TikTok.) "The right choice is guided by audience behavior, not
  platform popularity."
- **Is there a clear promise to join?** A space without a reason to exist goes **noisy, inactive, or
  promo-dependent** fast. Define what members gain (early access, expert help, networking, learning,
  belonging) before opening a single channel.
- **Can you commit?** An owned space is an **ongoing program**, not a launch. It needs consistent
  presence and **moderation from day one** (trained mods, clear rules, escalation → `crisis-and-moderation`).

## Start small and earned

- **Begin in-feed**, prove members want to connect, then graduate the most engaged into a semi-owned or
  owned space. Don't build a big empty room.
- **One owned space, done well** beats three half-alive ones. Concentrate where the community actually
  is (echoes the engagement-routine "concentrate, don't spread thin" rule).
- **The #1 mistake:** treating an owned space like a broadcast channel. Members want **participation,
  access, and each other** — if it's an announcement feed, retention drops.

## Examples of the shape

Branded groups (e.g. a 40k-member Facebook Group around a product), VIP broadcast channels for content
drops, layered Discords (open rooms + focused channels + gated superfan sections + voice), and
fan-to-fan frameworks (rituals/identity markers that let fans connect with *each other*). The common
thread: the brand creates the **space and the reasons**, the **members create the community.**
