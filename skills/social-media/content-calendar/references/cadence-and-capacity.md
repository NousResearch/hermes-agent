# Cadence & Capacity

The first decision in any content calendar is **how often** — and it's the one most people get
wrong. This framework sizes cadence to reality so the calendar survives past week three.

> Cadence norms below are general 2026 guidance — verify quarterly, and always match to capacity,
> not to the platform's maximum.

## Why calendars fail: overcommitting

The number-one reason content calendars die is **ambition outrunning capacity.** Someone plans
daily posts on five platforms, sustains it for two weeks, burns out, and stops entirely. A
sustainable minimum you actually keep **beats** an ambitious plan you abandon — consistency is the
asset, and you only get it from a cadence you can hold on a bad week, not a good one.

So: design for the **floor**, not the ceiling. What can you maintain when you're busy, sick, or
travelling? That's your real cadence. You can always post *more*; you can't un-break a streak.

## Size cadence to real capacity

Estimate honestly:

- **Time** — hours/week actually available for content (creation + engagement, not just posting).
- **People** — solo, or who does what (create / edit / schedule / engage).
- **Budget** — tools, freelancers, editing, AI generation.
- **Energy** — the realistic floor on a bad week, not the heroic peak.

Then set a cadence that fits the floor. If the math doesn't work, **cut platforms or cut
frequency** — don't cut sleep.

## Per-platform sustainable cadence (general norms — verify)

Different platforms reward different rhythms; don't apply one number everywhere:

| Platform | Rewarded cadence (norm) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | high — ~daily to several/week | volume + native; rewatch-driven |
| Instagram (Reels/carousels) | ~3–5×/week | quality over volume |
| LinkedIn | ~3–5×/week | dwell-driven; daily often dilutes quality |
| X/Twitter | daily+ tolerated | higher volume is normal |
| YouTube long | ~weekly | Shorts can be more frequent |
| Pinterest | steady, ongoing pinning | evergreen, search-driven |
| Newsletter | weekly/biweekly | consistency matters most |

Pick **one or two primary platforms** to do well rather than five badly — especially solo.

## Repurposing multiplies output without multiplying work

The way to cover more platforms sustainably is **not** to create more from scratch — it's to
repurpose one core piece across platforms (a Reel → a TikTok → a LinkedIn clip → a carousel →
X posts). One idea, many native cuts. This is what makes a multi-platform calendar feasible on
limited capacity. Route the actual transforms to `cross-platform-repurposing`.

## Buffer and batching

- **Batch creation** — make several pieces in one focused session rather than scrambling daily.
  Cheaper context-switching, more consistent quality.
- **Keep a buffer/queue** — stay a week (or more) ahead so one bad week doesn't break the streak.
  The buffer is what makes the calendar *robust*, not just aspirational.

## The minimum-viable-cadence rule

When in doubt, start **below** what feels ambitious. A solo founder posting 3 strong LinkedIn posts
and 2 TikToks a week — held for six months — will beat the person who planned daily-everything and
quit in February. Add cadence only once the current one is comfortably sustained.
