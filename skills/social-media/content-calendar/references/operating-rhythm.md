# Operating Rhythm

A calendar is only as good as the workflow that keeps it running. The skeleton tells you *what* to
post; the operating rhythm is *how the work actually happens* week to week — and it's what separates
a calendar that lasts from one that's abandoned by week three.

## The planning ritual

Set a recurring, time-boxed planning cadence so content never depends on inspiration:

- **Monthly (≈60–90 min):** look at the month — anchors (launches, events), the theme/arc, any
  series adjustments. Decide the shape.
- **Weekly (≈30 min):** fill next week's slots from the skeleton into actual briefs (this is the
  hand-off to `batch-content-plan`), and pick what goes in the reactive slots.
- **Daily (≈10–15 min):** engage — reply to comments/DMs early (especially the first hour after
  posting). Engagement is part of the rhythm, not an afterthought.

Put these on the calendar as recurring blocks. The ritual *is* the system.

## Batch creation

Create in focused batches rather than scrambling each day:

- Group like work (film all TikToks in one session; write all carousels in another) to cut
  context-switching.
- Aim to produce a week (or more) at once, then schedule it out.
- Batching is what makes a buffer possible.

## The buffer / queue

Stay **ahead** of live:

- Keep at least a few days — ideally a week+ — of content queued in WoopSocial.
- The buffer absorbs bad weeks: when life happens, the queue keeps posting and the streak holds.
- Refill the buffer during the weekly ritual before it runs dry.

A buffer turns "consistency" from a willpower problem into a system property.

## Review & iterate (honestly)

Improve the calendar over time — without overclaiming data:

- **WoopSocial has no analytics surface yet**, so don't build the calendar around "our best
  times/top performers" as if that data lives here. Review using **native platform insights the user
  pulls**, or **qualitative judgment** (what resonated, what drained you, what's sustainable).
- Each month, ask: which slots/series are working? which drain effort for little return? Is the
  cadence still sustainable? Adjust the skeleton — kill what isn't working, double down on what is.
- Treat timing/cadence tweaks as **experiments to observe**, not optimizations you can prove.

## Roles (if a team)

If more than one person is involved, assign the recurring functions: **create · edit · schedule ·
engage · review.** Ambiguity here is where team calendars stall. One owner per slot type.

## Hand-offs

The calendar is the top of a chain it doesn't execute alone:

- **Fill a specific period →** `batch-content-plan` (turns slots into briefs).
- **Write each piece →** the content skills (`caption-writer`, `reels-script`, `tiktok-script`,
  `carousel-writer`, `thread-writer`, `linkedin-post-writer`).
- **Adapt across platforms →** `cross-platform-repurposing`.
- **Schedule/publish →** `scheduling-and-queue` (into the buffer/queue).
- **Reactive slots →** `trend-jacking`.

The content-calendar skill owns the **recurring structure and rhythm**; these own execution.
