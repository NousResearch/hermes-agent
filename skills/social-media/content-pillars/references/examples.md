# Examples — pillar sets across business types

Real, ownable pillar sets (illustrative businesses), each contrasted with the **generic version**
to show the difference. Notice: every pillar is a *theme*, every set has *one signature pillar*
(⭐), and promotion is the minority. These aren't intents or formats.

---

## 1. B2B SaaS — payroll tool for small-business owners
- ⭐ **Payroll without a specialist** — the signature POV: this scary, "expert-only" task made
  simple for owners. *(Owns it: that's the whole product thesis.)*
- **Small-business money mistakes** — the audience's broader pains around finances/taxes.
- **Behind the build** — transparent product decisions, what they ship and why. *(Connection.)*
- **Owner wins** — customers who took payroll off their plate. *(Proof / soft conversion.)*

*Generic version (avoid):* "Educational, product updates, testimonials, behind-the-scenes." →
intents and formats, not themes.

## 2. Ecommerce / DTC — sustainable sneakers
- ⭐ **Design-first sustainability** — the POV that you shouldn't sacrifice looks to be green.
- **The materials story** — where the recycled materials come from, how shoes are made.
- **How to wear them** — styling, outfits, real life. *(Audience-relevant utility.)*
- **In the wild** — customers wearing them, UGC. *(Proof / community.)*

*Generic version:* "Product photos, promos, eco facts, reviews." → catalog, not pillars.

## 3. Local — neighborhood coffee shop
- ⭐ **The craft of coffee, demystified** — making good coffee approachable, not intimidating.
- **Our corner of the neighborhood** — the place, the community, local life.
- **The people** — baristas, regulars, the owner. *(Connection.)*
- **What's on this week** — seasonal drinks, events. *(Soft promotion.)*

*Generic version:* "Menu posts, behind-the-scenes, promotions." → no theme to own.

## 4. Professional services — equity-comp financial advisory
- ⭐ **Equity comp, decoded** — the specialist POV: RSUs/options need a specialist, not a
  generalist.
- **Tech-employee money decisions** — the audience's real questions (vesting, taxes, timing).
- **How specialists think** — frameworks, mental models. *(Credibility.)*
- **Anonymized client outcomes** — real (compliant) results. *(Proof — within guardrails.)*

*Generic version:* "Finance tips, market news, company updates." → un-ownable and generic.

## 5. Personal brand — indie maker building in public
- ⭐ **Building in public, real numbers** — radical transparency as the differentiator.
- **Lessons from failures** — what broke and what it taught. *(Connection + credibility.)*
- **The craft of {their skill}** — how they actually do the work.
- **Tools & workflow** — what they use, how. *(Audience-relevant utility.)*

*Generic version:* "Motivation, tips, my journey, promos." → vague and interchangeable.

## 6. Nonprofit — youth literacy
- ⭐ **Kids choosing their own books** — the signature POV: agency over prescription.
- **Impact, in numbers and faces** — outcomes + kid stories. *(Serves donors.)*
- **Why reading changes lives** — the bigger case. *(Awareness.)*
- **Ways to help** — donate, volunteer, partner. *(The single conversion pillar.)*
  *(Note: pillars 2 & 4 serve donors/supporters; 1 & 3 serve the broader audience.)*

*Generic version:* "Stories, stats, donation asks, events." → mix of formats and intents.

---

## The pattern across all six

- Every pillar is a **theme the brand can own**, with a real reason.
- Each set has **exactly one signature pillar** (⭐) carrying the brand's POV.
- Pillars are about the **audience's world**, not the brand's catalog.
- Each could generate **dozens of posts** (sustainable).
- **Promotion is one pillar at most**, the minority.
- None of them are "educational / behind-the-scenes / promotional" — because those aren't
  pillars.

Aim for this level of specificity and ownership every time.
