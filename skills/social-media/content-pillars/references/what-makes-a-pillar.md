# What Makes a Pillar

This is the reference that separates real content pillars from the generic version everyone
ships. Get the definition right and the rest of the skill is easy.

## The definition

A **content pillar is a durable theme the brand returns to — a subject area it can own.** Not a
topic for one post; a well you can draw from forever. The strongest pillars sit at the
intersection of four things:

- **Ownable** — the brand is credible or distinctive here (from positioning, proof, POV).
- **Relevant** — the target audience genuinely cares (from their jobs-to-be-done and pains).
- **Strategic** — it advances a business goal (awareness, trust, conversion, community).
- **Sustainable** — it generates endless content; you could post on it weekly for years.

Miss *ownable* and you sound like everyone. Miss *relevant* and you talk to yourself. Miss
*sustainable* and you run dry in three weeks.

## The distinction everyone gets wrong

Pillars, intents, and formats are three different layers. Confusing them is the #1 reason
content strategies are weak.

| Layer | Question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Pillar** | *What theme?* | "Payroll without a specialist" · "The craft of coffee" · "Sustainable materials" |
| **Intent** | *What job?* | educate · entertain · inspire · connect · promote |
| **Format** | *What shape?* | Reel · carousel · thread · single image · story |

A **single pillar** is expressed through **many intents** and **many formats**. The pillar
"payroll without a specialist" can be an educational carousel, a funny relatable Reel, a
customer-win story, and a promotional post — all the same pillar.

So when someone says their pillars are "educational, behind-the-scenes, and promotional," they
haven't named themes at all — they've named two intents and a format. That set tells a planner
*nothing* about what the brand is actually about. Convert them: *what* will the educational
posts teach? *that* subject is the pillar.

## The pillar test

A candidate is a real pillar only if it passes all four:

1. **Ownable** — Can you finish the sentence "We can own this because ___" with something
   specific and true (expertise, proof, a distinct POV)? If the answer is "anyone could post
   this," it's not yours.
2. **Relevant** — Would the target audience care, or is it only interesting to the brand?
   (Product features usually fail here; the audience's *problem* the feature solves usually
   passes.)
3. **Strategic** — Does it move a real goal? A pillar that's fun but advances nothing is a
   hobby.
4. **Sustainable** — Brainstorm right now: can you list **10+ post ideas** from it in two
   minutes? If you stall at three, it's a topic, not a pillar.

Run every candidate through this. Sustainable and Ownable are the usual failure points.

## Anti-patterns — what is NOT a pillar

- **Intents as pillars:** "educational," "inspirational," "promotional." Those are jobs, not
  themes.
- **Formats as pillars:** "Reels," "carousels," "behind-the-scenes," "memes." Those are shapes.
- **Too broad to mean anything:** "business," "marketing," "lifestyle." Un-ownable, infinite but
  generic.
- **Brand-only relevance:** "our product features," "company news." Nobody followed for your
  changelog.
- **One-off topics:** a single campaign or moment isn't a pillar (it's a post or a series).
- **Too many:** eight "pillars" is no focus. Cap at 3–5.

## The signature pillar

Every brand should have **one signature pillar** — the theme that embodies its distinct point of
view, the thing it most wants to be known for. It's usually derived straight from the brand's
POV in the profile. The supporting pillars make the brand useful and human; the signature pillar
makes it *memorable*. If you can't identify one, the brand's positioning probably needs
sharpening — surface that.
