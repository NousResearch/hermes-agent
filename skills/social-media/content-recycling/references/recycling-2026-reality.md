# Recycling 2026 reality — verified

*Volatile. Re-verify quarterly (platform originality rules and reach norms shift).*

## Why recycling works (the case)
- **Most of your audience never saw the original.** Only ~5–10% of a network sees a given post; an
  estimated **80%+ miss your initial posts**. New followers never saw it at all.
- **Algorithms can re-push proven content.** Reposts/revisions of pieces that worked before have a
  good chance of being surfaced again in their refreshed form. **LinkedIn now values relevance over
  recency** — strong evergreen posts keep gaining traction long after publishing.
- **Evergreen has a long half-life.** Frameworks, tips, and core messaging stay useful for months/
  years; repetition strengthens brand recall (especially ideas that need several exposures to land).

## The hard constraint — refresh, not duplicate
- **Meta's Original Content rules** (enforced since July 2025; verify-quarterly): **penalties for
  unoriginal/duplicate content** — reduced reach (applied account-wide, not just the offending post),
  demonetization, and "non-recommendable" status for accounts repeatedly reusing material; Instagram
  **redirects viewers to the original** version of a clip rather than showing reposts; other-platform
  watermarks penalized. Reaction videos / trend participation / added-take content are exempt. **Quality over volume** — fewer original
  posts outperform more low-effort reposts.
- Net: **identical reposting is risky.** Treat recycling as **editing, not duplication.** "Never
  repeat the same opening line twice."

## What's recyclable vs not
- **Recycle:** evergreen + proven — practical tips, frameworks, timeless case studies, product guides,
  customer success, core brand messaging. Also **strong-but-underseen** pieces that deserved more.
- **Don't recycle:** time-bound — events, seasonal promos, outdated news, trend-jacks. Always check an
  old post still aligns with current facts and trends before reusing.

## Cadence + spacing (verify-quarterly)
- Recycle a top performer roughly **every 90–120 days** — long enough that the audience has moved on
  and new followers have arrived.
- Space assets across the calendar; **don't dump** a backlog at once. Sprinkle recycled pieces among
  native new content so the feed doesn't read as repetitive.
- Let **native analytics** drive which assets re-enter the queue (data over guessing).

## Refresh tactics (quick wins)
Rewrite the first sentence · swap the opening visual · tighten the first five seconds · update dates/
prices/stats/claims · flip the format (carousel ↔ Reel ↔ text) · adapt to season ("Summer Posting
Guide") · re-check compliance/endorsement language. **Change one major variable per variant** (hook,
proof point, or CTA) so you can learn what moved the metric.
