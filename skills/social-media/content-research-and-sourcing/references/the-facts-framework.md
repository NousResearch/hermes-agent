# The FACTS framework — verification as craft, sourcing as strategy

A piece of content is a stack of claims wearing a voice. This framework verifies the stack before it ships —
proportionately (color gets a glance; load-bearing claims get the full chain). Most published errors trace to
a skipped F (nobody listed the claims) or a skipped T (an AI citation trusted because the link worked).

## F — Find the load-bearing claims
Inventory the draft's claims and triage: **load-bearing** (the piece stands on them — the headline stat, the
causal claim, the quote) vs **color** (illustrative, low-stakes). Load-bearing claims get the full FACTS
treatment; color gets a sanity check. This proportionality is what makes rigor sustainable at social-media
cadence — verify everything equally and you'll verify nothing consistently.

## A — Ascend to the primary source
Trace each load-bearing claim UP the chain: the marketing blog cites the news article cites the report — cite
the **report.** Aggregators are leads, not sources. At the primary, check: who ran it, sample and method, who
funded it (a vendor study is attributed *as* a vendor study), and what the finding actually says (abstracts
and headlines routinely overstate). Citing the primary is also strategy: most competitors quote the
aggregator; the account that reads the source becomes the source.

## C — Check freshness + context
Three decay modes kill true-once claims: **staleness** (a 2015 figure quoted as current), **supersession**
(later research reversed it) and **retraction** (known-false, however popular). Plus the zombie-stat tell:
citations that circle blogs forever without reaching a named study. Context check: does the stat mean what
the draft implies (Mehrabian-class misapplication)? Scope check: does the sample generalize to the claim's
subject? Date-stamp every stat in the source log so future reuse re-checks age.

## T — Test AI output hard
AI-supplied citations are **guilty until verified** — the 2026 evidence (60%+ wrong news citations; 12-fold
fabrication surge; 5-of-45 corporate reports) makes this structural, not paranoid. The protocol per citation:
**(1) the source exists** — search the title/authors independently; **(2) the link is honest** — a working DOI
can resolve to an unrelated paper (identifier hijacking); **(3) the source says what's claimed** — open it and
find the claim. Anything failing any step gets replaced or cut. AI is excellent at *drafting* and at
*summarizing sources you hand it* — the failure mode is asking it to *supply* the sources.

## S — Show your sources
Attribution that survives social formats: **in-line** naming where the claim appears ("per the 2026 Lancet
audit"), **in-graphic** credit on quote cards and infographics (source + date in the design — route to those
skills' rules), **links** in caption/comments per platform norms. Keep a **source log** per piece (claim →
source → date → link) — it makes corrections fast, reuse safe, and audits painless. Quoting ethics: short
quotes with attribution, never wholesale lifting, never fabricated or context-stripped quotes (→
`quote-cards-and-text-graphics`). **YMYL claims** (health/money) carry the heightened bar: official/peer-
reviewed primaries, qualified language, advice disclaimers. Then the human signs off and **WoopSocial
publishes** — verification happens before scheduling, never after.
