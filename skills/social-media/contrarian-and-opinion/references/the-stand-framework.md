# The STAND framework — a take that builds authority, not outrage

Taking a stand is the job — but a *defensible* one, genuinely held and honestly argued. Each letter is a gate;
the counterfeits fail at S (no real belief / manufactured) or A (no evidence underneath).

## S — Stake a belief you actually hold
A real position that goes **against your niche's consensus** — surfaced from what the human actually believes
(a friction they've hit, a thing they've watched go wrong), never manufactured for reach.
- **The narrowness test:** would your three closest competitors refuse to publish it? If they'd all happily post
  it, it isn't a stance — it's best-practice in a costume (fake-contrarian).
- **Not ragebait:** the goal is to be *right and early*, not to provoke. If the only point is the reaction, stop.

## T — Target the idea, punch up or sideways
Aim at the **practice / conventional wisdom / the powerful** — never punch **down** at vulnerable people or
individuals. Attack the **idea, not the identity.** A critique of a competitor goes after their *approach*, fairly
and accurately, never the people (no defamation, no misrepresentation).

## A — Argue it (earn the take)
A naked hot take is noise; a defended one is authority. Back the claim with **reasoning + evidence** — lived
experience, a specific example, data (pull from `data-and-original-research` / `social-proof-and-testimonials`).
"Contrarian-for-attention backfires when there's no evidence underneath" — the argument (why the consensus is
wrong) *is* the content. Any factual claim inside the opinion must be **true and sourced**.

## N — Nod to the steelman
Name the **strongest** version of the opposing view, in good faith, before you disagree. This is the move most
people skip and the one that matters most: it's what separates a credible authority from a troll, it pre-empts
the obvious objection, and it **disarms the pile-on**. Steelman, never strawman.

## D — Declare it clearly, then engage
- **One specific, unhedged claim** — no mush, no weasel-words, no strawman. Clear that it's an *opinion*, stated
  with conviction.
- **Invite genuine debate, not outrage** — a real "where am I wrong?" beats "comment YES if you agree"
  (engagement-bait, now suppressed).
- **Show up in the comments in good faith** (route to `reply-and-comment-writer`); have a de-escalation path
  (`crisis-and-moderation`) if it heats up. The **human owns the stance** and the reputational call; WoopSocial
  publishes.

## The gut check before you post
Three questions: (1) Do I actually believe this, or am I performing it for reach? (2) Could I defend it to an
expert in the room without flinching? (3) Am I attacking an idea, or a person? If any answer is wrong, it's not
ready — fix the take, don't soften it into mush.
