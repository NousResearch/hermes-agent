# The Response Playbook

Once triaged (`triage-and-severity.md`) and a human is in the loop, this is how to respond. The arc:
**Acknowledge → Investigate → Respond → Follow Up.**

> The agent **drafts**; a **human approves and posts.** For high-severity, legal/leadership approve the
> wording first (`escalation-pause-and-safety.md`).

## 1. Acknowledge — fast, human, honest

- **Speed:** get a first acknowledgment out **within ~30–60 minutes** (up to ~2 hours) of identifying a
  real issue — **even without all the answers.** Silence creates a vacuum that rumor and misinformation
  fill, and the algorithm amplifies the negativity in the meantime.
- **You don't need every answer yet:** *"We're aware of this and we're looking into it now. We'll share
  more shortly."*
- **Speak human, drop the legalese.** Empathetic, plain, in brand voice (a touch more serious than
  usual). *"We understand the concern and we're sorry for the worry this caused."*
- **Don't be defensive, don't spin, don't argue in the replies.**

## 2. Investigate — get the facts (in parallel)

While the acknowledgment holds the space, **confirm what happened** internally. Don't commit to a cause
or a fix you haven't verified. Keep the human/decision-makers aligned on what's known.

## 3. Respond — the substance

Tailor to the type (from triage):

- **Your fault / a real failure:** **take responsibility plainly** (no non-apology "we're sorry if
  anyone was offended"), say **what you're doing about it**, and **pair words with a real operational
  action** — a fix, a refund, a policy change. Words without action read as PR.
- **Misinformation / a false claim:** **correct the record with evidence**, calmly and factually — do
  **not** apologize for something that didn't happen. Link proof.
- **A deepfake / impersonation:** state clearly it's fake, show evidence, report it, and get the true
  version in front of your audience fast.
- **Centralize:** for anything sustained, use a **pinned post / landing page** with **timestamped
  updates** so people have one source of truth instead of chasing fragments.

## 4. Follow up — close the loop

When resolved, **say what happened, what you did, and what changes.** A well-handled crisis can leave
trust *higher* than before. Then debrief internally: what triggered it, what to prevent, what to add to
the playbook.

## Transparency over scrubbing

**Don't delete legitimate criticism to make it look clean.** Scrubbing genuine complaints triggers the
**Streisand effect** — hiding it draws far more attention and reads as a cover-up. (What you *may*
remove is in `moderation.md`.) Transparency is the defensive move.

## Pre-approved holding lines (draft, then human-approves)

- Blunt founder voice: *"We messed up the shipping this week. That's on us, not you. Here's what we're
  doing about it, starting today: [action]. Updates as we have them."*
- Warm studio voice: *"We know some of you have been waiting longer than you should have — we're sorry,
  and we hear you. Here's exactly what we're doing to fix it, and we'll keep you posted right here."*

Keep critical wording **approved** before it goes out; never let the agent post a crisis response on its
own.
