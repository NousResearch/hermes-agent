# Examples — repurposing end to end

Two worked flows: one atom fanned out to five native versions, and a long-form piece atomized.
Voice is the blunt indie-founder voice from earlier skills (short, dry, no exclamation marks,
concrete). Your output uses the loaded `voice.md`.

---

## Example 1 — one atom → five native versions

**The atom:** we deleted our most-requested feature because nobody used it; the "boring"
replacement became the most-used in two days. Lesson: build less, watch behavior not requests.

### Instagram — Reel
- **Hook (visual):** hand hitting "delete" on a block of code. **On-screen:** "Deleting 6 months
  of work." **Spoken:** "We just deleted our most-requested feature."
- Beats: the why → the boring fix → the result. **CTA:** save this for your next roadmap meeting.

### LinkedIn — text post
- **Hook line:** "'Requested' is the most expensive word in product."
- Whitespace-formatted, expands the lesson (built for words, not behavior), ends on a genuine
  question. **CTA:** "Anyone actually build straight from requests and have it work?" Link in
  comments if any.

### X — single post
- "'Customers requested it' is how you justify six months building something nobody uses.
  Deleted ours today. Watching behavior from now on."
- Two lines, the point up front. No hashtags.

### Instagram — carousel
- Cover: "We deleted our most-requested feature →". Slides: 1) the ask vs the use, 2) requested ≠
  used, 3) build the boring thing, 4) shipping less is a feature. **CTA:** save.

### Pinterest — pin
- Keyword-rich: "Product roadmap lesson: why customer requests mislead | build less". Image: the
  "requested vs used" contrast graphic. Links to the full blog. Evergreen, searchable.

**Notice:** same atom, five genuinely different builds — different hooks, formats, lengths, CTAs,
register. None is a copy-paste of another.

**The bad version (avoid):** the LinkedIn paragraph pasted identically into IG, X, and a Pinterest
description. Fits none, looks like spam to anyone following on two, and underperforms everywhere.

## Example 2 — long-form → many atoms

**Source:** a 1,800-word blog post, "10 things we learned in our first year."

**Atomize (don't summarize):** pull the strongest standalone atoms —
1. the deleted-feature lesson (→ the five posts above),
2. "our first hire was a mistake — here's the signal we missed,"
3. "the pricing change that doubled revenue,"
4. "the channel we wasted 6 months on,"
5. "the one metric we should've watched sooner."

Each atom → its own native post(s) across platforms. One blog → ~3 weeks of content. Optionally,
one post points to the full blog (the hero link), but every atom stands alone.

**Anti-pattern:** "Just published a new blog: 10 things we learned 👇 [link]" as the *only* post.
That throws away nine atoms.

---

## What both examples show

- **Atomize first**, then build native — don't translate a wrapper.
- Each version is **different and stands alone**.
- **Voice constant, register shifts** per platform.
- Long-form yields **many** posts, not one summary.
- Quality per platform is the gate; volume is the byproduct.
