# The PROVE framework — original research that earns links + AI citations

Design a study people *have* to cite. Each letter is a gate; skipping one is where studies fail (no data void,
soft method, cooked analysis, buried finding, or no distribution).

## P — Pick a question inside a data void
A claim worth proving where good public data doesn't exist **and** your audience + journalists + AI engines
would cite the answer. Your unfair advantages, in order of strength:
1. **Proprietary/internal data** — your platform, your customers, your transactions. Highest originality; no one
   else can replicate it. (Anonymize before analysis.)
2. **A recurring survey of your niche** — become the annual source ("State of X"); the cadence compounds.
3. **Fresh analysis of a public dataset** — lower originality but real and fast; great when you have no list.
Reject questions already answered well, questions you can't get real data for, and questions whose answer no one
would repeat. The test: *write the headline stat you hope to find — would a journalist or an AI cite it?*

## R — Run a sound method
Choose the method that fits the question, then design it **before** collecting:
- **Define**: population, sample frame (who's eligible + how you reach them), target n, recruitment, time window.
- **Instrument**: neutral, non-leading questions (not "don't you agree X is overpriced?"); one idea per question;
  answer options that don't bias.
- **Plan the analysis up front** (which comparisons, which segments) so you're not fishing later.
Method ladder: proprietary-data analysis > representative survey > public-dataset analysis > simple experiment.
The agent designs the instrument; the **human/tool fields it and gathers the real data**.

## O — Observe honestly (collect + analyse without cooking it)
- Real data only. **Never invent or AI-synthesize data points** — the fastest way to lose the asset and the brand.
- No p-hacking (don't hunt until something is "significant"); no cherry-picking the flattering cut; only segment
  where n supports it.
- **Disclose**: sample size, dates, method, and limitations — prominently, not in fine print. Small n? Frame as
  *directional*, not definitive; never say "most people" off 42 responses.
- Conflict of interest (a self-funded study about your own category) gets disclosed, not hidden.

## V — Voice the one finding that travels
Find the **surprising-but-defensible** headline — one number people will repeat — fully supported by the data
and never inflated (38% is not "nearly half"). Shape it: a clear claim (**X% of Y do Z**), the *why it matters*,
and a memorable name/number. One hero stat carries the study; 2–3 supporting findings back it. Counter-intuitive
beats confirmatory — but only if the data truly says it.

## E — Engineer for citation, then distribute
Build the asset to be cited and then spread it:
- **Report page**: visible methodology + sample + date, a **"Last Updated" stamp**, clean charts, and a
  **copy-paste stat box with an attribution link** (so anyone quoting you links back).
- **Atomize** into cuts → the format writers (carousel/thread/reels/caption) + infographic-and-data-viz.
- **Distribute wide**: pitch journalists the news angle; seed across multiple publications (distribution is the
  citation multiplier); refresh on a cadence (beat the freshness cliff).
- **WoopSocial publishes** the cuts + promo posts and drives traffic to the report; measure citations + saves +
  referral traffic, never a fabricated metric.
