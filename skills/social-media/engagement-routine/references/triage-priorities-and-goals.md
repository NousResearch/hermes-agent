# Triage, Priorities & Goals

How to spend limited engagement time well — what to do first, who to focus on, and how to know it's
working (without WoopSocial analytics).

## Triage: the priority order

When time is short, do the highest-return things first:

1. **Golden-hour comments on your fresh post** — first ~15–20 min; biggest reach lever.
2. **DMs** — relationships and conversions live here; reply while warm.
3. **Negative / urgent** — complaints, mistakes, anything time-sensitive; acknowledge fast, take
   specifics private (→ `reply-and-comment-writer`); route a real crisis to `crisis-and-moderation`.
4. **Older comments** still worth a thoughtful reply.
5. **Proactive comments** — add value on ICP/peer/leader posts (borrows audience).
6. **Skip:** spam and trolls — delete/ignore; don't feed them.

### Worked example — "I have 15 minutes"

Reply to every comment on the post that went out an hour ago (golden-hour leftovers) → answer the 2 new
DMs → one quick, genuine reply to a complaint with "let's sort this in DMs" → if time remains, one
value-adding comment on a niche leader's post. Stop. Done beats perfect.

## The right people, not vanity

Engagement should reach **the people who matter to the business** — your ICP and niche peers — not
maximize raw volume. A flood of comments from the wrong crowd (or from bait) doesn't build anything.
**Make every interaction count**; engage where it compounds.

## Map to a goal (and signals you can actually see)

Pick a **goal** for engagement — relationships, leads, community, support — and **2–3 meaningful
signals**, not vanity metrics:

- **Comment depth** (real conversations, not just "🔥").
- **DM conversations started** (and where they lead).
- **Replies to your replies** / people coming back.
- **Proactive comments that sparked a reply** (the audience-borrowing working).

**Honesty about measurement:** WoopSocial has **no analytics**, so these are read **natively** (the
platform's own insights, your inbox, your eyes) — not from WoopSocial. **Never fabricate performance
data or promise a specific number.** Engagement is a leading indicator; judge it by depth and direction
over weeks, not by a vanity count today.

## What to stop doing

- Chasing likes/followers as the scoreboard (they're the weakest signal).
- Replying to everything everywhere (spreading thin) instead of the right things well.
- Engagement bait ("comment YES") and pods — detected and penalized (→ `linkedin-growth`).
- Buying/automating engagement — fake, detected, self-defeating (see the SKILL's no-automation rule).
