# Hook Mechanisms — why hooks work

This is the difference between this skill and a list of swipe-file templates. Templates ("The
secret to ___ that nobody tells you") go stale because everyone uses them and they name a
*mechanism* without earning it. Learn the mechanisms and you can generate fresh hooks forever,
in any voice.

Every hook works by creating something the reader must resolve. Below are the core mechanisms:
what each is, **why it works** (the underlying human wiring), when to reach for it, and the
failure mode that turns it into noise. Generate across several of these, not one.

> The master rule under all of them: the gap must be **real and resolved by the content.** A
> mechanism used to bait a click the content doesn't pay off is the fastest way to train the
> algorithm — and your audience — to scroll past you.

---

## 1. Curiosity gap
**What:** open a loop — promise an answer you make them stay for.
**Why it works:** an information gap between what we know and want to know creates a genuine
itch we're driven to scratch.
**When:** you have a surprising answer or payoff worth withholding briefly.
**Example shape:** "The reason this post outperformed everything else has nothing to do with the
photo."
**Failure mode:** vague tease with no real payoff ("you won't believe what happened"). Be
specific enough to intrigue, withholding enough to compel.

## 2. Contrarian / pattern interrupt
**What:** say the opposite of what the reader expects.
**Why it works:** a violated expectation demands resolution, and signals a fresh point of view
worth hearing.
**When:** the brand has a real, defensible POV (pull it from brand-profile).
**Example shape:** "Posting every day is hurting your account."
**Failure mode:** contrarian for its own sake, or manufactured outrage. The take has to be true
and defensible, not edgy theater.

## 3. Stakes & cost
**What:** lead with what's at risk, what went wrong, or what something cost.
**Why it works:** we're wired to attend to threats and losses more than equivalent gains (loss
aversion). Stakes make attention feel mandatory.
**When:** there's a real consequence, mistake, or price involved.
**Example shape:** "This one line in our contract cost us $40,000."
**Failure mode:** invented or inflated stakes. If the cost wasn't real, don't claim it.

## 4. Specificity & numbers
**What:** an exact, almost oddly specific number or detail.
**Why it works:** specificity signals truth (vague = made up) and itself provokes curiosity —
*why that number?*
**When:** you have concrete data or a precise detail.
**Example shape:** "We deleted 80% of our features and revenue went up 12%."
**Failure mode:** round, generic numbers ("a lot of people"), or fake precision. The number
must be real.

## 5. Identity / callout
**What:** name the exact person the content is for.
**Why it works:** self-relevance — we instantly attend to things that are *about us*.
**When:** you have a sharp audience segment (from brand-profile).
**Example shape:** "If you're a founder doing your own payroll, stop."
**Failure mode:** calling out "everyone" — the opposite of self-relevant. Be specific about who.

## 6. In-media-res story
**What:** drop into a scene already in motion, no setup.
**Why it works:** narrative transportation — an unfinished moment creates tension we want
closed, and stories pull us in faster than claims.
**When:** you have a real moment, scene, or anecdote.
**Example shape:** "It's 11pm and I'm refreshing Stripe for the fourth time tonight."
**Failure mode:** starting with backstory or context instead of the moment. Begin in the action.

## 7. Mistake / negativity
**What:** lead with the error, the "don't," the failure.
**Why it works:** negativity bias — we weight and attend to negative information more heavily
than positive.
**When:** there's a real lesson from a real failure.
**Example shape:** "I've been writing hooks wrong for three years. Here's the fix."
**Failure mode:** pure negativity with no payoff or lesson. The mistake must lead somewhere
useful.

## 8. Transformation / before → after
**What:** hold up the gap between two states.
**Why it works:** we're wired for progress and change narratives; the distance between before
and after creates a pull to learn how it was crossed.
**When:** you can show a real, believable shift.
**Example shape:** "From 200 to 40,000 followers without posting daily. Here's what changed."
**Failure mode:** a transformation so dramatic it reads as a scam. Believable beats impressive.

## 9. Question
**What:** a sharp, self-relevant question the reader can't help answering in their head.
**Why it works:** combines an open loop with self-reference; the brain auto-answers a good
question.
**When:** the goal is comments, or the topic invites opinion/identity.
**Example shape:** "What's the one task you open 14 tabs to avoid?"
**Failure mode:** generic or yes/no questions ("Want to grow your audience?"). Make it specific
and easy — but interesting — to answer.

## 10. Timeliness / FOMO
**What:** tie the hook to right now — a trend, a deadline, a moment.
**Why it works:** relevance plus mild scarcity/urgency raises the cost of scrolling past.
**When:** there's a genuine timely moment to ride.
**Example shape:** "Everyone's switching to ___ this week. Here's why it's mostly hype."
**Failure mode:** manufactured urgency with no real time element. Don't fake the clock.

## 11. Authority / social proof
**What:** lead with credible proof — a result, a name, a number.
**Why it works:** we defer to credibility and evidence; proof lowers skepticism before it
starts.
**When:** you have real, usable proof (per brand-profile's proof section).
**Example shape:** "After analyzing 1,000 of our posts, one pattern explained the top 5%."
**Failure mode:** vague authority ("trusted by industry leaders"). Use specifics that are
verifiable.

## For video, the hook is three channels at once

In the first 1–3 seconds, the hook is **visual + verbal + on-screen text** simultaneously, and
it must work **muted**:

- **Visual hook:** motion, a pattern interrupt, a striking or mid-action frame. Often outweighs
  the words — the first frame stops the thumb.
- **Verbal hook:** the first spoken words (one of the mechanisms above), no slow intro.
- **On-screen text:** the written hook for sound-off viewers; carries the gap on its own.

Never open a video with a logo, a slow build, or "Hey guys, in today's video…" — those are
attention donations to the scroll.

## Choosing a mechanism

- **Reach/shareability** → contrarian, story, relatable specificity.
- **Comments** → question, contrarian.
- **Saves/authority** → specificity, authority, mistake-lesson.
- **Conversion/trust** → transformation, authority (with real proof).

When stuck, draft one hook each from curiosity, contrarian, specificity, story, and stakes —
five different angles on the same content — and let the scoring rubric pick the winner.
