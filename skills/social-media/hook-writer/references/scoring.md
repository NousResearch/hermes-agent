# Scoring & Selection

Good hooks come from volume plus ruthless selection, not from nailing the first attempt. This is
the method and the rubric.

## The method: generate many, keep one

1. **Generate 5–10** candidate hooks for the piece, deliberately spanning *different
   mechanisms* (curiosity, contrarian, specificity, story, stakes, question…). Forcing variety
   is what surfaces the strong angle you wouldn't have found by polishing the first idea.
2. **Score** each against the rubric below.
3. **Keep the top 1–2.** Most hooks you write should die — that's the process working, not
   failing.
4. **Refine** the winner for format and voice.

Writing one hook and shipping it is the single most common reason hooks underperform.

## The rubric

Score each candidate 0–2 on each dimension (0 = no, 1 = partly, 2 = yes). The winner is rarely
the cleverest — it's the one that clears every line.

| Dimension | The question |
|---|---|
| **Gap** | Does it open a real curiosity/tension/stakes gap a stranger needs to close? |
| **Specificity** | Is there a concrete number, detail, or claim — not a vague tease? |
| **Truth** | Does the content actually pay this off? (No bait-and-switch.) |
| **Fit** | Does it land inside the format's cutoff / first 3 seconds? |
| **Voice** | Does it sound like them — fingerprint hit, "never" list respected? |
| **Pull** | Read cold: would a stranger *need* the next beat? |

**Truth and Pull are pass/fail gates.** A hook that scores high but overpromises (Truth = 0) is
disqualified no matter how compelling — it wins the tap and loses the trust, and the bounce
hurts reach. A hook that doesn't make you need the next beat (Pull = 0) isn't a hook yet,
whatever else it does.

## Anti-patterns — delete these on sight

- **Throat-clearing:** "In today's video…", "So I've been thinking about…", "Let's talk about
  ___." The hook is buried after the warm-up.
- **Greetings:** "Hey everyone!", "Happy Monday!" — attention donated to the scroll.
- **Describing the asset:** "Here's a photo of our team at the event." The eye already sees it.
- **Vague tease / empty curiosity:** "You won't believe this." No real gap, no specificity.
- **Overpromise / clickbait:** a sensational claim the content can't deliver. Burns trust and
  bounces.
- **Giving away the payoff:** the first line answers its own question, so there's no reason to
  continue. Hold something back.
- **Too broad an address:** "Attention everyone…" — the opposite of self-relevant.
- **Template-by-rote:** a swipe-file formula in an obviously borrowed voice. Use the *mechanism*,
  write it fresh in their voice.

## A fast scoring pass (in practice)

For each candidate, read only the hook (cut to the cutoff) and ask in order:
1. Would a stranger need the next beat? *(no → cut it)*
2. Is the content going to deliver this? *(no → cut it)*
3. Is it specific? *(no → sharpen or cut)*
4. Does it sound like them? *(no → rewrite in voice)*

What survives all four is your shortlist. Recommend the strongest and say why in one line.
