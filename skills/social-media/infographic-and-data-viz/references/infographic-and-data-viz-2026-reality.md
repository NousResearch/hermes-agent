# The reality of data viz & infographics in 2026 (verify-quarterly)

Volatile figures — attribute, mark **verify-quarterly**. The craft principles (title = takeaway; honest scales;
data-ink; accessibility) are stable and well-established (Tufte, storytelling-with-data, WCAG).

## The job: make one insight impossible to miss
- A visualization doesn't add information — it makes the insight that was always in the numbers **impossible to
  miss.** Most charts fail by burying the takeaway or quietly distorting it.
- **The title is the takeaway, not a label.** "Revenue grew 28% YoY" beats "Revenue by Quarter"; "Global Sales
  Declined 5% in Q4" beats "Quarterly Sales." A reader should get the point in ~3 seconds without decoding.
- **One chart, one message.** Limit the big idea to a central theme (~2–3 concepts max); if you're showing three
  things, make three visuals.

## Honest scales (the integrity spine — stable)
- **The #1 chart crime is a truncated y-axis** (Tufte's "lie factor"): a y-axis starting at $90M turns $95M→$100M
  (a real 5.3% rise) into a bar that looks ~2× taller. **Bar/column charts must start at zero.** Line charts may
  use a non-zero baseline only if clearly labeled and appropriate to the range.
- **Avoid 3D, skewed perspective, area/bubble distortion, dual axes, inconsistent panel scales, cherry-picked
  ranges** — all manufacture a false read.
- **Context prevents misreads:** per the University of Utah Visualization Design Lab, missing contextual info
  drives misleading interpretations in up to ~84% of cases (attribute) — add the denominator/caveat.
- **Honest viz is strategy, not just ethics:** misleading charts get called out and fact-checked; the credibility
  hit outweighs the punchy distortion.

## Reduce to the signal (data-ink ratio)
Every element should show data or essential context; everything else is noise. Strip 3D, drop shadows, gradients,
decorative backgrounds, and unnecessary gridlines (they make charts harder to read). **Direct-label** instead of
leaning on a busy legend; drop axis labels the title already implies; one color + an accent to highlight the key
value. **Default to a bar chart when unsure** (versatile, rarely misleads). Sometimes the honest answer is **not a
chart** — for one or two numbers, a big stat or a small table is clearer.

## Accessibility (craft, not an afterthought)
~8% of men / ~0.5% of women have color vision deficiency — **never rely on color alone**; use redundant encoding
(labels, shapes, arrows ↑/↓, +/−, patterns). Meet **WCAG contrast** (4.5:1 normal text / 3:1 large). **Alt text
states the takeaway/trend** (formula: "chart type of data where takeaway"), not every value; pair complex charts
with a data table.

## House metrics
Saves/shares (data viz over-indexes on saves) + AI-citation share (well-sourced data is highly citable) + clicks
— read natively; never fabricate a number or a source.
