# The REACT framework — participation people actually want to give

Interactive content converts scrollers into participants — but only when the ask is worth a tap and the loop gets
closed. Each letter is a gate; most interactive content fails at A (a generic ask) or C (no follow-through).

## R — Reason to participate
Every ask does a **job for the audience**, not just for your metrics: involve them in a **real decision** ("which
cover — A or B?" makes followers feel ownership), name a **relatable struggle** ("do you also…?"), open a
**genuine opinion split** (a this-or-that people actually debate), or offer a **payoff for playing** (a quiz
where the answer teaches something). If the only beneficiary is your engagement graph, it's filler — and filler
gets ignored.

## E — Effortless first tap
Match the friction to the ask. The ladder: **poll tap** (one touch) → **slider** (nuance without typing) →
**quiz** (a game) → **question box** (typing) → **comment/UGC** (highest effort). Open with the lowest-friction
ask and escalate: a poll ("have you tried X? yes/no") followed by a question box ("if yes — what happened?") is a
classic stack that converts tap-participants into storytellers. Low-friction taps are also **high-value
algorithmic signals** — participation compounds reach.

## A — Ask something specific
Vague stalls, specific converts. **Scope the AMA** ("ask me anything about [topic]" — never a bare AMA); make the
poll concrete ("which of these two covers?" not "thoughts?"); make quiz questions have a **teaching payoff**
(wrong answers are fun/myth-busting, never punishing); make every option meaningfully different. Every question is
true — no fabricated quiz "facts," no rigged options.

## C — Close the loop
The follow-through is where the value lives: **share the results back** ("78% of you said…"), **answer publicly**
(with permission — visible answers are the social proof that pulls more in), and **batch 5–10 answers into a
series** — one question box feeds days of content. Reply to participants (route to `reply-and-comment-writer`;
the human sends). An unclosed loop teaches the audience that participating does nothing.

## T — Turn responses into content + insight
Every interaction is a **micro-survey**: poll splits become follow-up content ("you asked for X — here it is"),
struggles become the content calendar, quiz misses reveal what to teach next. Feed findings into
`audience-research` and the pipeline — honestly framed (a self-selected Story poll is direction, not a study;
real research routes to `data-and-original-research`). The **human reads the native results** and adds the native
stickers in-app; the agent designs, drafts, and turns the real responses into the next round; **WoopSocial
publishes** the feed-post versions.
