# Avoiding LinkedIn Cringe

This is the differentiator. LinkedIn is full of engagement-bait formats that the algorithm
increasingly down-ranks and that real professionals quietly judge. A top-1% skill does what works
**and refuses the cringe** — even when asked for it. When a user requests one of these, decline the
*approach* and offer the genuine alternative that performs better and protects credibility.

## What NOT to do

### Broetry
The gimmick of **one short dramatic fragment per line** to manufacture profundity:
> "I failed.
>
> Hard.
>
> But then…
>
> Everything changed."

Whitespace is good for *readability*; broetry abuses it for fake drama. Use paragraphs that help
people read, not a slot machine of one-liners.

### Engagement bait
"Agree? 👇" · "Comment YES if you agree" · "Repost if…" · "DM me 'GROWTH' for the guide" ·
"Type '1' for the link." LinkedIn explicitly down-ranks obvious engagement bait, and it reads as
desperate. Earn comments with a real question, not a command.

### Fake vulnerability / humble-brags
"I cried in my car. Then I closed the biggest deal of my life." Manufactured emotional stories
engineered for reach. People can smell it, and it erodes trust. Real vulnerability is fine;
performed vulnerability is the cringe.

### The fabricated parable
"I fired my best employee. Here's the heartwarming lesson." · "A homeless man taught me everything
about marketing." · "A 7-year-old gave me the best business advice I've ever heard." Made-up
morality tales. Don't invent them; tell something that actually happened.

### Growth-hacking junk
Follow-for-follow, engagement pods, mass-tagging 20 people, reposting your own post hourly,
"comment and I'll send you…". Short-term tricks that cost long-term credibility.

### Manufactured controversy / ragebait
Taking a deliberately inflammatory stance you don't hold, just to farm angry comments. It works
once and brands you as a troll.

## What works instead

- **A real story you actually lived** → the personal-story→lesson format, told honestly.
- **A genuine POV you'll defend** → the contrarian format, with real reasoning and acknowledged
  nuance.
- **Actual value** → a how-to, framework, or breakdown someone can use.
- **A real question** → one that invites people to share their experience, because you're curious.
- **Specifics and proof** → real numbers and outcomes beat vague inspiration.

## The test

Before posting, ask: **would I say this, this way, to a respected peer's face?** If the line would
make you cringe in person — the dramatic one-word paragraph, the "Agree? 👇", the tear-jerker you
invented — cut it. Substance and honesty age well on LinkedIn; engagement bait ages into
embarrassment.

## When the user asks for cringe

Don't just comply. Briefly explain why the bait approach underperforms and risks credibility, then
deliver a genuinely strong post that achieves the real goal (reach, comments, authority) the
honest way. That redirect *is* the skill doing its job.
