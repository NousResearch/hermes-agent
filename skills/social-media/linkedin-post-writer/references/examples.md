# Examples — LinkedIn posts that work

Worked posts formatted for dwell, with the "see more" hook and a comment-driven close — plus a
cringe version for contrast. Voice is the blunt indie-founder voice from earlier skills (short,
dry, no exclamation marks, concrete). Your output uses the loaded `voice.md` at a LinkedIn
register.

---

## Example 1 — Personal story → lesson (founder, personal profile)

> "Requested" is the most expensive word in product.
>
> *(…see more)*
>
> We spent six months building a feature customers asked for in every sales call.
>
> We deleted it last week. Nobody used it.
>
> The ask was real. The need wasn't. People will tell you what sounds good in a meeting and do
> something completely different in the product.
>
> What they actually used was a boring little export button we kept skipping. Shipped it in two
> days. It's now the most-used thing we've built.
>
> So we changed how we decide. We watch what people do, not what they say they'll do.
>
> Has a "most-requested" feature ever flopped on you — or am I the only one who learned this the
> hard way?

*Hook wins the "…see more"; whitespace for dwell; real story → transferable lesson; genuine
question close; any link would go in the first comment. On-voice, no exclamation marks, no
"excited to announce."*

## Example 2 — Contrarian / POV

> Posting every day on LinkedIn is hurting most people's reach.
>
> *(…see more)*
>
> Not because volume is bad — because daily forces you to post when you have nothing to say. And
> the algorithm notices a weak post faster than it rewards a frequent one.
>
> We dropped from daily to twice a week and reach went up. Fewer posts, each actually worth
> reading, more dwell per post.
>
> Consistency matters. But consistency you can sustain at quality beats a cadence that turns into
> filler.
>
> Where am I wrong — has daily actually worked for you?

*A real, defensible take (not manufactured controversy); reasoning + nuance; invites genuine
disagreement in the comments.*

## Example 3 — How-to (document-carousel post copy)

> 5 hooks that actually earn the "see more" tap. Stealable 👇
>
> *(…see more)*
>
> Most LinkedIn posts die on the first line. Here are 5 openings that don't — with why each works.
> (Full breakdown in the carousel.)
>
> Save it for your next post.
>
> Which of these do you already use — and which feels too bold for you?

*Short text post pulling people into the PDF (slides handed to `carousel-writer`); save-prompt +
a real question. High dwell/saves format.*

## Contrast — the cringe version (do NOT do this)

> I fired my best employee today.
>
> Best decision I ever made.
>
> Here's why. 👇
>
> (Comment "GROWTH" and I'll DM you the 7 lessons.)

*Broetry fragments, a fabricated-sounding parable, and a "comment GROWTH" engagement-bait CTA —
down-ranked by LinkedIn and judged by readers. If a user asks for this, redirect to a real story
told honestly with a genuine question.*

---

## What the good examples share

- A first line that **wins the "…see more"** within the cutoff.
- **Whitespace for readability**, not broetry drama.
- A **real** story / POV / value — nothing fabricated.
- A **genuine question** close that invites real comments.
- **On-voice**, LinkedIn register; links to comments; no engagement bait.
