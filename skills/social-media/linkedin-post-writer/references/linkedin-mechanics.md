# LinkedIn Mechanics

LinkedIn rewards different behavior than any other platform. Get these mechanics right and an
ordinary post travels; ignore them and a great post dies.

> Specs and algorithm behavior shift; treat numbers as approximate and re-verify quarterly. The
> principles are durable.

## The two signals that matter most

1. **Dwell time** — how long people spend on the post. LinkedIn reads a long read as a good post.
   So: write to be read all the way through, and format so people *can*.
2. **Early, meaningful comments** — especially in the first hour or so. Comments (real ones, not
   "Nice!") signal value and expand reach. The author replying to early comments compounds it.

Everything below serves these two signals.

## The "…see more" hook

On mobile, ~140 characters show before "…see more" (a bit more on desktop). That expand tap is the
**first dwell signal** — if the opening line doesn't earn it, nothing else is seen. Front-load the
hook (via `hook-writer`); never spend the first line on a greeting, "I'm excited to announce," or
context the reader doesn't have yet.

## Format for dwell (readability, not gimmickry)

Long posts work *only* if they're readable on a phone:

- Short sentences; **one-to-two-line paragraphs**; generous whitespace.
- A clear build — hook → story/argument → payoff → question.
- Scannable: a reader skimming should still get the through-line.

This is whitespace as genuine readability. It is **not** "broetry" — the gimmick of one dramatic
fragment per line for fake profundity (see `avoiding-cringe.md`). Use whitespace to help people
read, not to manufacture drama.

## Personal vs company

Posts from a **personal profile** (founder, employee) consistently out-reach and out-trust company
pages. People follow people. So:

- **Thought leadership, stories, POV → post from a person** (founder/employee). This is the
  reach-and-trust play.
- **Announcements, brand, hiring → the company page is fine**, but pair it with a personal post for
  reach (employee advocacy).

(This skill writes the post. The account-level system — cadence, commenting, golden hour — is
`linkedin-growth`; the page strategy and advocacy program are `linkedin-company-pages`.)

## The link problem

LinkedIn suppresses reach on posts with **external links in the body** (it wants people to stay
on-platform). Tactics:

- Put the link in the **first comment** and write "link in comments."
- Or post **link-free** and let people ask / DM.
- **Native content beats link posts** — text, document carousels, native video, polls.

## Native formats (use them)

- **Text post** — the default; story/POV/insight.
- **Document / PDF carousel** — a swipeable PDF; very high dwell + saves. A LinkedIn power format.
  (Write the post copy here; hand the slides to `carousel-writer`.)
- **Native video** — uploaded directly (not a YouTube link); captioned for sound-off.
- **Poll** — strong for quick engagement and a low-effort post.
- **Newsletter** — for consistent long-form.

## Mechanics checklist

- **Hashtags:** 3–5 relevant, at the end. Not the reach lever they once were.
- **Length:** up to ~3,000 chars; medium-to-long performs if it earns the read.
- **Mentions/tags:** tag people only when genuinely relevant (mass-tagging is spammy).
- **Timing:** professional audience skews weekday business hours (mid-morning often cited) — treat
  as *general guidance, not analytics* (WoopSocial has no analytics yet); don't present it as the
  user's own data.
- **Author reply move:** reply to early comments (and ask a question) to boost the post — schedule
  time to be present for the first hour.
