# LinkedIn Post Formats

Pick the structure that fits the idea, then write it in the brand voice at a LinkedIn register.
Each delivers the post's payoff and sets up a real comment. Rotate them so a feed doesn't become
one-note.

For each: when to use, the shape, and the close.

---

## 1. Personal story → lesson
**Use when:** you have a real experience with a professional takeaway. The highest-trust,
highest-dwell LinkedIn format — *when genuine*.
**Shape:** drop into the moment → the tension/turn → the lesson, generalized for the reader.
**Close:** "Has this happened to you?" / "What would you have done?"
**Warning:** must be a real story. Manufactured emotional parables are the cringe everyone sees
through (see `avoiding-cringe.md`).

## 2. Contrarian / POV
**Use when:** the brand has a defensible point of view (from brand-profile). Drives the best
comments.
**Shape:** state the unpopular position plainly → back it with reasoning/experience → acknowledge
the nuance.
**Close:** "Where am I wrong?" / "Curious if anyone's seen the opposite."
**Warning:** a *real* take, not manufactured controversy or ragebait.

## 3. How-to / listicle
**Use when:** you can teach something practical. High save value; great as a **document carousel**.
**Shape:** promise the outcome → numbered, concrete steps/points → a takeaway.
**Close:** "What would you add?" / "Which of these do you skip?"

## 4. Case study / result
**Use when:** you have a real result and can show the how. Authority + proof.
**Shape:** the result up front (the number/outcome) → brief context → what you actually did → the
transferable principle.
**Close:** "Happy to go deeper on any step — what's most useful?"
**Compliance:** only verifiable results; respect client confidentiality and the brand's proof
rules.

## 5. Observation / insight
**Use when:** you've noticed something true about your industry or work. Pure thought leadership.
**Shape:** the sharp observation → why it's happening / why it matters → the implication.
**Close:** "Are you seeing this too?"

## 6. "I did X — here's what happened"
**Use when:** an experiment, a change, a journey with a result. Curiosity + relatability.
**Shape:** the thing you tried → the honest outcome (including what didn't work) → the lesson.
**Close:** "Worth trying in your world?"

## 7. Document-carousel post
**Use when:** a how-to or framework that benefits from swiping. Highest dwell/saves.
**Shape:** a short, strong text post (hook + promise + one line of context) that pulls people into
the PDF; the slides carry the depth. Hand the slides to `carousel-writer`.
**Close:** "Save this for later — and tell me what's missing."

---

## The comment-driven close (every format)

LinkedIn reach is gated on early comments, so the close matters as much as the hook. End with a
**genuine question**:

- Relevant to the reader's actual work.
- Easy to answer but interesting enough to want to.
- Open-ended, not yes/no, and **never** "Agree? 👇" or "Comment YES."

The best questions invite people to share their own experience — that's what produces the
real, reach-expanding comments (and gives you something to reply to). Pair it with at most **one**
soft CTA.
