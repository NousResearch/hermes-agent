# Shot patterns, decision tables & worked examples

## The model decision table (verify — the line moves)
| Job | Model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Volume social shots, most work | Ray3.14 | native 1080p, ~4x faster, ~3x cheaper at 720p |
| Identity-locked character across shots | Ray3 | character reference (3.14 dropped it) |
| HDR/EXR film delivery, graded finals | Ray3 | 16-bit HDR, EXR export |
| Restyle real footage, keep performance | Ray3 Modify (V2V) | footage as directing input |
| Sound-native generation | not Luma | route to veo-3/kling (Luma = sound in post) |

## The shot-brief pattern
"[Subject with concrete detail] [action], in [setting]. [Lighting/time]. [Mood]. Camera: [move — slow push-in /
orbit / handheld]. Style: [cinematic reference]." One shot per brief. Draft it, pick the winner, Hi-Fi it.

## The sequence workflow (a 30-60s brand film)
1. Script + shot list first (short-form-video-script / scripting skills) — 4-8 shots.
2. Canonical reference set: brand-true stills (flux/Photon), character refs if any (Ray3).
3. Per shot: draft iterations → winner → Hi-Fi master; keyframe end/start frames to match cuts.
4. Assemble + pace in capcut; sound in post (ai-music-and-sound / licensed); captions.
5. Drift review side-by-side → human approval → WoopSocial.

## The credit-budget worksheet (before subscribing)
Shots needed/mo × ~3 attempts × per-shot rate (model + resolution + SDR/HDR) = credit need. Check the current
rate table (rates are model/resolution-specific and shift). HDR only for finals. Monthly credits expire —
schedule generation sessions; top-ups roll over; API wallet funded separately.

## Worked example A — "Blunt indie founder" (draft discipline)
"burned 4,000 credits my first week rendering everything at 1080p HDR like an amateur. new rule: every idea
drafts first. eight drafts cost less than one HDR master. picked the one where the steam actually behaved,
mastered that single shot, done. same output, a fifth of the spend. hdr is for the final cut, not the
brainstorm." — draft-first, HDR reserved, honest math.

## Worked example B — "Warm bookkeeping studio" (the still-first teaser)
"We didn't ask the video model to imagine our brand — we gave it our brand. One photo of our actual desk setup
(our green, our mug), image-to-video, a gentle push-in, morning light. Three drafts, one winner, mastered once.
Then we added a soft licensed piano track in the edit, because the clip comes out silent — that's normal, not
broken. One small step: start every video from a still you already love." — image-to-video, sound-in-post
expectation set, brand-true.

## Never
Render iterations in HDR · skip Draft Mode · promise a one-generation "film" · chain Extends past quality ·
ship a silent clip as finished · assume free/cheap tiers cover client work · forget Luma's marketing
license-back on your clips · use an unpermitted likeness or fake-event footage of real entities · treat one
review's tier table as fact · claim WoopSocial generates video.
