# Element Playbook

Every profile element has a **job**. "Fill it in" isn't optimization — making each element do its job
is. Here's each one, what it's for, and how to optimize it.

## Handle / username

- **Job:** be searchable, memorable, consistent. Ideally your name or brand; easy to spell and say.
- **Do:** keep it the **same across platforms** (recognition + cross-platform discovery). Avoid
  number/underscore soup. A niche keyword in the handle helps local/service accounts (e.g.,
  `@austinhomeexpert`).

## Name / display field (the most-wasted field)

- **Job:** this field is **searchable and indexed** (Instagram weighs it heavily in search; LinkedIn
  the headline). It's how people find you by *what you do*, not just your name.
- **Do:** put **name + a niche keyword** — "Jane Doe | SaaS Growth", "Austin Realtor | Jane Smith".
  Don't waste it on just "Jane ✨". Think like your audience: what would they *search*?
- Note the character limit is tight (see `platform-specs.md`) — lead with the keyword that matters.

## Profile picture

- **Job:** instant recognition at thumbnail size.
- **Do:** **a clear face for personal brands/creators** (faces build trust and convert), a clean
  **logo/mark for companies**. High contrast, recognizable small, **consistent across platforms**.
  No busy, low-res, or zoomed-out shots.

## Bio — the pitch

- **Job:** pass the 3-second test and earn the follow. Who you help · what you do · why follow · CTA.
- **The formula (adapt, keep it human):**
  1. **Who + what** — "I help [audience] [outcome]" or "[What you make] for [who]."
  2. **Proof / credibility** — a specific number, credential, or notable ("+300 projects," "ex-X,"
     "trusted by N teams"). Real only.
  3. **CTA + link prompt** — the next step ("Free playbook ↓", "DM 'GROW'", "Book a call ↓").
- **Do:** clarity first, personality second; **skimmable lines**; tight (respect the limit); keywords
  for search; emojis as sparing signposts, not clutter. Cut filler ("Welcome to my page!" wastes
  space). On-voice (`voice.md`).

## Link / link-in-bio — the conversion mechanism

- **Job:** turn the visit into an action.
- **Do:** **match the link to the current goal/CTA** (one clear destination beats a junk drawer). If
  you need several, use a tidy **link hub** that **leads with the priority action**, not 15 equal
  links. Update it per campaign. Make the bio say *why* to click (the result they get), not just
  "link in bio." Use a trackable link where possible (native insights, not WoopSocial analytics).

## CTA

- **Job:** tell the visitor the one next step. Don't make them guess.
- **Do:** pick **one** primary action aligned to the goal (follow / subscribe / shop / book /
  download / DM). Make it explicit in the bio and on the pinned content.

## Pinned / featured posts

- **Job:** control the first impression beyond the bio — your "best of."
- **Do:** pin posts that **introduce you**, **prove value**, or **convert** (a strong intro, a
  best-performer, a clear offer). On LinkedIn use **Featured**; on X/TikTok/IG, pin to the top.

## Highlights (Instagram) / categorized evergreen

- **Job:** answer "tell me more" without scrolling.
- **Do:** a few clear categories — **Start here / About**, **Proof / results**, **What I offer /
  Products**, **FAQ**. Clean, on-brand covers; ordered by priority.

## Banner / header (LinkedIn, X, YouTube, Facebook)

- **Job:** prime real estate at the top of the profile — most people waste it on a stock photo.
- **Do:** make it carry a **value prop, proof, or CTA** plus visual brand. Provide a **banner brief**
  (what it should say/show), and hand the visual to a designer or an image tool
  (`tools/integrations/ideogram.md` / `nano-banana.md`) — Ideogram is good for legible text on a
  banner.

## Category / contact & action buttons (business profiles)

- **Job:** enable the action.
- **Do:** set the right **category**, enable **contact/book/shop** buttons where the goal needs them.

## The deliverable

For each element, hand the user **ready-to-paste copy or a clear spec** — not advice to "improve your
bio," but the actual bio, name field, CTA, link choice, pinned plan, highlights plan, and banner
brief, each passing the 3-second test.
