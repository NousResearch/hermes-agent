# The Profile Funnel

The reframe that makes this skill work: **your profile is a conversion landing page, not
decoration.** Content does one job — earn reach and send a curious stranger to your profile. The
*profile* does the converting: stranger → follower → lead. Most accounts pour everything into content
and leave a vague, leaking profile — so the visits never turn into follows or clicks.

A useful diagnostic: **if a profile gets visits but few follows or link taps, it's usually the bio,
not the algorithm.**

## The 3-second test (clarity beats cleverness)

A visitor decides in seconds whether to follow. Above the fold (handle + name field + first bio line
+ picture) the profile must instantly answer three questions:

1. **WHO is this for / who do you help?**
2. **WHAT do you do / offer?**
3. **WHY should I follow / what do I get?**

If a stranger can't answer those at a glance, they leave. **Clarity beats cleverness every time** —
a witty-but-vague bio ("dream big ✨ | living my best life") converts worse than a plain, specific one
("I help solo founders grow on social without burning out. Free playbook ↓"). Be clear first;
personality second.

## The funnel — each element serves a stage

Optimize the profile as a four-stage funnel, and know which element does each job:

| Stage | The job | Elements that serve it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Discovered** | be findable in search | handle, **name field / headline (searchable keywords)**, category |
| **2. Clarity** | pass the 3-second test | name field, first bio line, profile picture |
| **3. Follow** | earn the follow | bio (who/what/why + proof), pinned posts, highlights |
| **4. Convert** | drive the action | link-in-bio, CTA, contact/action buttons |

A profile can fail at any stage: unfindable (no keywords), unclear (vague bio), unconvincing (no
proof/CTA), or a dead end (no/weak link). Diagnose which stage leaks and fix that first.

## "For everyone" converts no one

The instinct to appeal to everybody is the enemy of conversion. A specific profile lets the *right*
visitor instantly think "this is for me" and follow; a generic one gives no one a reason. Niche down
— the narrower and clearer the positioning (from `social-strategy` and `brand-profile`), the higher
the conversion. You're not trying to be followable by all; you're trying to be unmissable to your
people.

## Derive it from the foundation, don't invent it

The profile is positioning made concrete. Pull from:
- **`brand-profile` / `social-strategy`** — who you help, the wedge, the primary goal (which sets the
  CTA and link).
- **`audience.md`** — their language and the outcome they want (so the bio speaks to them).
- **`voice.md`** — the tone of the bio.

The profile isn't a creative-writing exercise; it's the clearest possible statement of who you are
and why someone should care — in the audience's words.

## Honest scope

This is **advisory**: it produces the spec (handle, name, bio, link, CTA, pinned/highlights, banner
brief). **WoopSocial publishes posts and media — it does not edit profile fields**, so the user
applies the spec in each app. And WoopSocial has no analytics surface, so judge results via **native
profile insights** (profile visits, link taps, follows) the user pulls, or the qualitative 3-second
test — never claimed performance data.
