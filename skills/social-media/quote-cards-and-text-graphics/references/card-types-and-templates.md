# Card types, checklists & worked examples

## Text-graphic types (pick the fit)
| Type | What it frames | Source it pulls from |
|---|---|---|
| Original-line card | the brand's own sticky line | hook-writer, contrarian, text-post-and-microblog |
| Attributed quote card | a real third-party quote (in context) | verified source (human confirms wording) |
| Stat card | one real, sourced number | data-and-original-research (a lone stat routes here) |
| Testimonial card | a real, consented customer quote | social-proof-and-testimonials (FTC rules) |
| Pull-quote / carousel set | a series of lines in one system | design-and-templates (consistent set) |

## The quotability test (before you design)
Does the line stand alone (no surrounding context needed)? · Would someone save/re-share it? · Is it short
enough to breathe? · Is it a claim/reframe/truth, not a mundane statement? · Is it TRUE and, if attributed,
verifiable? If it fails, sharpen the line — don't dress up a weak one.

## The legibility checklist (before you publish)
Main quote ≥ ~24pt ✓ · high contrast (overlay/panel over any photo) ✓ · two fonts max ✓ · key phrase leads ✓ ·
line breaks on meaning, not mid-word ✓ · safe margins ✓ · attribution present + correct ✓ · reads clearly at
thumb-size on a real phone ✓.

## Worked example A — "Blunt indie founder" (original-line card)
Line: "You don't need more followers. You need more trust." Spec: black card, one bold sans, "more trust" in the
accent color and largest weight, small "— [brand handle]" bottom-left, generous margins. One idea, outsized,
on-brand, thumb-legible. Attributed to the brand (safe + honest).

## Worked example B — "Warm bookkeeping studio" (stat card, sourced)
Line: big "96%" + "of our monthly-plan clients filed on time last year" + small "source: our 2025 client base,
n=180." Warm cream card, serif for the number, one accent. Real number, sourced on the card, no rounding games.
(If it had been unsourced, we'd get the source before rendering.)

## The line-break craft (the pro tell)
Where you break the lines changes how a quote reads. Break on the unit of meaning so each line is a coherent
phrase, land the strongest word at the end of a line (it gets the most weight), and never hyphenate or split
mid-word to fit. A well-broken three-line quote reads as designed; a badly-broken one reads as a screenshot.

## Never
Frame a line that isn't quotable · fabricate a quote · attribute an invented/fictional line to a real person ·
put words in a competitor's mouth · trim a quote to flip its meaning · reproduce copyrighted lyrics/poems · put a
tiny quote over a busy photo · fabricate a stat/source · claim WoopSocial generates the image (a design tool does).
