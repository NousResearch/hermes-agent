# The reality of quote cards & text graphics in 2026 (verify-quarterly)

Volatile figures — attribute, mark **verify-quarterly**. The craft principles (quotability first; one idea,
outsized; mobile legibility; honest attribution) are stable typography + integrity fundamentals.

## Why the format works (and its ceiling)
- A quote card packages **one idea** into a self-contained, save-worthy mini-billboard — the format over-indexes
  on **saves and re-shares** because people repost a thought they want to be seen holding.
- Marketing sources report quote/visual posts lift engagement meaningfully (e.g. "~150% vs plain text", "visuals
  ~94% more views") — treat these as **directional and unverified**; attribute, verify-quarterly. The reliable
  point: **the design amplifies a strong line; it can't create one.** A mundane sentence in a beautiful font is
  still mundane.

## Quotability (the whole game)
- **Not every sentence earns a card.** The test: does the line **stand alone** and make someone stop/save/share?
  Favor a bold claim, a reframe, a sticky truth — and **shorter is better** (more room to breathe; it shouldn't
  read like a paragraph).

## Typography & legibility (well-established craft)
- **Mobile-first:** ~80%+ of social consumption is mobile, so design for thumb-size — **min ~24pt** main quote
  text, **high contrast**, safe margins, and **not text over a busy image** (add an overlay/solid panel).
  **Test on an actual phone** before publishing.
- **Two fonts max** (one is often enough) — one for the quote, one for attribution/accent; more dilutes impact.
- **Hierarchy:** one line/key phrase leads (size/weight/contrast); **break lines where the meaning stays strong**,
  never mid-word to fit; whitespace makes it feel premium.
- **Consistency across a set** = brand recognition (route the system to `design-and-templates`).

## The honesty spine (stable — sharp for this format)
A quote card is an **attribution machine**, and quote cards *recontextualize* a line by design — which makes
integrity central:
- **Never fabricate a quote** or attribute an invented/fictional line to a real person (the rule against
  fictional quotes from real public figures). A misattributed quote card is **misinformation with nice
  typography.**
- **Never strip context** to change a quote's meaning; **verify the exact wording + source.**
- **Consent** for a person's words/face (real or AI lookalike); customer quotes follow the social-proof/FTC rules;
  a stat on a card must be **real + sourced**; don't reproduce **copyrighted** lyrics/poems.

## House metrics
Saves/shares (the format's superpower) + reach + profile visits — native only; never fabricate a quote, source,
or stat.
