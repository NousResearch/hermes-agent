# The QUOTE framework — text graphics people save (and that stay honest)

A quote card lives or dies on the line and its legibility — and, uniquely, on its honesty (it's an attribution
machine). Each letter is a gate; most cards fail at Q (a line that isn't quotable) or E (unreadable at thumb-size).

## Q — Quotable line first
Pick a line **worth framing.** The test: does it **stand alone** and make someone stop, save, or re-share? Favor
a bold claim, a reframe, a sticky truth — and keep it **short** (a quote card is not a paragraph; short lines have
room to breathe). Not every sentence earns a card. **The design amplifies a strong line; it can't create one** —
if nothing is quotable, sharpen the line before designing.

## U — Undistorted + attributed
The integrity gate:
- **Never fabricate** a quote or attribute an invented/fictional line to a real person (no fake Einstein/celebrity
  quotes; no words in a competitor's mouth — defamation risk).
- **Never strip context** to change the meaning (don't trim a critic's line into false praise).
- **Verify** the exact wording + source; **consent** for a person's words/face (real or AI lookalike); customer
  quotes follow social-proof/FTC rules; a stat must be **real + sourced**; don't reproduce **copyrighted**
  lyrics/poems. A card of the brand's **own** original line is always safe — attribute it to the brand.

## O — One idea, outsized
One idea per card, and the **key phrase leads** (size/weight/contrast). Break lines **where the meaning holds**,
never mid-word to fit. Shorter quote → bigger type → more impact. No wall of text; if it needs a paragraph, it's
a `text-post-and-microblog` or a carousel, not a card.

## T — Type on-brand
**Two fonts max** (one often enough) — one for the quote, one for attribution/accent. Use the **brand system**
(colors, type, logo) so a set is instantly recognizable; whitespace and consistent margins read premium. Route the
system + the render to `design-and-templates` / a design tool.

## E — Ensure legibility
**Mobile-first:** min ~24pt main text, **high contrast** (an overlay/solid panel over any photo), safe margins,
clean spacing — and **test at thumb-size on an actual phone.** A gorgeous card that's unreadable failed;
legibility **is** the deliverable. Then a design tool renders the final image; the human approves; **WoopSocial
publishes** it (it doesn't generate media). Make it saveable + citable (attribution visible).

## The three-second read test
Before it ships: glance at the card for one second, then look away — can you recall the line and read the
attribution? If not, it's too small, too long, or too low-contrast. A quote card that needs studying has already
lost the scroll; the whole point is that one idea lands instantly and cleanly.
