# Hooks & Retention on Reels

Watch-through and sends are the Reels ranking system's top signals — so the hook, the muted-first
retention design, and the "who forwards this?" question are where a Reel is won or lost.

## The first 3 seconds

The first ~3 seconds decide whether a Reel is pushed to non-followers or throttled. Fire on three
channels at once:

- **Verbal hook** — the first words spoken (or the voiceover's). No preamble. On Instagram this
  doubles as SEO: put the target keyword in the first spoken seconds (see `instagram-seo`).
- **On-screen text hook** — a bold text line stating the stakes or curiosity. On Reels this is the
  **primary** channel: most viewers are on mute, so the text hook has to work silently.
- **Visual hook** — the most striking frame first; motion, a result, a pattern interrupt.

Pull the hook mechanism from `hook-writer`. Banned openings: "Hey guys," a logo/intro card, a slow
establishing shot, "In this Reel I'm going to…". Start *in* the action or the claim.

## Hook angles that work on Reels

- **The bold claim / contrarian** — "Your most-requested feature is the one to delete."
- **The result / transformation** — show the after first, then earn it ("this took 14 days").
- **The open loop** — "I lost $40k learning this. Here's the 20-second version."
- **The callout** — "If you run a SaaS, stop doing this."
- **The relatable / POV** — the shared experience your audience forwards to a friend ("send this to
  your cofounder" energy, without saying it that lazily).
- **The save-bait tutorial** — "the exact settings, step by step" (saves + rewatches).

Whatever the angle, it must be **true** — an over-promising hook with an under-delivering payoff
creates a mid-video retention cliff the algorithm punishes, and it burns trust for the next Reel.

## Write the muted version first

Draft the Reel as **on-screen text only** and check it tells the whole story. Then add the spoken
track. If the muted cut doesn't hook and pay off on its own, fix that before polishing the audio.
Burned-in captions from word one (kept in the safe zone — `instagram-reels-publishing`).

## Retention design — keep them to the end

- **No intro tax.** Value starts at second 0. Cut everything before the hook.
- **Fast pace.** Tight cuts, no dead air; change the visual every ~2–4 seconds.
- **Open loops.** Pose the question early, answer it late ("the third one surprised me").
- **Re-hook mid-video** for anything past ~35 seconds.
- **Front-load value, hold one payoff** for the end to pull completion.

## Design for the rewatch / loop

- **Seamless loop** — end so the last line/frame flows back into the first; it replays without a
  seam and the viewer watches twice before noticing.
- **The rewatch trigger** — a fast detail, a dense list, or a "did you catch it?" beat that rewards
  a second watch.
- **Density** — pack enough that one watch isn't enough to absorb (listicles, settings, steps).

## The sends lever — script for the forward

Sends (DM shares) are the dominant discovery signal, and they can be *designed*:

- **Relatable** — "this is so us" content people tag a specific friend on.
- **Useful reference** — the checklist/settings/how-to someone forwards to the person who needs it.
- **Conversation-starting** — a defensible hot take people send to argue about.

The test for every script: **who sends this, to whom, and why?** If there's no concrete answer,
the idea is reach-capped no matter how good the hook is.

## Retention killers (cut these)

- A slow or branded intro; a logo sting.
- A hook that only works with sound on.
- Burying the point; dead air; filler.
- A hook the Reel doesn't pay off.
- Ten seconds of content stretched to sixty.

## The measurable check

A good Reels script can answer: what's the **3-second hook** (all three channels)? does the **muted
cut** carry the story? where's the **open loop**? what's the **loop/rewatch trigger**? **who sends
it to whom?** If the hook is weak or the sends answer is vague, fix those before anything else.
