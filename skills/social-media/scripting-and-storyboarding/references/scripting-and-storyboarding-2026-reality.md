# The reality of scripting & storyboarding in 2026 (verify-quarterly)

Tool specifics shift — attribute, **verify-quarterly**. The production craft (the AV script, the shot list,
the paper edit) is decades-stable broadcast/commercial practice; the AI layer is what moved.

## Why pre-production is the highest-ROI hour
The stable truth reviews keep restating: the storyboard/plan is "where you catch problems early, before they
become expensive fixes on set or in post" — validating visual intent early reduces avoidable reshoots and
late-stage churn (Boords, mStudio). The corollary for creators: chaotic shoots and endless edits are usually a
**pre-production deficit**, not a talent deficit.

## The stable craft (established practice — state as such)
- **The two-column AV script** (Audio | Visual) is the broadcast/commercial standard: every audio beat
  (VO/dialogue/SFX) is paired with a *written* visual (B-roll, punch-in, text pop, angle). A words-only script
  plans half the video — the improvised half defaults to a static talking face.
- **Runtime math:** spoken delivery ≈ **130–150 words/minute** (~75 words per 30s); pauses and B-roll moments
  cost seconds too. A timing pass with a stopwatch belongs in pre-production.
- **The storyboard is a decision document, not art** — its job is "what is on screen at every beat." A shot
  table or stick figures answer it; drawing skill is optional.
- **The shot list unlocks batching:** script everything first, then regroup shots **by setup** (location,
  framing, outfit, lighting) instead of by video — the mechanism behind "two weeks of content in one afternoon."

## The 2026 AI layer (attribute; verify-quarterly)
- **Script→board pipelines matured:** paste a script/brief and get scene-broken, character-consistent frames,
  animatics, and exported shot lists (Boords — free tier, no watermark, sign-off/client-approval layer;
  Storyboarder.ai — 250K+ creators, unlimited generations, animatics; LTX Studio, mStudio, Studiovity — 30+
  shot parameters + script→shot-list sync; free: Wonderunit Storyboarder). Character consistency has moved
  from feature to **baseline expectation**. Market sized ~$284M (2025) → ~$410M by 2032 (attribute).
- **The tested practitioner benchmark** (CrePal, Dec 2025, first-person): 12-frame explainer boards went from
  ~3 hours manual to **40–60 minutes** with the LLM-beats → visual-directives → seeded-frames → assemble
  workflow. The hard-won craft rules: tag beats **[VO]/[SFX]/[Action]** (clear tags reduce hallucination);
  **state camera intent explicitly** ("Close-up on hands" — *"the AI will guess, don't make it"*); lock tone +
  palette; keep a **3–5-line character bible + one reference image**; declare props/no-gos.
- **Honest ceiling:** AI boards are **directional, not Pixar-ready** — break complex scenes into micro-beats;
  label versions ("back to 3B").
- **The economics that explain the shift:** traditional storyboard illustration runs ~$50–300/frame
  ($5K–$30K for a feature/major commercial) — attribute.

## The generative-video twist
AI video sequences need **more** pre-production, not less: each storyboard panel maps to one generation's shot
brief (subject/action/setting/light/camera — luma's DREAM pattern), consistency comes from canonical reference
stills + the character bible, and **previz frames cost cents where video generations cost credits** — board
first, generate second.

## House metrics
Shoot-day efficiency (shots planned vs re-shot) + edit speed + the published video's retention (native, via
analytics-and-reporting) — never fabricated.
