# Why Social Search

The case for social SEO in 2026, and the mental model the rest of this skill builds on.

> Platform behavior changes — re-verify quarterly. The direction (platforms are search engines,
> keywords over hashtags, social content surfacing on Google + AI) is well established and
> strengthening.

## All social platforms are now search engines

Search no longer starts and ends with Google. A large and growing share of people — especially
younger audiences (roughly a third of millennials and nearly half of Gen Z) — **search inside social
apps first.** And they search *different* platforms for different intents:

- **TikTok** — products, recommendations, tutorials, local finds (a primary search engine for Gen Z).
- **YouTube** — how-to and depth (the world's second-largest search engine).
- **Pinterest** — visual inspiration and planning (a pure visual search engine).
- **Instagram** — products, aesthetics, lifestyle, local.
- **LinkedIn** — B2B insight, expertise, people.
- **X** — real-time/news; **Facebook** — local businesses, groups, communities.

If your content isn't optimized for the **search bar** (not just the feed), you're invisible to people
who are *actively looking for what you offer* — the highest-intent audience there is.

## The three layers of social SEO

1. **In-platform search** — ranking in the search results *inside* TikTok / IG / YouTube / Pinterest /
   LinkedIn. Levers: keywords in audio, on-screen text, captions, titles, descriptions, profile — plus
   engagement signals (watch time, saves, comments).
2. **Cross-platform (Google) search** — your social content increasingly surfaces in **Google** (Short
   Videos panels, image results, AI Overviews). Google indexes public social profiles and content;
   notably, **public Instagram professional accounts are now indexable**, so captions and alt text can
   drive cross-surface discovery. Optimized social assets work *outside* the app too.
3. **AI search (GEO)** — LLM/AI engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode) increasingly cite social
   content. **This is a related but separate discipline → hand off to `ai-search-optimization`.** This
   skill covers layers 1–2.

## The hidden multiplier: search optimization = recommendation optimization

The keywords that get you found in search are the **same signals the recommendation algorithm reads to
categorize your content** (TikTok's interest graph, IG's topic matching, LinkedIn's "topic DNA"). So
optimizing for search **also** improves how the feed/FYP/Explore matches you to the right people. One
effort, two payoffs. (This is why the growth skills route here.)

## Evergreen vs ephemeral — SEO turns posts into assets

A feed post flashes for ~48 hours and dies. A **search-optimized** post answering a real query keeps
getting discovered for **months or years** — and resurfaces every time someone searches that topic. SEO
is how you build a **compounding back-catalog** instead of a treadmill of disposable posts. It's the
difference between renting attention and owning a discoverable library.

## What this skill does (and its boundaries)

- **Owns:** in-platform + Google search discoverability — keyword research for social, what each
  platform indexes, the on-content technique, topical clusters, the profile as a search asset.
- **Routes to:** `hashtag-strategy` (hashtags are a *minor category signal*, not the lever),
  `caption-writer` (the caption craft this adds a keyword layer to), `profile-optimization` (the
  profile as a *conversion* funnel — this skill treats it as a *search* asset), the growth skills.
- **Hands off:** AI-search/LLM citation → `ai-search-optimization` (GEO).
- **Honest scope:** this is about **searchability**, not guaranteed #1 rankings (algorithms +
  competition decide), and it never fabricates search volumes (see `keyword-research-and-technique.md`).
