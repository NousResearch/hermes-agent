# Examples — strategies that make real choices

Abbreviated strategies across business types. The point of each: it **chooses** — names the
channels it will win on *and the ones it drops*, commits to one objective, and has a wedge. Each
is contrasted with the "be everywhere" non-strategy. (Illustrative.)

---

## 1. B2B SaaS — payroll tool for small-business owners
- **Objective:** leads (demo sign-ups) + authority. Metric: demo requests + DMs (manual).
- **Channels:** **LinkedIn** (primary, founder-led) + **X** (secondary). **Drop:** TikTok,
  Pinterest, Instagram, YouTube long-form.
- **Why:** the audience (owner-operators, and the practitioners who follow) is on LinkedIn/X; the
  brand's strength is written insight, not video; LinkedIn fits the leads/authority goal.
- **Wedge:** radical transparency + the "payroll without a specialist" POV — saying what bloated
  competitors won't.
- **Cadence:** LinkedIn 3×/week (founder), X ~daily, repurposed from the same atoms.
- *Non-strategy (avoid):* "Be on all 10 platforms and post daily." → spreads a small team thin,
  wins nowhere, and forces video the brand can't sustain.

## 2. Local — neighborhood coffee shop
- **Objective:** local awareness + community. Metric: saves, story replies, "saw it on IG"
  mentions (manual).
- **Channels:** **Instagram** (primary) + **Facebook** (secondary — older/local + Groups).
  **Drop:** LinkedIn, X, TikTok (unless the owner enjoys it).
- **Why:** locals are on IG/FB; the brand's strength is warm visual moments; goal is local reach,
  not B2B.
- **Wedge:** the human, neighborhood angle — the owner's real voice, regulars by name.
- **Cadence:** IG ~4×/week, FB cross-posted/adapted.

## 3. Ecommerce / DTC — sustainable sneakers
- **Objective:** awareness → sales. Metric: link clicks + sales (native/manual).
- **Channels:** **Instagram** + **TikTok** (primary — visual/video) + **Pinterest** (evergreen
  search traffic). **Drop:** LinkedIn.
- **Why:** the audience discovers products on IG/TikTok; Pinterest drives evergreen buying intent;
  the brand has strong visual content.
- **Wedge:** design-first sustainability — looks good first, eco is the bonus.
- **Cadence:** IG/TikTok several×/week (much repurposed), Pinterest steady evergreen pins.

## 4. Personal brand — indie maker
- **Objective:** audience growth + authority. Metric: followers *that engage* + email sign-ups.
- **Channels:** **X** (primary) + **LinkedIn** (secondary). **Drop:** the rest, at least in
  Phase 1.
- **Why:** builders live on X; the brand's strength is written, candid insight; one person's
  capacity suits one or two text-first channels.
- **Wedge:** build-in-public with *real numbers* — honesty as the moat.
- **Cadence:** X daily-ish, LinkedIn 2–3×/week repurposed.

---

## The pattern across all four

- Each names a **single primary objective** tied to the business.
- Each **picks 1–3 channels by fit** and **explicitly drops the rest** — that's the strategic act.
- Each channel choice matches the **audience** *and* the brand's **content strength**.
- Each has a **wedge** from its POV — a reason it wins, not just "post good content."
- Cadence is **sustainable** and leans on **repurposing** to cover multiple channels.

A strategy that doesn't drop anything isn't choosing — and a strategy that doesn't choose isn't a
strategy.
