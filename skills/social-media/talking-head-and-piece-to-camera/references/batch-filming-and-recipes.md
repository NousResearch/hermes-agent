# Batch days, setup recipes & camera-shy on-ramps

## The batch day (a month of talking heads in one afternoon)
Pull 4–8 scripts from `short-form-video-script` (slots from batch-content-plan / content-calendar), then:
1. **Prep (night before):** print/queue each script as a beat map (3–5 beats + verbatim first/last lines); stack
   3–4 tops by the tripod; charge the phone, clear storage (4K eats it).
2. **Setup (2 min):** eye-level phone, face the window, mic check with a 10s test clip — *listen to it on
   headphones* before rolling anything.
3. **Order:** hardest/highest-stakes script first; the fun one last as a reward.
4. **Per script:** say the hook first and fresh · retake per beat, keep rolling · three-strike rule on stubborn
   lines · cap ~10 min per short script.
5. **Between scripts:** swap top, 60s off-camera, water, reset to +20% energy; log the keeper take number.
6. **Stop at ~60–90 min** even mid-list — a dead-eyed take costs more than an empty slot.
7. **Hand off:** footage → captions-and-clipping / capcut / descript; publish via scheduling-and-queue →
   **WoopSocial**; read results natively / via analytics-and-reporting.

## Setup recipes (phone-first, no gear snobbery)
| Recipe | Setup | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Desk** (default) | Phone on tripod at eye level, window in front or beside, wired lav or quiet room | Overhead lights carving eye shadows; window behind you |
| **Walking** | Front camera handheld at arm's length, slightly above eye level, outdoors | Wind (cup the mic side, or wireless lav with a windscreen); traffic noise; don't read while walking |
| **Car** (parked) | Phone on a dash/vent mount, daylight through the windscreen | Engine off (audio hum); it reads casual — match the content to that register |
| **Standing/whiteboard** | Phone further back, landscape or 9:16 with headroom, lav essential at distance | Energy must go up with distance; check on-screen text stays in safe zones |

Upgrade path (only when a named limit is hit): wired lav → wireless lav (moving shots) → soft key light (rooms
with no usable window). That's the whole list; a new camera is last, not first.

## The camera-shy user (a valid requirement, not a flaw to fix)
Never shame someone onto camera. Offer three honest routes and let them choose:
- **Avatar route:** a disclosed synthetic presenter via `heygen` (creator/social lane) or `synthesia`
  (enterprise/L&D/localization lane). Honest trade-off: avatars scale but read polished-clinical — trust-led
  founder content genuinely works better with a real face, which is why those skills route it here. The hybrid
  is the pro move: film the short trust layer yourself, let the avatar carry the informational volume.
- **Faceless route:** screen-records, B-roll, photos + a voice track — their real voice, or `ai-voiceover`
  (disclosed). Plenty of large channels are faceless; it's a format, not a consolation prize.
- **The on-ramp** (for "I want to, but…"): voice-only first → hands/desk/over-the-shoulder shots → face on
  camera for a single 15s piece delivered to one imagined friend. Small, reversible steps; no deadline.

## The perfectionist (the other failure mode)
Symptoms: 30 takes, "just one more," a folder of unpublished footage. Prescription: **cap takes per beat at 3**,
pick the take where they sound like themselves (not the smoothest one), and ship it. Take quality peaks by ~2–3;
after that you're trading presence for polish, and the audience only pays for presence. A published good-enough
take gathers data; a perfect draft gathers dust.

## Worked example A — "Blunt indie founder" (desk recipe, batch of 5)
Sunday, 75 min: phone on books at eye level, window left, wired lav. Five 30s scripts as beat maps; hooks
delivered first-take-fresh; per-beat retakes with claps; top swapped each script; stopped after script 5 with
energy intact. Monday: footage to capcut, queue via scheduling-and-queue → WoopSocial. One afternoon, two weeks
of posts, zero new gear.

## Worked example B — "Camera-shy bookkeeping studio" (hybrid)
Owner won't film weekly explainers — fine. Route the 12-part FAQ series to synthesia (disclosed stock avatar,
locked scripts). Keep ONE real piece to camera: a 20s "why I started this" filmed on the on-ramp (voice memo →
desk shot → face), used on the profile and in social-proof-and-testimonials content. Trust layer human,
volume layer avatar, nobody shamed.

## Never
Judge a take you can't see, or fabricate "that looked natural" · prescribe a camera upgrade as the fix · shame a
camera-shy human onto camera · let a word-for-word prompter read ship when a beat-map riff was available · film
anyone else without consent · claim WoopSocial films, edits, or analyzes footage (publishing only) · sand a
take past the point where the human sounds like themselves.
