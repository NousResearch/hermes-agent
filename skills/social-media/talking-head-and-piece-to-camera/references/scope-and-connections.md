# Scope, distinctions & connections

## Honest scope (never violate)
- **The agent** coaches the setup (framing, lens height, light, audio, background, wardrobe), formats the script
  as a beat map or teleprompter text, writes shot lists, retake rules, and batch-day plans, and supplies a
  self-review checklist. That's the job.
- **The human** films, performs, and picks the take. The agent **cannot see the footage** — it never judges a
  take, never says "that looked natural," never fabricates a verdict on delivery it can't observe. When asked to
  review a take, it hands over the self-review checklist and lets the human decide.
- **WoopSocial publishes** the finished, edited file and that is all — it does **NOT** film, edit, caption,
  clip, or analyze footage. Read performance natively / via analytics-and-reporting.
- **Never** prescribe buying gear as the fix. Phone-first is the doctrine: recommend an upgrade only when the
  human names a limit the phone actually hit (e.g. low-light noise in an unfixable room), and name the cheapest
  fix first (usually a $15–25 wired lav or moving the desk to the window).
- **Consent:** anyone else appearing on camera (or audible) consents first — employees included. Filming in
  public: know the local norms; recognizable strangers in a monetized video is a risk, not a vibe.
- **Enhancement honesty:** AI touch-ups on a real human (descript Eye Contact, retouch, background replacement)
  are edits of a real performance — fine, but stay within platform disclosure rules for altered content, and
  never use them to fake a performance that didn't happen (e.g. syncing new words onto a face).
- **Never shame a camera-shy human onto camera.** "I don't want to film myself" is a valid product requirement —
  route to avatars or faceless formats without a lecture about authenticity.

## Distinct from its siblings (route correctly)
- **short-form-video-script** = writes the script (spoken + on-screen text + beats); **this** = delivers it to
  camera. They pair on almost every shoot.
- **scripting-and-storyboarding** = plans a multi-scene production (shot lists, storyboards); **this** = the
  shoot-day performance craft for the human in front of the lens.
- **heygen / synthesia** = synthetic presenters. Route there when the human can't or won't film, or when scale/
  localization beats presence. They route *back here* for trust-led founder content — the hybrid (human films
  the trust layer, avatar scales the informational layer) is the pro move.
- **captions-and-clipping / capcut / descript** = post-production (captions, cuts, clips). descript's Eye
  Contact AI patches a prompter read; it doesn't replace delivery.
- **livestream-and-realtime** = live piece to camera — no retakes, different craft.
- **ai-voiceover** = the voice without the face (one of the faceless routes).
- **hook-writer** = the hook line itself; this skill delivers it fresh, first, every session.

## Where this connects
- **Reads first:** brand-profile + voice-builder (the person's off-camera voice is the on-camera target).
- **Takes the script from:** short-form-video-script (short vertical) or youtube-long-form (long pieces); the
  shoot plan from scripting-and-storyboarding; batch slots from batch-content-plan + content-calendar.
- **Feeds:** captions-and-clipping / capcut / descript (the edit), opus-clip (clipping a long piece to camera
  into shorts), cross-platform-repurposing (one performance, many placements),
  social-proof-and-testimonials (founder-delivered trust content).
- **Routes away:** heygen / synthesia (avatar instead of filming), ai-voiceover (faceless voice track).
- **Publishes via:** the edited file → scheduling-and-queue → **WoopSocial.**
- **Measure with:** native + analytics-and-reporting on 3s hold / AVD / completion — never fabricated.
