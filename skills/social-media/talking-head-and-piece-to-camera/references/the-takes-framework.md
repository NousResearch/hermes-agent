# The TAKES framework — delivery engineered for presence

A talking head lives or dies on **presence** — does the viewer believe a real person is talking to *them*? Each
letter is a gate; most first-timers fail at A (reading word-for-word) or E (sanding a take lifeless).

## T — Tape the setup
Two minutes, once per session — then forget the gear exists.
- **Phone at eye level** (books under a $20 tripod), arm's-length-plus away, lens end at the top.
- **Face the biggest window**; never sit with it behind you. After dark, one soft lamp behind the phone.
- **Audio first:** quiet room, phone/mic within ~60cm, or a $15–25 wired lav. Clap at the top of each take.
- **Frame:** 9:16, eyes in the top third, hands in frame when they gesture, caption-safe zones clear,
  clean-but-real background with depth. Shoot 4K for punch-in room.

## A — Anchor the map, not the lines
Memorization strategy decides whether the eyes look alive.
- **Memorize the map:** 3–5 beats on a sticky note by the lens, plus the **first line and last line verbatim.**
  Riff the middle in your own words — a slightly imperfect riff reads as human; a perfect read reads as read.
- **Teleprompter, only if unavoidable** (dense/legal/localized copy): text in a narrow column tight to the
  camera dot, big type, slow scroll, rehearse the script twice *before* rolling so it's a reminder, not a
  revelation. Or the **line-at-a-time method:** read a line silently, look up, say it to the lens, cut, repeat —
  jump-cuts are a native style now, not a flaw.
- If the words won't stick, the words are wrong — send the script back to `short-form-video-script` to be made
  more spoken.

## K — Kick the first 3 seconds
The script won the first 3 seconds on paper; don't lose them on the day.
- **Start already talking.** No settle-breath, no chair adjust, no "okay so." Roll, wait a beat, then hit the
  hook mid-energy — the edit trims to the first strong word.
- **Say the hook first and fresh** every session, while energy is highest — never leave it for the tired end.
- **Deliver to ONE person.** Picture a specific friend/customer behind the lens; "hey everyone" energy is how
  presence dies. Smile just before speaking — it warms the voice even if the smile isn't in the take.
- **Run at +20%.** Camera flattens energy; what feels slightly too much lands as normal. Gesture; a talking head
  with dead hands is a portrait.

## E — Embrace the retake rules
Retakes are a system, not a mood.
- **Retake per beat, not per video.** Flub a line → keep rolling, pause, say the line again. Jump-cut later.
  Whole-video retakes reset your energy to zero and peak by take 2–3 anyway.
- **The three-strike rule:** a line that fails three times is a *writing* problem, not a performance problem —
  rewrite it in your own words on the spot or send it back to the script.
- **The good-enough doctrine:** ship the take where you sound like yourself. Take 9 is smoother and deader than
  take 2. Presence beats polish; the audience rewards a human, not a newsreader. (For the perfectionist spiral:
  cap takes per beat at 3, then move on — the edit hides more than you think.)
- **Self-review checklist** (since the agent can't see footage): hook lands in the first 2s? audio clean? eyes
  on the lens? did I sound like me? would *I* keep watching on mute? Human verdict is final.

## S — Stack the batch
One setup amortized across a month of content.
- **4–8 short scripts per session**, printed/queued as beat maps before you roll. Hardest or most important
  script first, while energy is highest. Stop at ~60–90 minutes — dead-eyed takes aren't worth the slot.
- **Swap tops between scripts** (keep a small stack next to the tripod) so posts don't look same-day.
- Between scripts: 60 seconds off-camera, water, reset to +20%. Log which take was the keeper while you still
  remember.
- Then hand the footage to `captions-and-clipping` / `capcut` / `descript` for the edit, and publish via
  `scheduling-and-queue` → **WoopSocial.**
