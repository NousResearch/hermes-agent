# The reality of text posts in 2026 (verify-quarterly)

Volatile figures — attribute, mark **verify-quarterly**. The principles (the first line is most of the job; white
space + restraint win; structure beats length) are stable; the exact numbers/limits move.

## The fold is everything
- **The "see more" cutoff is a hard wall.** On LinkedIn it lands at roughly **~210 chars on desktop / ~140 on
  mobile** (design for mobile); everything above it is the entire pitch, everything below needs a deliberate tap.
  Treat the first ~2-3 lines as a **mini-post whose only job is to earn the click** — "if those first sentences
  don't compel the tap, the rest of your post doesn't exist." (attribute; verify-quarterly)
- **Lead with the insight**, not "I'm excited to announce…", a greeting, or a bland question. Front-load the
  strongest, most specific line.

## White space + the 2026 over-formatting tell
- **White space is a feature**, not wasted space — short 1-2 sentence paragraphs with line breaks reportedly lift
  reading time by up to ~20% (ConnectSafely; attribute). Walls of text don't get read. "Space = oxygen."
- **OVER-formatting now backfires.** The biggest 2026 mistake isn't under-formatting — it's the
  Unicode-bold-everywhere, emoji/checkmark-per-line, rigid "Problem/Solution/Takeaway" look, which reads as
  **AI-generated** and gets scrolled past. Restraint (white space + a strong first line + at most one emphasis)
  beats decoration. "Formatting should make a genuine thought easier to read, not paper over a thin one."
- Unicode styling isn't searchable and **breaks screen readers** — keep key terms/hashtags plain (accessibility).

## Length: match content + platform (structure does the work)
- **LinkedIn:** 3,000-char cap (held since June 2023); the engagement sweet spot is ~**1,300-1,900** for
  thought-leadership/storytelling, **under ~500** for hot takes; posts over ~2,000-2,500 see a reported ~35%
  engagement drop. **X** favors brevity; **Threads** ~500 chars; **Bluesky** ~300. (attribute; verify-quarterly)
- A reader experiences "a hook, a turn, a payoff, a CTA" — **not** "1,600 characters." Structure, not length,
  does the work.

## Engagement (comments/saves/dwell beat likes)
- Comments + dwell time are the heaviest distribution signals; a 1,400-char post with real saves + substantive
  comments beats an 1,800-char post with 50 likes and three "🔥" replies.
- **The CTA must be a genuine, specific invitation** ("What am I missing?") — "comment YES / like & share" is
  engagement-bait, now suppressed. **Native beats external links** (put links in comments on LinkedIn).
- **Write to one specific reader** — a post for everyone resonates with no one.

## House metrics
Saves/comments/dwell + profile visits/follows + clicks — read natively; never fabricate a number or stuff bait.
