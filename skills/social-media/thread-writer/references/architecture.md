# Thread Architecture

The shape of a thread and the structures that fit different ideas. Get the architecture right and
the writing is easy; skip it and you get a chopped article with no flow.

## The shape

Every thread is three parts:

**Opener (post 1) → Body (the build) → Close (payoff + CTA).**

- **Opener** — hooks *and* promises the payoff. The open loop that makes someone read all the way
  down. Also the most-reshared post, so reach depends on it. (Craft in `post-craft.md`.)
- **Body** — the substance, one beat per post, maintaining momentum.
- **Close** — delivers/summarizes the payoff, then one CTA.

## The spine — outline before you write

Before writing any post, outline the **spine**: the opener's promise, then the ordered sequence of
posts that deliver it. The spine is the through-line that makes a thread feel cohesive rather than
a pile of related tweets. If you can't state the spine in one sentence ("the opener promises X,
then posts 2–7 walk through the steps, the close lands the lesson"), the idea isn't ready.

## One idea per thread, one idea per post

- **Per thread:** the whole thread makes *one* point or tells *one* story. Two ideas = two
  threads.
- **Per post:** each post advances the spine by exactly one beat — one step, one lesson, one scene.
  A post juggling three points loses the reader.

## Thread structures — pick the one that fits

- **Listicle** — "N lessons / tips / ways." Opener promises the list; each post = one item; close
  recaps. Easiest to follow, high save value. Great default for teaching.
- **Story / narrative** — a sequence of events with tension. Opener drops into the story; each post
  is a beat; the payoff is the resolution or lesson. Highest emotional pull.
- **Argument / contrarian** — a claim defended across posts. Opener states the take; the body
  builds the case point by point; the close drives it home. Drives replies.
- **How-to / process** — steps to a result. Opener promises the outcome; each post is one step; the
  close shows the finished result.
- **Breakdown / case study** — deconstruct something (a launch, a viral post, a result). Opener
  names what you'll dissect; each post is one component; the close extracts the takeaway.

Match the structure to the idea — a process is a how-to thread, a journey is a story thread. Don't
force a list onto a story.

## The re-hook — keep momentum down the thread

Attention leaks at every post boundary. Counter it:

- **Numbered progress** ("3/") orients the reader and signals there's more.
- **Mini-hooks / forward pulls** — end a post on a slight lean-forward where natural ("but that's
  not the part that surprised us").
- **Transitions** that connect beats rather than restarting each time.

Don't, however, withhold value for cheap cliffhangers — that frustrates. Each post should be
valuable *and* pull forward.

## Length — as many posts as the idea needs, no filler

Usually 5–12 posts. The rule isn't a number — it's that **every post earns its place**. If a post
doesn't add a distinct beat, cut it. A tight 6-post thread beats a padded 15-post one. Length
follows the idea, not a target.
