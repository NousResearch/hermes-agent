# Examples — full worked threads

Two complete threads showing architecture + post craft, in the blunt indie-founder voice from the
earlier skills (short, dry, no exclamation marks, concrete). Plus a "should've been one post"
contrast. Your output uses the loaded `voice.md`.

---

## Example 1 — Listicle thread (X) · goal: saves + follows

**Spine:** opener promises 5 lessons from deleting a feature → one lesson per post → close recaps +
RT mechanic.

**1/ (opener — hook + promise)**
> We spent 6 months building our most-requested feature. We deleted it this week. Nobody used it.
> 5 lessons, so you don't burn 6 months too 🧵

**2/**
> "Requested" is not "used."
> People told us they wanted it in every sales call. Then nobody touched it. Watch what users do,
> not what they say.

**3/**
> The boring feature wins.
> The thing we kept skipping — a dull little export button — shipped in 2 days and is now our most-
> used feature. Build the boring thing people already do by hand.

**4/**
> Sales calls are not a roadmap.
> Prospects describe the feature that would close *them*. Build for the words and you ship things
> nobody opens.

**5/**
> Shipping less is a feature.
> Every feature is forever: docs, bugs, support, UI clutter. Deleting the dead one made the whole
> product better.

**6/**
> The meta-lesson: we were building to feel productive, not to be useful.
> Now we watch behavior first, build second.

**7/ (close + CTA)**
> That's it — 5 lessons from torching 6 months of work.
> If this was useful, RT the first post so the next founder sees it 👇
> (I write about building small software in public — follow for more.)

*Notice: opener hooks + promises and stands alone; one lesson per post; each front-loads its point
and pulls forward; the close lands and asks for the reshare.*

## Example 2 — Story thread (X) · goal: reach + connection

**Spine:** opener drops into near-failure → beats of the 30 days → the turn → the lesson.

**1/**
> Two years ago we had 6 weeks of runway and no idea why churn was killing us. Here's the 30 days
> that saved the company 👇

**2/**
> First we did the thing founders avoid: we called 20 customers who'd left. Terrifying. Also the
> most useful week of the year.

**3/**
> The pattern was brutal and obvious. They didn't churn because of price or features. They churned
> because they never got the first win. Onboarding was the leak.

**4/**
> So we threw out the feature roadmap for a month and rebuilt one thing: the first 10 minutes.

**5/ (close)**
> Churn halved in 6 weeks. Same product, different first impression.
> The lesson we almost died learning: people don't leave bad products, they leave products they
> never figured out.
> If you're pre-churn-panic, fix the first 10 minutes.

## Contrast — this should've been ONE post

> "Unpopular opinion: posting daily is overrated. Consistency you can sustain beats volume you
> can't. 3 great posts a week > 14 mediocre ones."

That's a single sharp take with no sub-beats. As a "thread" it'd be padded filler. **Leave it as
one post.** Knowing this is half the skill.

## What the examples show

- The **opener hooks + promises** and works alone.
- **One idea per thread, one beat per post.**
- Every post **front-loads** and **pulls forward**; no filler.
- The **close lands the payoff** and makes **one** CTA (the reshare mechanic on X).
- Some ideas **shouldn't be threads** — and the skill says so.
