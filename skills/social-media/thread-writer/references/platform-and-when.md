# Platform Mechanics & When Not to Thread

The mechanics differ between X and Threads, and — just as important — not every idea should be a
thread at all.

> Specs change; treat numbers as approximate and re-verify. The principles are durable.

## X / Twitter

- **Limit:** 280 characters per post (free); much longer with Premium, but short posts travel
  further and read better in a thread. Keep posts punchy.
- **Thread culture:** strong. Threads are a native, expected format; numbering ("1/", "🧵") is
  common and helps orientation.
- **Reshare-the-opener mechanic:** the key growth move — the close asks people to RT post 1, which
  drives new readers to the top. X-native and powerful.
- **Links:** fine, but many believe in-thread links dampen reach — a common tactic is to put the
  link in a **reply** to the thread, or in the final post. Lead with value, link last.
- **Hashtags:** minimal (0–2) or none; more reads as spam.

## Threads (Meta)

- **Limit:** ~500 characters per post — roomier than X.
- **Culture:** more casual and conversational; less "thread culture" than X (single posts and
  short chains are common). Threads still work, but lean more relaxed and less numbered.
- **Tags:** one topic tag per post, not multi-hashtag.
- **Links:** clickable and fine.
- **Adapt, don't clone:** a strong X thread can move to Threads, but loosen the register, drop
  heavy numbering, and consider whether a couple of conversational posts serve better than a long
  formal thread.

## When NOT to thread (this is half the skill)

A thread is the right format only when the idea genuinely needs **multiple beats**. Force one and
it underperforms a single good post. Don't thread when:

- **The point fits one post.** A single sharp insight is stronger as one punchy post than padded
  into five. One idea, no sub-parts → single post.
- **You're padding to hit a number.** If posts 4–8 are filler to "make it a thread," it shouldn't
  be one.
- **There's no real payoff.** A thread promises a destination; if there's no lesson, list, story,
  or result at the end, sharpen the idea before threading.
- **It's a hot take or a one-liner.** Those live and die as single posts; a thread dilutes them.

Signs it *should* be a thread: a list, a step-by-step, a story with beats, an argument that needs
building, or a breakdown with parts. If you can name the beats, it's a thread. If you can't, it's
a post.

When unsure, default to the **single strong post** and offer the thread as an option — restraint
reads as confidence and respects the reader's time.
