# The Growth Engine

How TikTok growth actually works in 2026 — the model the rest of this skill builds on. It's
meaningfully different from Instagram, so don't carry over IG assumptions.

> The US algorithm is being retrained on US user data through ~mid-2026 (ownership transition), so
> expect distribution fluctuations — focus on fundamentals (watch time, completion, shares), which
> carry over regardless. Re-verify specifics quarterly.

## TikTok is an interest graph, not a social graph

The defining fact: **follower count is not a ranking factor.** TikTok evaluates **every video
independently** and tests it on a cold audience. A zero-follower account can hit millions; a big
account can flop. **~70–80% of the For You feed is content from accounts the viewer doesn't follow.**

The consequence: **you don't grow by accumulating followers for reach** (TikTok gives reach to
non-followers freely). You grow by **winning the FYP test repeatedly** with content people watch to
the end, and by **owning search**. Followers are the *business* asset (LIVE, Shop, owned audience),
not the reach mechanism (see `features-and-diagnosis.md`).

## The test-and-expand model (the "at-bats" engine)

Every video runs a lottery in phases:

- **Phase 1 — the test (~100–500 viewers, first 30–90 min).** A mix of your followers and
  interest-matched non-followers. **Watch-time/completion %** is the key metric here. Most videos
  live or die in this window — which is why the **first ~1 second** matters more than anything.
- **Phase 2 — expansion (~1K–50K, progressively less targeted).** Can it hold a broader, colder
  audience that has no context for you? Most "good" videos plateau here.
- **Phase 3 — breakout (100K+).** Only a small share reach this.

Two implications: (1) each post is a **cheap at-bat** — more quality at-bats = more chances to hit;
(2) **videos can resurface days or weeks later** if engagement picks up, so the back-catalog keeps
working (TikTok isn't chronological). Note: a warm, **engaged following helps early signals** in the
Phase-1 window, so retention still matters — but follower *count* itself isn't the lever.

## The signals (2026), strongest first

1. **Completion rate** — #1. The bar has risen: aim for **~70%+ completion** to have a real shot at
   going wide (up from ~50% in 2024). Shorter, tight videos with high completion beat longer ones
   that lose people.
2. **Rewatch / loop rate** — now outranks follower count; one of the strongest distribution signals.
   Design a loop or a second-watch reward.
3. **Shares + saves + meaningful comments** — strong "this triggered action" signals. Comment
   *quality* beats count.
4. **Follows-from-video, profile visits** — sustained-interest signals.
5. **Likes** — the **weakest** signal. Don't optimize for likes.

Metadata (captions, keywords, hashtags) mostly helps **search** and clustering, not the initial FYP
test (see the search pillar in `completion-search-volume.md`).

## The growth loop

```
consistent niche content (sustainable at-bats)
   → Phase-1 test: a 1-sec hook + ~70%+ completion + early shares/comments
   → Phase-2 expansion: holds a colder audience (rewatch/loop)
   → breakout: cold viewers watch, rewatch, share
   → profile visit → binge the back-catalog (Series helps) → follow
   → fans (LIVE / Shop / community) + an engaged following that warms early signals
PLUS a parallel evergreen lane: SEARCH (keywords) → discovery independent of the FYP
```

Every growth problem is a **specific broken stage or phase** in this loop — diagnose it before
prescribing (see `features-and-diagnosis.md`). Don't apply random tips.

## Native, not polished

TikTok rewards **TikTok-first, authentic** content over polished ads (audiences prefer a
less-polished aesthetic; "make TikToks, not ads" — see `tiktok-script`). Low-effort content gets
detected and buried, so "volume" means **consistent quality at-bats**, not spam.
