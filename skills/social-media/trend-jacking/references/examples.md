# Examples — trend-jacks, good and refused

Worked examples showing the three gates in action and the remix — including the refusals, which are
half the value of this skill. Voice context is the blunt indie-founder voice from earlier skills.

---

## Example 1 — A good trend-jack (format, fits, fast)

**Trend:** a rising "expectation vs reality" format (trending sound + split structure).
**Gates:** FIT ✓ (the founder's SaaS has a perfect "what customers ask for vs what they use" angle)
· SAFETY ✓ (light, harmless format) · TIMING ✓ (sound still rising).
**Remix:** *expectation* = "the feature everyone begged us to build" / *reality* = "the boring
export button they actually use." The trend's structure, the brand's specific truth.
**Execution:** hand to `tiktok-script` (1-second hook, the trending sound, native/handheld), caption
to `caption-writer`, schedule via `scheduling-and-queue`. Shipped same day while the sound was hot.

→ Works because the fit was real and the angle was theirs — it could only be *this* brand's video.

---

## Example 2 — Refused: trend-jacking a tragedy

**Ask:** "A natural disaster is trending — tie our product to it for reach."
**Response:** Decline. You don't market on top of human suffering — it reads as exploitation and the
backlash is severe and deserved. SAFETY gate: hard no. If the brand genuinely wants to help, that's
a real, non-promotional action (a donation, sharing verified relief info) with no product tie-in and
no reach motive — or simply staying out of it. Not a trend-jack.

---

## Example 3 — Redirected: a forced, irrelevant trend

**Ask:** "Everyone's doing this teen dance — put our B2B accounting product on it."
**Response:** FIT gate fails — there's no authentic connection, and your audience would find it
confusing, not charming. Skip it. If you want the reach of a trend, let's find a **format** trend
that actually fits accounting (a "things clients say vs what they mean" format, a relatable
busy-season bit) where you can add a real angle.

---

## Example 4 — Flagged: a dead trend

**Ask:** "Let's do that trend with the sound from a few months ago."
**Response:** TIMING gate — that one's likely saturated or over; doing it now reads as late. Quick
check: is it still active in your niche's feed? If it's clearly past peak, skip it and grab a
currently-rising sound/format instead. (And remember: I can't see live trends — check your FYP /
the platform's trend tab to confirm what's actually hot right now.)

---

## Example 5 — Cautioned: a divisive moment

**Ask:** "This political controversy is trending — let's post a hot take for attention."
**Response:** Strong caution / decline. Riding a divisive moment to farm reach is a brand-safety
landmine — you alienate half the audience and look opportunistic. If the issue is genuinely core to
the brand's values and you'd stand by it regardless of reach, that's a *values* statement (different
skill, different intent), not a trend-jack. For reach, pick a safe, on-brand trend instead.

---

## What these share

- **The gates run first** — fit and safety before timing, before any drafting.
- **Refusals are clean and explained** — with a safer alternative where one exists.
- **Good trend-jacks add the brand's angle** in the brand's voice — never a logo-slap copy.
- **Honesty about live trends** — the skill vets/executes; discovery needs the user's live sources.
