# The Three Gates

Trend-jacking works only when a trend clears **all three** gates — FIT, SAFETY, and TIMING. Most
brand trend-jacking fails because someone skipped a gate: they forced an irrelevant trend (fit),
touched a sensitive moment (safety), or arrived late (timing). Run every trend through all three, in
this order, before any execution. **Any single fail = don't do it.**

The order matters: check FIT and SAFETY before TIMING, because a fast no is better than a fast
mistake. Speed never overrides fit or safety.

---

## Gate 1 — FIT (is this *your* trend?)

The question: **can you add a genuine angle, or are you just slapping your logo on someone else's
moment?**

Pass if:
- There's an **authentic connection** to your brand, product, niche, or audience.
- You can bring a **real perspective** — your take, your world, your value — not just participation.
- Your **audience would actually enjoy** you doing it (it fits how they see you).

Fail (skip it) if:
- The only reason to do it is "it's popular."
- You'd have to contort the trend to make it relate.
- It clashes with your brand identity or would confuse your audience.

**Not every trend is your trend.** A B2B accounting tool doesn't need to do a teen dance. The best
trend-jacks feel inevitable in hindsight because the fit was real.

---

## Gate 2 — SAFETY (should anyone touch this?)

The question: **would a brand inserting itself here look opportunistic, tone-deaf, or
exploitative?** This is the gate that protects the brand from getting dragged.

**Hard no — do not trend-jack:**
- **Tragedies, disasters, deaths, violence, crises, illness.** Marketing on top of human suffering
  reads as exploitation, full stop. (A genuine, non-promotional response — real help, a donation,
  staying silent — is different from a trend-jack.)
- **Politically or socially divisive moments** ridden for attention. Inserting a brand to farm reach
  off controversy is a brand-safety landmine.
- **Anything mocking or punching down** at a group, or built on a stereotype.
- **Sensitive cultural/religious moments** treated flippantly.
- A trend whose **origin is harmful/misleading** (misinformation, a harmful challenge).

**Proceed with caution (judgment call):**
- Edgy humor — fine if it fits the brand and punches up/sideways, risky otherwise.
- A trend with murky origins — verify before joining.

When in doubt, **sit it out.** The reach from a risky trend is never worth the reputational damage,
and "we were just joining the trend" is not a defense anyone accepts.

---

## Gate 3 — TIMING (is the window open?)

Trends have a lifecycle: **emerging → rising → peak → saturated → dead.** The window to ride one is
on the way **up**.

- **Emerging/rising → GO.** Early is where the reach is; you look in-the-know.
- **Peak → maybe**, if you can ship *now* and add something fresh.
- **Saturated/dead → skip.** Jumping on a played-out trend reads as late and out-of-touch — worse
  than not doing it.

Trends decay fast (a sound can be over in days). So once FIT and SAFETY pass, **speed matters** —
but speed is the *third* gate, not the first. A fast post on a trend that fails fit or safety is just
a fast problem.

---

## The one-line test

Before executing, you should be able to say: **"This trend genuinely fits us (fit), it's safe and
non-exploitative to touch (safety), and it's still on the way up (timing)."** If you can't say all
three, don't post it.
