# Audio & Camera — the superpower

Veo's defining advantage is **native synchronized audio**, and its cinematic power comes from
**camera/motion direction.** Prompt both deliberately.

> Re-verify quarterly. Audio and motion are strong but **experimental and improving** — always review
> the output.

## Native audio (the differentiator)

Veo generates **video and audio in a single pass** — dialogue with lip-sync, sound effects, ambient
sound, and music — temporally matched to the action. This is what sets it apart. How to prompt it:

- **Dialogue:** put the **exact line in quotes**, and say who speaks and how. e.g. *…the chef looks to
  camera and says, "Here's the only step people skip," in a calm, confident tone.* Keep lines short for
  reliable lip-sync.
- **Sound effects:** name them and when ("the pan sizzles as the steak hits"; "a soft click").
- **Ambient:** set the soundscape ("quiet café murmur, distant traffic").
- **Music:** describe mood/style if you want underscore ("soft jazz in the background").
- **An explicit audio cue in the prompt meaningfully improves** the native audio — don't leave it to
  chance; describe what should be heard.

**Honest handling of audio:**
- Treat generated audio as a **draft / guide track** for branded work — for the final, many teams
  **record real voiceover, license music, and mix** to spec, using Veo's audio as timing reference.
- **Verify** the audio (wrong words, off lip-sync, odd artifacts happen). A generation can be **blocked
  by audio safety filters** (you aren't charged when blocked) — rephrase if so.
- Generated **voices/likenesses** must not imitate real, identifiable people (see safety in the SKILL).

## Camera & motion (the cinematic layer)

Direct the camera like a filmmaker — this is most of the "cinematic" feel:

- **Shot type:** wide / establishing / medium / close-up / macro / POV / over-the-shoulder.
- **Movement:** static / slow push-in (dolly) / pull-back / pan / tilt / tracking / crane / 180° arc /
  handheld.
- **Angle & lens:** low/high angle, eye-level; "shallow depth of field," "wide lens," "lens flare."
- **Motion of the subject:** describe it physically ("steam rising," "fabric billowing in the wind") —
  Veo's physics handle realistic movement when you describe it concretely.
- **One clear motion per clip.** In ~8 seconds, a single, well-described camera/subject move reads far
  better than three competing ones.

## The honest caveats

- **Artifacts happen** — small faces, hands, fine textures, complex physics can glitch; **review every
  clip** before publishing.
- **Vertical framing** — 9:16 is native, but wide/group scenes can feel cramped; **explicitly describe
  vertical framing** ("subject centered, vertical composition") for mobile.
- **Consistency across clips** can drift — use reference images/first-last frame
  (`inputs-length-and-recipes.md`) and review the set.
