# Examples — teardowns in practice

Worked teardowns showing the framework, the counterfactual, the replicability check, and the
applied principle — including a non-replicable case. Voice context is the blunt indie-founder voice.

---

## Example 1 — A replicable teardown ("I quit my job" founder video, 4M views)

**Layers:**
- Hook (0–2s): "I quit a $200k job to build something nobody asked for." — bold, high-stakes.
- Emotional/share driver: **identity + inspiration** (viewers project their own escape fantasy).
- Structure: open loop ("here's what happened") → honest arc → payoff.
- Format: talking head, handheld, native.
- Topic/angle: relatable founder leap.
- Share-trigger: **identity** — people share it to signal their own ambitions.
- Distribution: mid-size account, no trending sound, normal timing.

**Counterfactual:** remove the sound → still works (no sound). Flatten the hook → dies. Strip the
identity/inspiration angle → just another vlog. **Drivers = the load-bearing hook + identity/
inspiration trigger.** Format and topic supporting; nothing incidental drove it.

**Replicability:** mid-size account, no one-time event, the mechanism recurs across many founder
videos → **transferable.**

**Principle → applied:** *a high-stakes, identity-driven hook + an inspiration trigger.* Your niche
version: "We deleted our most-requested feature to keep the product simple" — same mechanism (bold
stakes + an identity/values trigger for builders), your subject. → `tiktok-script` / `hook-writer`.

---

## Example 2 — Isolating the real driver (the "it was the sound" trap)

**Ask:** "It went viral because of the trending sound — let's just use the sound."
**Teardown:** the sound was on thousands of flopped videos too. Counterfactual: a flat video with
the *same* sound wouldn't have popped. The driver was a **relatable hook** ("POV: the feature
everyone begged for, that nobody used") + **recognition** trigger. The sound was incidental.
**Lesson:** copy the *hook + relatability mechanism*, not the sound. Copying the sound reproduces the
noise, not the success.

---

## Example 3 — Non-replicable (flag honestly)

**Ask:** "An 8M-follower creator's post blew up — what's the formula?"
**Response:** Most of this is **account size + reach they already had** — a 200-follower account doing
the identical post likely gets a fraction of it. There may be a small structural lesson (a clean
hook), but the "formula" here is largely the audience, plus possible timing luck. I won't hand you a
false principle. To find what's actually transferable, let's tear down several pieces that hit from
*small* accounts in your niche and look for the mechanism that recurs.

---

## Example 4 — Your own overperformer (small-sample caution)

**Ask:** "Our post got 5× our usual views — what do we repeat?"
**Teardown (from visible signals):** the standout was a contrarian hook + a practical-value
share-trigger (people sent it to colleagues). **But** one post is an anecdote — repeat the
*mechanism* (contrarian + useful) across a few more and see if it holds before declaring it your
formula. (No WoopSocial analytics — judged from native insights you pulled.)

---

## What these share

- The **counterfactual** isolates 1–2 real drivers from the noise.
- The **replicability check** catches account-size/luck/one-time confounds and small samples.
- The output is a **transferable mechanism applied to the user's niche** — never a copy.
- **Honest scope**: visible signals, no analytics claims, no virality guarantees.
