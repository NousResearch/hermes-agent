# Replicability & Application

A teardown is only useful if what you extract is **real and transferable.** This is the honesty layer
(don't learn the wrong lesson) and the action layer (turn the mechanism into your own content).

## The replicability check — is this even a lesson?

Before extracting a principle, screen the virality for things that aren't about the content at all:

- **Account-size confound** — a creator with millions of followers gets a running start no small
  account has. Their "formula" may just be *reach they already had*. Ask: would this have worked from
  a 200-follower account? If not, it's not a content lesson you can copy.
- **Luck / algorithmic variance** — some posts catch a wave for reasons no one can reproduce.
  Virality has genuine randomness; not every hit has a transferable cause.
- **One-time moments** — it rode a specific news event, a cultural moment, a first-mover novelty.
  That window is closed; the "principle" doesn't generalize.
- **Survivorship bias** — you only see the winners. A thousand creators did the same thing and
  flopped; the one that hit isn't proof the tactic works. Don't reverse-engineer only the survivors.
- **Sample size** — **one viral post is an anecdote, not a pattern.** A reliable mechanism shows up
  across *many* viral pieces in a niche. Treat a single-example takeaway as a hypothesis to test, not
  a law.

If a piece's success is mostly confound/luck, **say so plainly** and don't manufacture a false
principle. "This isn't replicable for you, and here's why" is a more valuable answer than a
confident-but-wrong formula.

## Find patterns across many examples

The trustworthy way to extract principles: tear down **10+ viral pieces in your niche**, then look
for the **mechanisms that recur.** A hook style, an emotional trigger, or a structure that shows up
across many winners (from different-sized accounts) is a real, transferable lever. A feature that
appears once is probably noise. Patterns beat anecdotes.

Keep a **swipe file / pattern library**: collect the examples, your teardowns, and the recurring
drivers. Over time this becomes *your* playbook of what spreads in *your* corner — far more reliable
than generic "viral tips."

## Extract the transferable principle → apply to your niche

Turn the mechanism into a usable instruction:

1. **State the mechanism** in one line ("relatable 'expectation vs reality' hook + recognition
   trigger drove the shares").
2. **Translate it to your world** — what's *your* niche's version of that hook/trigger? Not the same
   topic; the same *mechanism* on your subject.
3. **Hand it to the content skills** to execute (`hook-writer`, `tiktok-script`, `reels-script`,
   `caption-writer`, `carousel-writer`), in your `voice.md`.

The output is never "copy this." It's "the lever here is X; here's X applied to you."

## The ethics line — inspiration vs theft

Reverse-engineering a **mechanism** and being inspired is fair game and how all creators learn.
**Copying someone's specific content, script, or creative wholesale** is plagiarism and can infringe
IP — and it flops anyway because it's derivative. The rule: take the *principle*, add your *own*
substance and angle. If your output is recognizable as a particular creator's piece with the serial
numbers filed off, you've crossed the line — go back to mechanism + your own material.

## Measure honestly

Judging "what's viral/top" needs numbers. WoopSocial has **no analytics surface** — so work from
**visible/native signals** (public view/like/share/comment counts, the user's own platform insights,
or pasted data). Be clear about that scope, and apply small-sample caution to a single own-post
("overperformed" ≠ a proven repeatable win).
