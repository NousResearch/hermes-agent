# Why Things Spread

Virality is about **shares** — people sending content to other people — far more than passive views.
So the central question in any teardown is: *why did someone share this?* This is the psychology
behind the share-trigger layer, grounded in established research on why content spreads.

> These are durable principles from research on virality (e.g., Berger & Milkman's work on
> high-arousal emotion, and the social-transmission factors popularized as STEPPS). Paraphrased and
> applied here; verify nothing changes about human psychology quarterly (it won't).

## The share-triggers

People share for a handful of recurring reasons. Most viral content hits one or more **hard**:

- **Identity / self-expression** — "this is so me," or "this says what I believe." People share to
  signal who they are. The strongest trigger: content that lets the sharer express their identity or
  values *to their audience*.
- **High-arousal emotion** — emotions that activate the body drive sharing: **awe, anger, anxiety,
  excitement, amusement, inspiration.** Low-arousal emotions (mild contentment, sadness) spread
  less. The intensity matters more than positivity — anger spreads, mild "nice" doesn't.
- **Social currency** — sharing it makes the sharer look good: smart, funny, in-the-know, ahead of
  the curve. People pass on things that enhance how others see them.
- **Practical value** — genuinely useful enough that you send it to a specific person who needs it
  ("you have to see this"). Save-and-share utility.
- **Relatability** — "it me." Naming a shared, slightly-unspoken experience so precisely that people
  tag their friends ("this is literally you").
- **Triggers / topicality** — tied to something already on people's minds (a season, an event, a
  recurring moment) so it gets re-shared whenever that cue appears.
- **Story** — wrapped in a narrative people retell, carrying the message (or brand) along with it.

## Using the triggers in a teardown

When you reach the share-trigger layer, name the **specific** trigger(s) at work — don't stop at
"it's engaging." Examples:

- A founder's "I quit my job" video → **identity** (viewers project their own dream) + **inspiration**
  (high-arousal) + **story**.
- A "things clients say vs mean" post → **relatability** ("it me") + **social currency** (sharing it
  signals you're in the know).
- A sharp how-to → **practical value** (sent to a colleague) + maybe **social currency**.

The trigger is usually the real driver the counterfactual test surfaces. A piece with a competent
hook but **no share-trigger** gets views, not virality — that's why it plateaus.

## The honest caveat

Hitting a trigger **stacks the odds**; it doesn't guarantee a share-storm. Distribution, timing, and
luck still decide outcomes (see `replicability-and-application.md`). The triggers tell you *why*
something spread and *how* to give your content a real chance — not how to manufacture a guaranteed
hit.
