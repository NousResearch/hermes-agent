# Examples — voice extraction, end to end

Two contrasting walk-throughs showing the full path: sample → analysis → fingerprint →
generated post. They demonstrate the *level of specificity* to aim for and that the method
works across very different voices. Samples are illustrative.

---

## Example A — a blunt, dry indie founder

### Sample (real-ish input)
> "Spent 6 months building a feature nobody asked for. Classic. Talked to 4 customers last
> week — turns out they wanted the boring thing I kept skipping. So I shipped the boring thing
> in two days. It's already my most-used feature. Build less. Ask more. I'm slow to learn this."

### Analysis (selected observations, with evidence)
- **Lexicon:** plain, always contracts ("nobody," "it's," "I'm"). Concrete ("6 months," "4
  customers," "two days"). Bans hype entirely.
- **Syntax:** short declaratives, frequent fragments ("Classic." "Build less. Ask more.").
  One-word and two-word sentences for emphasis.
- **Rhetoric:** self-deprecating ("I'm slow to learn this"), builds by contrast (months of
  wrong work vs two days of right work), lands on a terse imperative.
- **Structure:** confession → turn → lesson. Opens mid-story, no preamble.
- **Stance:** peer, vulnerable, anti-overbuilding. Shares failure openly.
- **Negative space:** no exclamation marks, no emoji, no buzzwords, no "I'm excited to."

### Fingerprint
1. Opens mid-story with a blunt confession, no setup.
2. Short sentences and deliberate fragments for emphasis.
3. Concrete numbers in almost every post.
4. Self-deprecating; the joke is always on himself.
5. Lands on a 2–4 word imperative ("Build less. Ask more.").
- **Never:** exclamation marks, emoji, hype words, humble-brags.

### Generated post (new topic: hiring)
> "Hired for a résumé. Fired three months later. The résumé was great. The work wasn't.
> Started hiring for a paid trial project instead — watch them do the actual job for a week.
> Hit rate went way up. Stop reading résumés. Watch people work."

→ Hits every signature; reads plausibly like the source. ✅

## Example B — a warm, encouraging service brand (a bookkeeping studio)

### Sample (real-ish input)
> "Tax season can feel like a wall of dread — we get it. Here's the good news: you don't have
> to face it alone, and you definitely don't have to understand every form. That's literally
> our job. This week, just gather your receipts in one folder. That's it. One small step, and
> we'll handle the scary parts together."

### Analysis (selected observations, with evidence)
- **Lexicon:** warm, reassuring ("we get it," "the good news," "together"). Plain, contracts.
  "Scary parts" — names the emotion plainly.
- **Syntax:** medium, flowing sentences; gentle. Em-dashes for warmth ("dread — we get it").
- **Rhetoric:** direct address throughout ("you don't have to…"), reassurance pattern
  (acknowledge fear → relieve it), one tiny actionable step.
- **Structure:** empathy → reassurance → one small ask. Always ends with a doable next step.
- **Stance:** mentor/ally, warm, low-pressure, "we're in this with you."
- **Negative space:** no jargon, no pressure/urgency tactics, no fear-mongering, not cutesy.

### Fingerprint
1. Opens by naming the reader's feeling ("can feel like a wall of dread").
2. Pivots to reassurance with a "good news" / "you don't have to" move.
3. Direct address throughout — warm and personal.
4. Ends with exactly one small, doable step.
5. Em-dashes for a gentle, spoken rhythm.
- **Never:** jargon, urgency/pressure tactics, fear-mongering, cutesy tone.

### Generated post (new topic: quarterly estimated taxes)
> "Quarterly taxes sneaking up on you? Take a breath — this one's smaller than it looks. You
> don't need to calculate anything tonight. The only thing to do this week is forward us your
> Q2 income total. We'll do the math and tell you the exact number to pay. One email from you,
> and the rest is handled."

→ Hits every signature; warm, low-pressure, ends on one step. ✅

---

## The takeaway

Two opposite voices, same method: observe with evidence, distill a falsifiable fingerprint,
nail the negative space, then prove it by generating on a new topic. If your generated post on
a fresh topic reads like the source, the `voice.md` is doing its job. If it doesn't, the rules
aren't specific enough yet — go back to the layers.
