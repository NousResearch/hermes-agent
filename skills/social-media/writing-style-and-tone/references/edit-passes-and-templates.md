# Edit passes, the tone map & worked examples

## The four-pass edit (run in order on any draft)
1. SUBSTANCE: mark every sentence anyone could have written → replace or cut; insert one only-us specific.
2. WEIGHT: delete fluff sentences whole; cut throat-clearing; verbs over adjectives; front-load.
3. RHYTHM: vary sentence lengths (long-build → short landing); read aloud; fix every stumble.
4. TELLS: sweep the cluster (delve-class vocabulary, "It's not X it's Y", forced threes, rigid shape,
   misplaced social proof, relentless positivity) — with judgment, never bans.

## The tone map (one voice, rendered per moment)
| Moment | Register | The playful-brand example |
|---|---|---|
| Celebration | full personality | "We did the thing. The thing is DONE." |
| Education | warm, clear | "Here's the part everyone skips — don't." |
| Sales | confident, restrained | "It works. Here's the proof; you decide." |
| Complaint reply | human, accountable | "You're right, we got this wrong. Fixed by Friday." |
| Crisis / bad news | plain, direct | "We're reducing the team by 12 people. Here's why, and what they receive." |
Voice fingerprints persist; cleverness dials to zero as stakes rise.

## Before/after (the tells, fixed with substance)
BEFORE: "In today's fast-paced world, it's not about working harder — it's about working smarter. First,
prioritize. Second, delegate. Third, automate. This is a testament to the power of leverage."
AFTER: "I tracked my week once: 11 hours went to tasks a €40 tool now does. Working 'smarter' meant admitting
a machine files receipts better than I do. My Thursday afternoons came back."
(What changed: owned specifics replaced the parallel constructions, the forced three, and the hallmark words —
nothing was 'hidden'; something real was said.)

## The read-aloud protocol
Print or full-screen the draft → read aloud at speaking pace → mark every stumble, breath-gasp, and eye-skip →
each mark is an edit (shorten, split, or cut) → re-read. Two passes maximum; then ship or escalate to a human
editor.

## Worked example A — "Blunt indie founder" (the substance fix)
"a reader commented 'nice chatgpt post' on something i actually wrote myself. rude, but fair — i'd written it
like a press release. rewrote it with the actual number (we lost 3 clients in march), the actual mistake
(mine), and one opinion i'd defend at dinner. same topic. nobody asks who wrote my posts anymore, because
they sound like a person who was there." — the tells were symptoms; specificity was the cure.

## Worked example B — "Warm bookkeeping studio" (the tone map in a hard week)
"Our voice is gentle and a little playful — but the week we wrote about a client's bankruptcy, playful would
have been grotesque. We kept our words simple and our sentences short, and readers said it was the most
'us' thing we'd published. One small step: write down how your brand sounds on its best day, then decide —
before you need it — how it sounds on its hardest one." — voice constant, tone chosen deliberately.

## Never
Invent personal anecdotes, failures, or credentials · stage typos or fake imperfection as 'humanity' · chase
detector scores · ban punctuation as policy · ship a fluff sentence · publish without the read-aloud pass ·
let platform-native voices erase the brand's · use cleverness in a crisis · fabricate the human's experiences
or feelings · skip AI-disclosure where required.
