# The reality of writing style in 2026 (verify-quarterly)

The tell lists shift as models change — attribute, **verify-quarterly**. The craft (rhythm, specificity,
tone control) is stable; the perception layer is what 2026 changed.

## The stakes, quantified (attribute)
- **52% of consumers report they become less engaged the moment they suspect copy is AI-generated** (Bynder's
  2,000-person UK/US study — which also found 56% *preferred* the AI article when it was unlabeled; the
  penalty is for suspected AI, not AI per se); **only 13% of consumers say they completely trust AI**
  (Klaviyo's ~8,000-consumer AI persona research).
- **The audience can tell:** research finds annotators who frequently use ChatGPT for writing achieve
  near-perfect detection of AI text, outperforming automatic detectors even against paraphrasing (Russell et
  al., via the multilingual-detection literature). Assuming readers can't spot it is a losing bet.
- **Machines penalize sameness too:** search systems' information-gain direction rewards unique perspectives;
  predictable-pattern content is treated as noise, and same-prompt content across competitors erases the
  advantage (attribute).

## The tell taxonomy (converging across 2026 sources — apply as a cluster, never one marker)
**Vocabulary:** delve, tapestry, leverage, testament, underscore, "in today's fast-paced world" — the "delve"
surge is measured: **44% of all PubMed appearances of "delve" since 1849 occurred in 2023–24 alone**
(medRxiv), with delve+underscore co-occurring in 98.8% of their joint 2023–24 articles. **Structure:**
repetitive sentence shapes; overused connectors (furthermore/moreover); rigid intro–point–point–conclusion;
forced rule-of-three ("First… Second… Third…"); "It's not X, it's Y" negative parallels; lists imposed on
ideas that don't need them; misplaced social proof in personal posts. **Substance:** fluff sentences that
sound professional but add no information. **Texture:** relentlessly polite neutral tone; positivity bias;
strictly on-topic organization where humans digress; typographic perfection (proper em dashes, curly quotes,
true ellipses) where fast human typing produces --, straight quotes, and ... (Paralect; PR Daily; oliviacal;
the arXiv slop-measurement and human-vs-AI literature).

## The counter-nuance the top 1% holds (attribute)
**No single marker proves AI — and tell-hunting overcorrects.** The em-dash panic ("the ChatGPT hyphen")
condemns punctuation great writers have always used (Rolling Stone documents the discourse); detectors are
unreliable arbiters — the Stanford study (Liang et al., *Patterns* 2023) found seven GPT detectors **misflagged
non-native-English TOEFL essays as AI at a 61.3% average false-positive rate**, with all seven unanimously
flagging 19.8% of them — while classifying US-student essays near-perfectly. The winning position: *"What matters isn't whether your content contains em dashes —
it's whether your content is so obviously written by a human that the question doesn't come up"* — via deep
thinking, personal anecdotes, fresh perspectives, original research (SALT); *"say something only you could
say"* (Paralect). Write for readers, never against detectors. One tactical footnote: if a specific audience
currently reads a marker as AI, using it sparingly is a perception choice — not a truth about writing.

## What human writing actually looks like (the research profile)
Versus AI's analytical, formal, organized, relentlessly on-topic, positivity-biased profile, human text is
**more subjective, colloquial, emotion-rich, and divergent** — it wanders, takes positions, varies rhythm
("burstiness"), and carries specific references and small imperfections (the arXiv human-vs-AI literature).
That profile is a craft target, honestly reached: real opinions, real specifics, varied sentence lengths —
never staged typos or invented anecdotes.

## House metrics
Read-through/dwell signals, saves/shares, and reply quality on published posts (native, via
analytics-and-reporting) — plus the absence of "did AI write this?" comments. Never fabricated.
