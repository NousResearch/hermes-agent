# YouTube long-form algorithm 2026 — the verified signal stack

*Volatile facts. Re-verify quarterly against YouTube Creator guidance and Studio analytics.*

## The headline: the opposite engine from Shorts
Long-form runs on **packaging + retention + session**, distributed through **Browse, Suggested,
Search, and external** — not a swipe feed. It's **decoupled from Shorts** (separate systems);
Shorts-gained subscribers rarely watch long content. *But* the **Short→long-form click-through is
now tracked and attributed**, so a deliberate bridge helps. (Sibling: **youtube-shorts**.)

## The two-metric gate: CTR then AVD
YouTube weights **click-through rate (CTR)** and **average view duration (AVD)** above all.
*CTR gets you in the door; AVD decides whether you stay in the building.* The quadrants:
- **High CTR + low AVD** → reads as misleading packaging → **demoted** (aggressively post-2024;
  repeat offenders get **channel-level** penalties).
- **Low CTR + high AVD** → good content, weak packaging → **fix packaging first**.
- **CTR benchmarks:** 4–10% healthy, ~6% average; <3% for a week = a packaging problem. AVD ~50%+
  is a strong signal. (A/B testing raised the baseline — 6% in 2024 ≈ 5% now.)

## Session contribution is the dominant 2026 signal
Session-time weighting **replaced absolute watch time** as the primary signal. Videos that lead
viewers to **watch more afterward** get far more Suggested placements; videos that **end sessions**
(viewer closes YouTube) get fewer impressions. Build for the **chain**, not the isolated upload —
end screens, playlists, series, "watch next."

## The impression test + the first 30 seconds
Every upload is shown to a small test audience (~1–5% of subs + a sample of new viewers). Beat the
niche threshold on **CTR + early retention** → distribution expands; miss → it stops (not a
shadowban — you "didn't pass the test"). **The first 30 seconds are everything:** a steep early
drop = "clickbait that didn't deliver," a worse signal than not getting the click. Open with the
payoff/stakes/question — no 45-second logo, no "hey guys welcome back."

## The three surfaces (different logic each)
- **Browse (Home/subscriptions)** — most viral growth; wins on CTR + first-30s retention.
- **Suggested (sidebar/autoplay)** — often the largest source; topical relevance + watch history +
  **session contribution** (the big 2026 weighting).
- **Search** — intent satisfaction + semantic NLP; **description/chapter alignment** matters more
  in 2026; pogo-sticking back to results demotes you. (80%+ Search traffic = plateau risk; healthy
  channels see Home + Suggested climbing.)

## Length, cadence, and what's penalized
- **Length:** 8–15 min optimal for most niches **when retention holds.** Total watch time can favor
  longer (20 min @ 40% AVD > 5 min @ 80% in Suggested) — but consistent drop-off kills it. **Stop
  padding;** cutting length 30–40% is often the highest-leverage fix.
- **Cadence:** ~1–2 long-form/week; consistency; upload gaps cause deprioritization + a ramp-up.
- **Penalized:** watch-time padding, clickbait/packaging mismatch, **mid-video "smash subscribe"**
  (session-killer drop-off), tag stuffing (tags largely ignored — 3–5 max).
- **Diagnostics that matter:** CTR (packaging), 30-sec retention (hook), AVD% (structure).
